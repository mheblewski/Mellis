# Open-Source Automated Insulin Delivery in Type 1 Diabetes (CREATE trial)

- **Authors:** Burnside MJ, Lewis DM, Crocket HR, Meier RA, Williman JA, Sanders OJ, Jefferies CA, Faherty AM, Paul RG, Lever CS, Price SKJ, Frewen CM, Jones SD, Gunn TC, Lampey C, Wheeler BJ, de Bock MI
- **Journal/year:** New England Journal of Medicine 2022;387(10):869-881
- **DOI:** 10.1056/NEJMoa2203913
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36069869/
- **Licence:** paraphrased (abstract only; NEJM, not open licence)
- **Saved:** summary from the PubMed/Europe PMC abstract on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus.

## What it is

The main randomised controlled trial of an oref-based open-source AID (AndroidAPS 2.8 with the OpenAPS 0.7.0 algorithm, DANA-i pump, Dexcom G6) vs sensor-augmented pump, in children and adults (New Zealand).

## Methods

- 97 participants: 48 children 7-15 y and 49 adults 16-70 y; 1:1 randomisation; 24 weeks; primary outcome TIR 70-180 mg/dL in the final 2 weeks.

## Results relevant to correction boluses

- TIR 61.2 % -> 71.2 % with open-source AID vs 57.7 % -> 54.5 % with control; adjusted difference +14 points (95 % CI 9.2-18.8), **no difference in effect by age**; +3 h 21 min/day in range.
- No severe hypoglycaemia or DKA in either arm; two withdrawals for connectivity.
- Establishes that oref is safe and effective in children in an RCT; the abstract does not report how manual corrections were handled or whether SMB was enabled, so it gives no evidence for or against adding a manual correction to a meal bolus.

## Limitations

- Abstract-level; open-label; one algorithm version; 24-week extension (Burnside 2023, DTT) not reviewed here.
