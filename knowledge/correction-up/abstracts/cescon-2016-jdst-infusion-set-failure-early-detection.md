# Early Detection of Infusion Set Failure During Insulin Pump Therapy in Type 1 Diabetes (the evidence the ATTD AID consensus cites for its set-failure rule)

- **Authors:** Cescon M, DeSalvo DJ, Ly TT, Maahs DM, Messer LH, Buckingham BA, Doyle FJ, Dassau E
- **Journal/year:** Journal of Diabetes Science and Technology 2016;10(6):1268-1276
- **DOI:** 10.1177/1932296816663962
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5094340/
- **Licence:** paraphrased from the abstract (© Diabetes Technology Society; not openly licensed)
- **Saved:** summary from the Europe PMC abstract on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus.

## What it is

An algorithm study that combined CGM glucose and pump insulin-delivery data to warn of infusion set failure, which the authors describe as a variable, unpredictable pattern of rising glucose despite increased insulin infusion. It was calibrated on 12 weeks of set wear without failures (4 patients) and validated prospectively on 18 weeks of set wear with and without failures (9 other patients) in ambulatory conditions.

## Key findings

- The algorithm flagged a failure on average 2.52 ± 1.91 days before the event recorded by the clinical team, with 50 % sensitivity, 66 % specificity and 55 % accuracy.
- Had the alarms run in real time, time above 180 mg/dL would have fallen from 82.7 ± 40.9 to 58.8 ± 31.1 h/week per subject (about 29 % less).
- Set failure can cause prolonged hyperglycaemia or DKA and matters especially for automated insulin delivery.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance (background):** this is reference 105 of the ATTD AID consensus (Phillip 2023), which teaches users to suspect set failure when glucose stays above 250 mg/dL and/or does not fall by at least 50 mg/dL within 1 hour of a correction bolus. The abstract itself does not state that 50 mg/dL / 1 h criterion; it supports the broader idea that "rising glucose despite more insulin" is the signature of a failing set, which is also how an AID loop behaves when it keeps raising delivery without effect.
- **Population:** small ambulatory cohort (adults and youth; exact ages not in the abstract).
- **Evidence level:** [M] algorithm development and validation study (small n).
