*Our summary (Mellis knowledge base), written in our own words. It is not the original text and not medical advice. Original: de Bock M, Agwu JC, Deabreu M, et al. International Society for Pediatric and Adolescent Diabetes Clinical Practice Consensus Guidelines 2024: Glycemic Targets. Horm Res Paediatr. 2024;97(6):546-554, https://doi.org/10.1159/000543266.*

## Key points

- **HbA1c ≤6.5% (48 mmol/mol)** is the target for children and adolescents who can reach it safely with advanced technology (CGM and automated insulin delivery, AID) and/or without a burden that harms quality of life [grade C]. It should be encouraged for everyone when safely achievable [HbA1c; What is new].
- **HbA1c ≤7.0% (53 mmol/mol)** in all other situations [grade A].
- CGM time targets [grade B]: **TIR >70%** at 3.9–10 mmol/L (70–180 mg/dL); **<4%** below 3.9 mmol/L (70 mg/dL); **<1%** below 3.0 mmol/L (54 mg/dL); **<25%** above 10 mmol/L (180 mg/dL); **<5%** above 13.9 mmol/L (250 mg/dL) [Continuous Glucose Monitoring].
- TIR may need to be **>80%** to reach HbA1c ≤6.5% [grade E].
- **Time in tight range (TITR)** 3.9–8 mmol/L (70–144 mg/dL): an emerging metric, target generally **>50%**; >55% may be needed for HbA1c ≤6.5% [grade C].
- The CGM-derived glucose management indicator (GMI) can be used on its own to judge glycaemic outcomes [grade C].
- Without CGM: fingerstick targets **4.0–8.0 mmol/L (70–144 mg/dL) before meals** and **4.0–10 mmol/L (70–180 mg/dL) after meals** [grade B], with at least 6 checks a day on insulin [grade B] [Self-Monitored Blood Glucose].
- Targets may be individualised after assessing the challenges for the person and carers [What is new].

## What changed from 2022

In 2022 ISPAD's HbA1c target was 7.0% (53 mmol/mol), with lower targets where feasible (e.g. with AID). The 2024 chapter deliberately sets 6.5% (48 mmol/mol) when technology and team support allow, arguing that lower HbA1c reduces complications and can be reached with modern tools without more hypoglycaemia or burden. Sweden and NICE already use 6.5%; the ADA calls <6.5% reasonable if safe and <7.0% appropriate. The chapter also discusses body weight and is shorter than earlier versions [Purpose of setting glycemic targets; What is new].

## HbA1c targets and the evidence behind them

- The link between glycaemia and complications is continuous and non-linear (landmark trials), which supports ≤7% as grade A; evidence for ≤6.5% is still emerging (grade C). A single number is used because it is easier to communicate and to benchmark [Glycated Hemoglobin].
- Measure HbA1c about every 3 months (it reflects roughly 90 days of glucose). Clear, consistent goals help avoid therapeutic inertia.
- **Retinopathy:** risk also depends on age at onset and duration; the lowest retinopathy rates at transfer to adult care were seen with HbA1c ≤6.5% [Diabetic Retinopathy].
- **Kidney disease:** lower HbA1c protects, but Swedish data showed no clear extra benefit for albuminuria at the 6.5% threshold; evidence below 7% is limited [Diabetic Kidney Disease].
- **Neuropathy:** HbA1c >7% raised risk in adolescents with >5 years of T1D; 6.5% was not studied [Diabetic Neuropathy].
- **Cognition:** better glycaemia is linked to better memory, learning and attention, but without CGM and insulin-suspending technology, a 6.5% target may carry unacceptable severe hypoglycaemia risk, so it should be set individually in that setting [Cognition].
- **Burden:** technology has barriers (workload, discomfort, alarms, glitches, visibility, stigma) but consistently improves quality of life and glycaemia; psychosocial needs must be addressed [Burden].
- **Body weight:** BMI-SDS is rising in registries; AID improves glycaemia without changing BMI; dietetic education matters so that better glucose is not offset by weight gain [Body Mass Index].

## CGM targets: time in range, time below range, time above range

| Metric | Range | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Time in range (TIR) | 3.9–10 mmol/L (70–180 mg/dL) | >70% (>80% may be needed for HbA1c ≤6.5%) |
| Time below range, level 1 | <3.9 mmol/L (<70 mg/dL) | <4% |
| Time below range, level 2 | <3.0 mmol/L (<54 mg/dL) | <1% |
| Time above range | >10 mmol/L (>180 mg/dL) | <25% |
| Time above range, high | >13.9 mmol/L (>250 mg/dL) | <5% |
| Time in tight range (TITR) | 3.9–8 mmol/L (70–144 mg/dL) | >50% (>55% may be needed for HbA1c ≤6.5%) |

[Continuous Glucose Monitoring; Fig. 1]

- These ranges match earlier international consensus recommendations.
- Mean sensor glucose correlates strongly with HbA1c and microvascular risk, and glucose variability helps predict hypoglycaemia; both are in standard AGP reports.
- CGM targets can replace or add to HbA1c targets; GMI alone is reasonable where CGM is easier to get than HbA1c (e.g. telehealth).
- TITR was already endorsed by ISPAD for preschool children, especially in the remission ("honeymoon") phase; it may be more sensitive than TIR at lower average glucose and reflects variability better. More evidence in youth is awaited.

## Fingerstick (SMBG) targets

- Before meals and fasting: 4.0–8.0 mmol/L (70–144 mg/dL). After meals: 4.0–10 mmol/L (70–180 mg/dL). The lower limit 3.9 mmol/L is rounded to 4.0 for teaching; some centres use 3.9.
- Bedtime: 4.0–8 mmol/L is appropriate, but carers may prefer values higher in the 4.0–10 mmol/L range after an earlier low, after exercise, or with hypoglycaemia unawareness [Self-Monitored Blood Glucose].
- These targets are meant to match HbA1c ≤7% and TIR >70%.

## Limitations of each measure

- **HbA1c:** wide spread of mean glucose for the same HbA1c; differences between ethnic groups; unreliable in anaemia, haemoglobinopathies and liver disease.
- **CGM:** can be disturbed by some medicines and is occasionally inaccurate, though overall accuracy is high.
- **SMBG:** six or more checks a day is a known barrier [Practical Considerations and Limitations].

## Limits of this summary

- Population: children and adolescents with diabetes; adult targets are not covered.
- The 6.5% HbA1c target is grade C and depends on access to CGM/AID; the TIR >80% statement is grade E; TITR is still being validated. Note that the ISPAD 2024 glucose monitoring chapter gives tight range as 70–140 mg/dL (3.9–7.8 mmol/L), while this chapter uses 70–144 mg/dL (3.9–8 mmol/L).
- Specific groups (preschool children, limited-resource settings, exercise) are covered in other ISPAD chapters.
- Targets should be set individually with the care team; read the original for clinical decisions.
