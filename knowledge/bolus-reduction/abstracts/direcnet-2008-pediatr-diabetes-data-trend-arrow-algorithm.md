# Use of the DirecNet Applied Treatment Algorithm (DATA) for Diabetes Management with a Real-Time Continuous Glucose Monitor (the FreeStyle Navigator)

- **Authors:** Diabetes Research in Children Network (DirecNet) Study Group; writing committee Buckingham B, Xing D, Weinzimer S, Fiallo-Scharer R, Kollman C, Mauras N, Tsalikian E, Tamborlane W, Wysocki T, Ruedy K, Beck R
- **Journal/year:** Pediatric Diabetes 2008;9(2):142–147 (epub 2008 Jan 24)
- **DOI:** 10.1111/j.1399-5448.2007.00301.x
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2390770/
- **Licence:** paraphrased (NIH author manuscript in PMC; publisher copyright)
- **Saved:** summary from the PMC author manuscript (including its appendix of patient instructions) on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus.

## Design

- 30 children and adolescents (mean age 11.2 ± 4.1 y, range 4–17; mean HbA1c 7.1% at baseline) on insulin pumps, using the FreeStyle Navigator real-time CGM for 13 weeks at five US centres. Families were taught the DATA rules; a satisfaction questionnaire was completed at 3, 7 and 13 weeks.
- This is the original source of the "±10% / ±20%" trend-arrow rule that later papers attribute to DirecNet/JDRF. It is sometimes cited as a 2005 Diabetes Technology & Therapeutics paper by Buckingham et al.; the only publication with this title found in PubMed/PMC is this 2008 Pediatric Diabetes article. According to Klonoff/Kerr 2017 and Parise 2023, the JDRF CGM study group used the same ±10/20% rule (not verified from the JDRF protocol itself).

## The pre-meal rule (appendix, paraphrased)

- **Glucose ≤70 mg/dL before a meal:** start eating but do **not** bolus yet. Bolus once glucose is above 80 mg/dL, and then cover all the carbohydrate in the meal (not a reduced dose).
- **Glucose >70 mg/dL:** calculate the usual dose for the meal's carbohydrate plus any correction for high glucose, then adjust by the Navigator arrow:

| Navigator arrow | Meaning | Change to the calculated meal dose |
|---|---|---|
| ↑ (90° up) | rising >2 mg/dL/min | +20% |
| ↗ (45° up) | rising >1 mg/dL/min | +10% |
| → | no significant change | none |
| ↘ (45° down) | falling >1 mg/dL/min | −10% |
| ↓ (90° down) | falling >2 mg/dL/min | −20% |

(The percentages were blanks that the clinician could set; 10% and 20% were the defaults shown.)

- Low alarm (set at 60–70 mg/dL): about 15 g carbohydrate. Predicted-low alarm: about 10 g carbohydrate. Confirm alarms with a meter in the first 2 weeks.
- High alarm after a meal bolus: wait at least 2 hours before a correction (insulin still active); correction target 80–120 mg/dL by day, 80–150 at night. Smart pumps used 3–5 h insulin action for insulin-on-board.
- Age-specific pre-meal targets: <7 y 90–180, 7–12 y 80–150, >12 y 70–130 mg/dL; bedtime 110–200 / 100–180 / 90–150.

## Results

- At 3 weeks, 82% of children and 96% of parents agreed the guidelines gave clear dosing directions (59% and 73% at 13 weeks). Use of the rules for pre-meal bolus adjustment "most of the time or always" fell from 68% to 41% of children between weeks 3 and 13 (p = 0.03), while more families adjusted their own ratios and basal rates.
- In the parent pilot study, HbA1c fell from 7.1% to 6.8% at 13 weeks with no severe hypoglycaemia. There was no control group, so the rule's own effect is unknown.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance to reducing a meal bolus when glucose is falling / a low is predicted:** central and paediatric. The instructions say to calculate the carbohydrate dose plus any correction and then change the "meal dose" by the percentage; Aleppo 2017 and Ziegler 2019 read this as a percentage of the total calculated dose (meal + correction). A falling arrow therefore trims 10–20% of the bolus and never zeroes it by itself. Below 70 mg/dL the meal bolus is postponed (not reduced) until glucose is above 80 mg/dL, then given in full. Later authors note the percentage approach scales with meal size, so it can under-correct for large meals and over-correct for small ones (Aleppo 2017).
- **Time horizon:** not stated beyond the arrow rates (>1 and >2 mg/dL/min).
- **AID:** none (2006–2007 pump therapy without automation).
- **Population:** children and adolescents 4–17 years, all on pumps.
- **Evidence level:** uncontrolled feasibility/satisfaction study of an expert-designed algorithm; later tested against the Ziegler scorecard in adults (Parise 2023).
