# The additional dose of insulin for high-protein mixed meal provides better glycemic control in children with type 1 diabetes on insulin pumps: randomized cross-over study

- **Authors:** Piechowiak K, Dżygało K, Szypowska A
- **Journal/year:** Pediatric Diabetes 2017;18(8):861-868
- **DOI:** 10.1111/pedi.12500
- **PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28117542/
- **Licence:** not open access (or open-access status not confirmed). This file contains a paraphrased summary of the abstract and key numbers written for the Mellis corpus, not the publisher's text.

## Summary of the abstract (paraphrased)

- **Population:** 58 children with T1D on pumps, age 14.7 ± 2.2 years (Warsaw).
- **Design:** randomised, double-blind, two-way crossover; standardised high-protein, low-fat meal with carbohydrate (Furthner 2021 table: 30 g carbohydrate / 5 g fat / 36 g protein). NORMAL bolus (carbohydrate only) vs DUAL-wave bolus with an additional dose for protein (fat-protein exchange: 100 kcal of fat/protein treated like 40 kcal of carbohydrate, i.e. like 10 g carbohydrate).
- **Glucose at 180 min:** NORMAL **162 mg/dL (9 mmol/L)** vs DUAL **130 mg/dL (7.22 mmol/L)**, P = 0.004. No differences at 60 and 120 min.
- **MAGE at 120 min:** 82.86 vs 54.76 mg/dL (P = 0.0008).
- **Hypoglycaemia:** no difference in the number of episodes.

## Notes for Mellis

- Polish paediatric RCT supporting extra insulin (dual-wave) for high-protein meals, with no hypoglycaemia increase over a 3 h observation window (short window; later lows may be missed, as Lopez 2018 argued for the original FPU studies).
