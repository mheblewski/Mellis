## Avoiding DKA on hybrid closed loop

- DKA on HCL systems has occurred through cannula failures, when glucose stayed high for a while and the user neither checked ketones nor changed the set, "trusting that the system would sort it out".
- It is "very unusual" for someone on HCL to have glucose above 15.0 mmol/L (270 mg/dL) for more than 2 hours.
- Repeated corrections through the pump will only give small doses, because the system has already been delivering extra insulin and believes there is a lot of insulin on board, although the cannula or set is not working.
- For unexplained highs, "only give the pump 1 chance to get it right"; if levels are not coming down after 2 hours, follow the flowchart.
- Change the full set every 2-3 days; poor sites or overused cannulas reduce absorption and also distort the system's learning of total daily dose.

## Unexplained hyperglycaemia and sick day rules (page 3 flowchart)

- **Trigger:** blood glucose > 15 mmol/L and not responding to bolus corrections, or feeling unwell: check blood ketones.
- **Ketones < 1.5 mmol/L (no or trace; "minor illness"): stay in closed loop.** Change cannula, infusion set and reservoir; the closed loop will raise basal and give corrections itself; check that sensor glucose matches a fingerstick; use the bolus calculator for additional corrective boluses if glucose is raised, even when not eating; usual boluses for carbohydrate; check glucose and ketones every 2-4 h; sip sugar-free fluids (at least 100 ml/h).
- **Ketones ≥ 1.5 mmol/L ("severe illness"): exit closed loop mode.** Calculate the average total daily dose (TDD). Ketones 1.5-3 mmol/L: give 10 % of TDD as quick-acting insulin by pen or syringe; ketones > 3 mmol/L: give 20 % of TDD by pen or syringe. Then change cannula, set and reservoir; do NOT restart closed loop; recheck glucose and ketones in 2 h.
- **If ketones persist (pump now on a new set, open loop):** 1.5-3 mmol/L: 10 % of TDD as a pump bolus every 2 h and basal increased by 30 %; > 3 mmol/L: 20 % of TDD every 2 h and basal increased by 50 % or more; add the usual meal bolus when eating (overriding the calculator); fluids at least 100 ml/h; recheck every 2 h.
- **Returning to closed loop:** once blood ketones are < 1.5 mmol/L, wait 4 hours after the last pen/syringe injection of quick-acting insulin before restarting closed loop.
- **Seek help** if hyperglycaemia has not improved after more than 4 hours of trying; go to hospital as an emergency if vomiting and unable to keep fluids down, or unable to control glucose or ketones.

## Other practical points

- When glucose rises during illness: use Boost (CamAPS FX), strengthen basal rates by 20 % (Control-IQ), or lower active insulin time / target (MiniMed 780G).
- Hypoglycaemia on HCL usually follows a period with no insulin delivery, so smaller treatments suffice (e.g. 4-5 g if glucose 4.0-6.0 mmol/L with a falling arrow; 8-10 g if < 3.9 mmol/L, when little or no active insulin is on board).
- DKA toolkit: ketone meter and in-date strips, pump sick day rules, rapid- and long-acting insulin pens or syringes, conversion doses for going back to injections, reviewed manual basal rates.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance (high):** this is the UK professional network's standard HCL rule set, and the most explicit statement that AID users should not keep correcting a high through the pump: one pump correction, then if no fall within 2 h (or > 15 mmol/L for > 2 h) think cannula, check ketones, change the set, and with ketones ≥ 1.5 mmol/L give 10-20 % of TDD by pen, leave closed loop and wait 4 h after the last injection before re-entering it. With ketones < 1.5 mmol/L, calculator corrections within closed loop are allowed (after a set change).
- **Thresholds differ between UK documents:** 15 mmol/L here vs 14 mmol/L in the NHS Scotland version of the same flowchart; ketone action threshold 1.5 mmol/L (ISPAD uses 0.6 mmol/L to stop closed loop in children).
- **Population:** all ages using NHS HCL systems (not child-specific; doses are % of TDD).
- **AID systems:** CamAPS FX, Control-IQ, MiniMed 780G (and other HCL).
- **Evidence level:** [Ed] national professional-network patient education leaflet.
