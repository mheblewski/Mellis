# In children and young people with type 1 diabetes using pump therapy, an additional 40% of the insulin dose for a high-fat, high-protein breakfast improves postprandial glycaemic excursions: a cross-over trial

- **Authors:** Smith TA, Smart CE, Fuery MEJ, Howley PP, Knight BA, Harris M, King BR
- **Journal/year:** Diabetic Medicine 2021;38(7):e14511
- **DOI:** 10.1111/dme.14511
- **PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33405297/
- **Licence:** not open access (or open-access status not confirmed). This file contains a paraphrased summary of the abstract and key numbers written for the Mellis corpus, not the publisher's text.

## Summary of the abstract (paraphrased)

- **Population:** 27 participants aged 10-23 years on pumps; HbA1c ≤ 64 mmol/mol (≤ 8.0%).
- **Meal:** high-fat, high-protein breakfast: **30 g carbohydrate, 40 g fat, 50 g protein**.
- **Doses tested (randomised order):** 100% (control), 120%, 140% and 160% of the ICR dose, always as a **combination bolus: 60% given 15 min before the meal and 40% over 3 h**.
- **6-h glucose AUC (mean, 95% CI):** 100% ICR 1249 mmol/L·min [1042, 1457]; 140% ICR 822 [605, 1039]; 160% ICR 567 [350, 784] (p ≤ 0.001 vs 100%).
- **Peak excursion:** 100% 6.0 mmol/L [5.0, 6.9]; 140% 4.0 [3.0, 4.9]; 160% 2.7 [1.7, 3.6] (p < 0.001).
- **Hypoglycaemia rates:** 100% 7.7%; 120% 7.7%; 140% 12%; 160% 19% (differences not significant, p ≥ 0.139).
- Mean glucose fell step-wise with dose from 1 to 6 h (p = 0.008).
- **Conclusion:** **140% of the ICR dose** improved glucose without a statistically significant increase in hypoglycaemia; recommended as a practical method.

## Notes for Mellis

- This is the main paediatric pump trial behind "+40% for a high-fat, high-protein meal". Note the delivery: 60% upfront, 40% extended over 3 h. Hypoglycaemia rose numerically (7.7% → 12% → 19%) as the dose increased, so the extra insulin is not free of risk.
- Open-loop pump, not AID.
