# A Simplified Approach Using Rate of Change Arrows to Adjust Insulin With Real-Time Continuous Glucose Monitoring

- **Authors:** Klonoff DC, Kerr D
- **Journal/year:** Journal of Diabetes Science and Technology 2017;11(6):1063–1069 (editorial; epub 2017 Sep 8)
- **DOI:** 10.1177/1932296817723260
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5951054/
- **Licence:** paraphrased (© 2017 Diabetes Technology Society; free to read in PMC, no open licence stated)
- **Saved:** summary from the PMC full text on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus. Numbers are taken from Tables 2–6; prose is paraphrased.

## Background in the paper

- Manufacturers define arrows differently (over 15 or 20 min, or unstated); Tables 1a–1d list Libre, Navigator II, Dexcom G4/G5, several Medtronic systems and Eversense.
- Dosing on an arrow implicitly assumes the current trend persists for roughly 30–60 minutes; predictions further than about 1 hour are unreliable.
- JDRF and DirecNet first suggested ±10–20% dose changes. A survey of 300 CGM users (Pettus/Edelman) found people consider that too timid (see table below). Most respondents also shifted bolus timing by the arrow (58% of T1D; 61% MDI, 58% CSII; 67% of T2D) and delayed meals after the bolus when glucose was rising.

## Other mealtime advice summarised by the authors

- Pettus/Edelman, falling ≥2 mg/dL/min at a meal: eat, wait until glucose stabilises, and give insulin only if glucose starts to rise or levels off above target. Rising ≥2 mg/dL/min: inject, then wait to eat until the arrow bends ("waiting for the bend").
- Scheiner, falling glucose: raise the carbohydrate eaten rather than (or as well as) cutting insulin: about +25% carbohydrate for a fall of 1–3 mg/dL/min and +50–100% for >3 mg/dL/min. Scheiner also preferred fixed-unit adjustments to percentages unless sensitivity is unusual.

## The Klonoff/Kerr proposal

- Take the midpoint of each arrow's range (1.5, 2.5 and ≥3 mg/dL/min) and assume it lasts **45 minutes** (midpoint of the 30–60 min window). Expected change: 67, 112 (113 in Table 6) and ≥135 mg/dL.
- Adjust the bolus by a fixed **1, 1.5 or 2 units** (added for rising, subtracted for falling). No percentage, no recalculation.
- Safety check: these units fully offset the expected change only if CF is about 67, 75 and ≤67 mg/dL per U. Using the 1500 and 1960 rules, that corresponds to a total daily dose of at least **20–29 U/day**. People who are more insulin sensitive, **including children** or adults on <29 U/day, should **not** use this formula, because the fixed decrement or increment could be too large.
- Population mean/mode CF for pump users cited as 40 mg/dL per U (Walsh), so the formula is "relatively safe" for most adults.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance to reducing a meal bolus when glucose is falling / a low is predicted:** moderate. Gives the simplest rule (−1 / −1.5 / −2 U by fall rate, applied to the whole bolus) and the explicit 45-minute horizon (rate × 45 min). It explicitly excludes children and adults with low insulin needs (<20–29 U/day), which matters for paediatric use. Also documents Scheiner's "eat more carbs when falling" alternative (+25% or +50–100% carbohydrate).
- **AID:** mentions closed-loop systems only as a future way to remove calculation burden; no AID guidance.
- **Population:** adults (T1D and T2D); MDI and pump.
- **Evidence level:** editorial/expert proposal with arithmetic safety reasoning; no clinical testing.
