# A comparison of postprandial and preprandial administration of insulin aspart in children and adolescents with type 1 diabetes

- **Authors:** Danne T, Aman J, Schober E, Deiss D, Jacobsen JL, Friberg HH, Jensen LH; ANA 1200 Study Group
- **Journal/year:** Diabetes Care 2003;26(8):2359
- **DOI:** 10.2337/diacare.26.8.2359
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12882862/
- **Licence:** paraphrased
- **Saved:** summary from the PubMed/Europe PMC abstract and the Mozzillo 2022 systematic review table on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus.

## Design

- Multicentre (Germany, Austria, Sweden) open-label randomized crossover, two 6-week periods.
- 42 children 6–12 y and 34 adolescents 13–17 y on basal-bolus injections (MDI) with insulin aspart at least 3×/day.
- Pre-prandial = immediately before the meal; post-prandial = immediately after the meal, at most 30 min after the meal started.

## Results

- HbA1c: pre 7.9 → 8.0%; post 8.0 → 8.3% (p = 0.14, not significant). Fructosamine similar.
- Only significant glucose difference: 2 h after breakfast, about 2.1 mmol/L lower with pre-meal dosing (p = 0.016).
- Hypoglycaemia (< 3.9 mmol/L): relative risk pre vs post 1.1 (95% CI 0.91–1.35), not different.
- Treatment satisfaction equally high for children, adolescents and parents.

## Authors' conclusion (paraphrased)

Pre-meal aspart is generally preferable, but post-meal aspart is a safe and effective alternative in children and adolescents.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance:** Supports giving the meal bolus after eating (within 30 min of meal start) when a pre-meal bolus is unsafe, e.g. glucose low or falling at mealtime or uncertain intake — at the cost of a higher post-breakfast peak, with no extra hypoglycaemia in this trial.
- **Population:** children/adolescents on MDI (NPH/lente/ultralente or long-acting basal), pre-CGM, pre-AID.
- **Evidence level:** randomized crossover trial (n = 76), 6-week periods.
