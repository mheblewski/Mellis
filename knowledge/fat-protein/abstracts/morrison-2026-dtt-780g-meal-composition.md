# A pilot study evaluating meal timing, macronutrient composition, and faster-acting insulin aspart versus insulin aspart on postprandial glycemia with the Medtronic advanced hybrid closed-loop system

- **Authors:** Morrison DJ, Lee MH, Vogrin S, Hennessy D, Netzer E, Jones HM, et al.
- **Journal/year:** Diabetes Technology & Therapeutics 2026;28(1):38-46
- **DOI:** 10.1177/15209156251362500
- **PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40719607/
- **Licence:** not open access (or open-access status not confirmed). This file contains a paraphrased summary of the abstract and key numbers written for the Mellis corpus, not the publisher's text.

## Summary of the abstract (paraphrased)

- **Population:** 12 adults (mean age 48 ± 13 years; HbA1c 6.7 ± 0.7%) on MiniMed 780G AHCL. ACTRN12619001340123.
- **Design:** 11 weeks each of insulin aspart and Fiasp; within each period 12 standardised meals with **60 g carbohydrate** varying in fat/protein (high fat low protein HFLP, high fat high protein HFHP, low fat low protein LFLP), eaten morning and evening, with a **full bolus or a 50% reduced bolus**. Primary outcome 4-h TIR (3.9-10.0 mmol/L).
- **Results:** Fiasp tended to give higher TIR (mean difference 5.17% [-0.09, 10.43]; P = 0.054), mainly for morning meals. Insulin type did not change the effect of full vs 50% bolus or meal type.
- **By meal type (4-h TIR):** **HFLP 83.7% (SD 19.6) > HFHP 74.7% (26.9) > LFLP 62.0% (23.1)**, P < 0.01.

## Notes for Mellis

- Over a **4-h window** on AHCL, higher-fat meals looked *better* than low-fat ones, consistent with fat flattening the early peak; the late (> 4 h) rise was not captured. Shows why the observation window matters when judging fat/protein strategies.
