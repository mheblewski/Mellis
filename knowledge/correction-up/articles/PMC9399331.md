*Our summary (Mellis knowledge base), written in our own words. It is not the original text and not medical advice. Original: Lewis DM, Hussain S. Practical Guidance on Open Source and Commercial Automated Insulin Delivery Systems: A Guide for Healthcare Professionals. Diabetes Ther 2022;13(9):1683–1699, https://doi.org/10.1007/s13300-022-01299-9.*

Narrative guidance for clinicians by an OpenAPS founder and an NHS consultant. This summary covers troubleshooting, cannula changes and insulin-on-board (IOB) calculations.

## Key points

- Many AID problems are basic: check the pump, cannula site, filled reservoir, an active and accurate CGM, and (for phone-based algorithms) that the phone is present and working [Troubleshooting AID].
- Without CGM data the AID system falls back to manual (non-automated) delivery [Troubleshooting AID].
- Cannulas: have a change routine; change in the daytime, preferably not right before a meal or the night; "if in doubt change it out" [Optimizing AID Use; Table 2].
- Some users see glucose rise for 1–2 h after a new cannula, cause unclear (possibly absorption delay or local inflammation) [Optimizing AID Use].
- Commercial calculators often count only bolus insulin as "active insulin"; open-source systems compute net IOB from all basal and bolus insulin, manual or automatic, which can be above or below zero [Optimizing AID Use].
- Not knowing everything the system has delivered can lead to a manual dose on top of insulin that has already corrected the high, causing hypoglycaemia [Optimizing AID Use].

## Keeping the basics working

- Upkeep is like a standalone pump and CGM: site hygiene and keeping the sensor running; schedule sensor changes outside meal times where possible.
- Much troubleshooting concerns communication between devices rather than diabetes itself, which adds burden; users may need new habits compared with manual pump use.
- Missing CGM data (sensor warm-up, noisy readings the system rejects) stops automation until data return.
- Stay in automated mode as much as possible: meet each commercial system's rules for staying automated; for most others keep the CGM session running and Bluetooth connected [Table 2].

## Cannula changes and absorption

AID algorithms assume insulin is absorbed at a set rate; any delay or reduced absorption harms performance. Rotate sites and keep a change routine; a short rise after a new cannula is common and not by itself proof of failure.

## Active insulin vs net IOB

- Traditional pump calculators base "active insulin" on insulin action time and show only remaining manual bolus insulin; temporary basals and suspensions are not included. Some commercial AID systems still work this way and may not show total IOB.
- Open-source AID (e.g. OpenAPS-type systems) computes net insulin from basal and bolus, manual and automatic, using duration of insulin action; the source notes net IOB can be above or below zero.
- Know how your system computes IOB before any manual action: correcting an ongoing high, planning activity, or deciding on extra insulin or snacks [Optimizing AID Use; Table 2].
