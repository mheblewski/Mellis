# Optimizing the combination insulin bolus split for a high-fat, high-protein meal in children and adolescents using insulin pump therapy

- **Authors:** Lopez PE, Smart CE, McElduff P, Foskett DC, Price DA, Paterson MA, King BR
- **Journal/year:** Diabetic Medicine 2017;34(10):1380-1384
- **DOI:** 10.1111/dme.13392
- **PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28574182/
- **Licence:** not open access (or open-access status not confirmed). This file contains a paraphrased summary of the abstract and key numbers written for the Mellis corpus, not the publisher's text.

## Summary of the abstract (paraphrased)

- **Population:** 19 young people with T1D on pumps (mean age 12.9 ± 6.7 years).
- **Design:** randomised repeated-measures trial; one high-fat, high-protein meal (in Furthner 2021 table: 30 g carbohydrate / 35 g fat / 40 g protein); 6 conditions: standard bolus and combination boluses **70/30, 60/40, 50/50, 40/60, 30/70** (upfront/extended), extended part **over 2 h**. Total dose = optimised ICR dose (no extra insulin). 6 h CGM.
- **Early control:** standard bolus and the **70/30 and 60/40** combination boluses controlled the glucose excursion up to 120 min.
- **Late control:** from 240 to 300 min the glucose AUC was significantly lower with **30/70** than with a standard bolus (P = 0.004).
- **Authors' conclusion:** high-fat, high-protein meals need **at least 60% of the ICR dose as the upfront (standard) part** to control the early rise, and additional insulin (up to 70% of the ICR dose) in the extended part to prevent late hyperglycaemia.

## Notes for Mellis

- This is the main paediatric source for "do not give less than 60% upfront". ISPAD 2022 cites it as "optimum split 60/40% or 70/30% delivered over 3 h" (wording in the ISPAD chapter; this abstract says the extended part ran over 2 h).
- Implication for a split proposal: first part ≥ 60% of the carbohydrate dose; the late part should be *added* insulin, not taken away from the first part.
