# Initiating Hybrid Closed Loop: A Program Evaluation of an Educator-Led Control-IQ Follow-Up at a Large Pediatric Clinic

- **Authors:** Messer LH, Berget C, Ernst A, Towers L, Slover RH, Forlenza GP
- **Journal/year:** Pediatric Diabetes 2021;22(4):586-593
- **DOI:** 10.1111/pedi.13183
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8252603/
- **Licence:** paraphrased (open access under CC BY-NC; summarised, short quotes only)
- **Saved:** summary from the PMC full text on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus.

## What it is

Quality-improvement evaluation (Barbara Davis Center, Colorado) of a 2-week educator phone check-in for youth starting Control-IQ, with a clinical decision tool. Relevant because the pediatric clinical tool treats manual bolus overrides as a cause of hypoglycaemia on closed loop.

## Methods

- 123 youth (mean 13.6 y) called a median 18 days after start; benchmarks >=71 % HCL use, >=71 % CGM use, >=60 % TIR and a hypoglycaemia limit; tool suggested dose and behaviour changes.

## Results relevant to correction boluses

- When a user had >5 % hypoglycaemia, educators were prompted to check behaviour first - exercise settings, treating lows with appropriate carbs and **"discouraging bolus overrides"** - and to change carb ratios or sensitivity if lows were due to bolus insulin.
- 60 % met benchmarks at the first call (94 % HCL use, 71 % TIR); among those who did not, a second call improved median TIR by 12.5 % (p = 0.03). Most behaviour advice concerned bolusing.

## Limitations

- Program evaluation, no control group; the effect of discouraging overrides was not measured separately; Control-IQ only; one author group with Tandem ties (LHM later a Tandem employee).
