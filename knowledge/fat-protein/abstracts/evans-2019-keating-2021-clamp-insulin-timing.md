# How much extra insulin and when: insulin-clamp studies of high-protein (Evans 2019) and high-protein high-fat (Keating 2021) meals in young people with T1D

- **Authors:** Evans M, Smart CEM, Paramalingam N, Smith GJ, Jones TW, King BR, Davis EA (2019); Keating B, Smart CEM, Harray AJ, Paramalingam N, Smith G, Jones TW, King BR, Davis EA (2021)
- **Journal/year:** Diabetic Medicine 2019;36(4):499-504; J Clin Endocrinol Metab 2021;106(9):e3611-e3618
- **DOI:** 10.1111/dme.13875; 10.1210/clinem/dgab318
- **PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30537305/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33954780/
- **Licence:** not open access (or open-access status not confirmed). This file contains a paraphrased summary of the abstract and key numbers written for the Mellis corpus, not the publisher's text.

Both studies used a modified insulin clamp (intravenous insulin titrated to keep glucose normal for 5 h), so they measure the *true* insulin need and its time pattern, not the effect of a fixed bolus.

## Evans et al. 2019 - protein (paraphrased)

- 11 participants, age 16.5 ± 2.7 years, HbA1c 6.9 ± 0.8%.
- High-protein (60 g) vs low-protein (5 g) meal, both 30 g carbohydrate and 8 g fat.
- Insulin over 5 h: **10.3 units (CI 8.2-12.57) vs 6.7 units (CI 4.7-8.8)**, P = 0.001: about **50% more** for high protein. Individual range: **0.9 to 6 times** the low-protein requirement.
- Timing of the extra insulin: about half in the **first 2 h** (+1.1 U/h, P = 0.001), +0.5 U/h in hours 2-4 (P = 0.148, not significant), +0.1 U in the last hour (not significant).

## Keating et al. 2021 - protein + fat (paraphrased)

- 10 participants aged 12-21 years, T1D ≥ 1 year, two Australian paediatric centres.
- High-protein high-fat (HPHF: **60 g protein, 40 g fat**) vs low-protein low-fat (5 g protein, 5 g fat), both **30 g carbohydrate**.
- Insulin over 5 h: **11.0 units (CI 9.2-12.8) vs 5.7 units (CI 3.8-7.5)**, P = 0.001: almost **double**.
- Extra insulin needed per period: **0-2 h +1.2 U/h**, **2-4 h +1.1 U/h**, **4-5 h +0.6 U/h**.
- Large individual differences: **0.3 to 5 times** more insulin for HPHF; **0% to 85%** of the extra insulin needed in the first 2 h. Most participants needed about half of the extra insulin in the first 2 h.

## Notes for Mellis

- These contradict the simple picture "fat/protein only matter after 3 h": with a fixed basal and glucose clamped, extra insulin is needed **early as well as late**. In practice, with a bolus, the early need is partly hidden by slower gastric emptying (glucose falls early with a fixed dose; see Paterson 2017, Bell 2020).
- For AID: early extra need can be covered by the loop (SMB/auto-corrections) if glucose rises, which supports giving only part of the extra insulin as a planned late dose.
