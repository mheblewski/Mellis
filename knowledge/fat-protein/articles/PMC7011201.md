*Our summary (Mellis knowledge base), written in our own words. It is not the original text and not medical advice. Original: Evert AB, Dennison M, Gardner CD, et al. Nutrition therapy for adults with diabetes or prediabetes: a consensus report. Diabetes Care 2019;42(5):731-754, https://doi.org/10.2337/dci19-0014. This summary covers only the part on medical nutrition therapy and insulin, including high-fat/high-protein mixed meals.*

## Key points

- For type 1 diabetes, intensive insulin therapy with carbohydrate counting is recommended because it can improve glycaemia [Consensus recommendations].
- For a mixed meal that contains carbohydrate and is high in fat and/or protein, the dose should not rest on carbohydrate counting alone [Consensus recommendations].
- Any increase of the mealtime dose for such meals should be cautious, and CGM or self-monitoring (SMBG) data should guide whether more insulin is given [Consensus recommendations].
- People on fixed daily insulin doses do better with carbohydrate intake that is consistent in timing and amount, taking insulin action time into account; this can also lower hypoglycaemia risk [Consensus recommendations].

## High-fat and high-protein mixed meals

- Studies show the glucose response to such meals varies from person to person, which is the reason for the cautious approach [How should nutrition therapy vary…].
- The concern is delayed hyperglycaemia that may appear 3 hours or more after eating [How should nutrition therapy vary…].
- On an insulin pump, a split bolus (part given at once, the rest over a set duration; also called dual-wave or combination bolus) may cover these meals better [How should nutrition therapy vary…].
- A glucose check about 3 hours after the meal can show whether to adjust, for example by increasing or stopping the bolus [How should nutrition therapy vary…].
- Because such dosing needs an estimate of the meal's nutrients, the person's health literacy and numeracy should be assessed, and results should be reviewed in a structured way with SMBG or CGM to tune individual doses [How should nutrition therapy vary…].

## Basal-bolus therapy and nutrition

- In basal-bolus therapy, nutrition education should focus on adjusting insulin for planned food (mainly carbohydrate), recent or expected physical activity, and glucose data; such programmes improved A1C and quality of life [How should nutrition therapy vary…].
- Dietitians (RDNs) should follow medication changes as part of the nutrition plan, and trained RDNs may help run organisation-approved insulin protocols to reduce hypo- and hyperglycaemia [What is the role of the RDN…].

## Limits of this summary

- Covers one short section of a long adult consensus report; other nutrition topics are not included.
- Written for adults; no specific extra-insulin amount, percentage or split ratio is given in this section.
- The ADA Standards of Care that cite this report may have newer wording; read the original for clinical decisions.
