# Postprandial glucose control with different hybrid closed-loop systems according to type of meal in adults with type 1 diabetes

- **Authors:** Scidà G, Corrado A, Abuqwider J, Lupoli R, Rainone C, Della Pepa G, Masulli M, Annuzzi G, Bozzetto L
- **Journal/year:** Journal of Diabetes Science and Technology 2025;19(5):1331-1340
- **DOI:** 10.1177/19322968241256475
- **PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38840523/ (PMC11571336; full text not available to automated download)
- **Licence:** not open access (or open-access status not confirmed). This file contains a paraphrased summary of the abstract and key numbers written for the Mellis corpus, not the publisher's text.

## Summary of the abstract (paraphrased)

- **Design:** cross-sectional; 7-day food records + HCL reports from **120 adults** (MiniMed 670G n = 40, MiniMed 780G n = 49, Control-IQ n = 31); 570 breakfasts, 658 lunches, 619 dinners split by the median ratio carbohydrate/(fat + protein) (C/FP).
- **Postprandial time in range (70-180 mg/dL):** breakfast 78.3 ± 26.9% (4 h), lunch 72.7 ± 26.1% (6 h), dinner 70.8 ± 27.3% (6 h); no difference between systems.
- Late glucose iAUC after lunch was lower with Control-IQ than with 670G and 780G (P < .05).
- **Insulin pattern differed by system:** 670G and 780G gave more insulin in 0-3 h than 3-6 h; Control-IQ gave more in 3-6 h than 0-3 h (lunch P = .026, dinner P < .001).
- **Conclusion:** independent of the meal's carbohydrate/fat+protein balance, glucose response was similar across systems, achieved through different automatic insulin patterns.

## Notes for Mellis

- Real-world adults; suggests AID systems compensate for fat/protein differently (some front-load, some deliver more late). Trio/oref1 was not studied.
