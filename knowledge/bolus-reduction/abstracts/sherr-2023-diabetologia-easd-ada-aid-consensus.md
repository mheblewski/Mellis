## What it is

The fourth joint EASD/ADA diabetes-technology consensus report. It reviews AID systems with a focus on safety (regulatory approval, adverse-event reporting, cybersecurity, liability, DIY systems) and ends with targeted recommendations for regulators, manufacturers, societies and clinicians. It is not a dosing guideline.

## Points relevant to meals and hypoglycaemia

- **Limitations table (Table 2, behavioural limitations):** users still have to bolus prandial insulin and give correction boluses; must avoid over-correcting hyperglycaemia and adding "fake carbs"; overtreatment of hypoglycaemia is listed as a behavioural limitation.
- **Meal bolus timing (section "Patient perspective"):** with hybrid AID the meal bolus should ideally be given before eating, with accurate carbohydrate assessment and attention to protein and fat. In some systems a delayed full meal bolus can cause hypoglycaemia, because the insulin the system already delivered in response to the post-meal rise overlaps with the late bolus. The report says that when bolusing after the meal **some patients may need to reduce the meal bolus** to account for insulin already given by the system. No percentage is given.
- **Hypoglycaemia treatment (same section):** because basal insulin will have been suspended, fewer carbohydrates are needed to return to normal glucose; people should be educated to overcome fear of hypoglycaemia and not overcorrect, which often causes hyperglycaemia on AID. The report notes an anecdotal observation that users can be high **30-40 min after a corrected low** even after a small treatment, because the system keeps delivery suspended after the low has resolved; it attributes this to exaggerated sensor lag during hypoglycaemia. No gram amount is given.
- **Insulin on board:** users should understand that the system's own extra insulin limits how large a manual correction the calculator allows.
- **Young children (section "Special populations"):** remote viewing of both CGM and insulin delivery by parents is described as critical; for young children (and older adults in care) it may be best to keep only basic functions on the pump to prevent errant bolus delivery.
- **DIY AID:** not approved by regulators or recommended by societies; clinicians should tell users the systems are off-label, support safety and a back-up plan, and document problems.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance to reducing a meal bolus when falling / predicted low:** supports the principle that a late meal bolus on AID should be smaller than the full calculated bolus (system insulin already given), and that rescue carbohydrate should be smaller than standard; gives no numbers for either and nothing on a meal eaten while low.
- **Population:** all people with insulin-treated diabetes; brief paediatric remarks only.
- **AID systems:** commercial systems in general; DIY systems (OpenAPS, Loop, AndroidAPS) discussed only from a regulatory/liability angle.
- **Evidence level:** joint society consensus report (expert opinion), safety-focused.
