# Therapy Adjustments Based on Trend Arrows Using Continuous Glucose Monitoring Systems

- **Authors:** Ziegler R, von Sengbusch S, Kröger J, Schubert O, Werkmeister P, Deiss D, Siegmund T
- **Journal/year:** Journal of Diabetes Science and Technology 2019;13(4):763–773 (epub 2019 Jan 22)
- **DOI:** 10.1177/1932296818822539
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6610609/
- **Licence:** paraphrased (© 2019 Diabetes Technology Society; free to read in PMC, no open licence stated)
- **Saved:** summary from the PMC full text (tables read from the PMC HTML) on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus. The numbers below are re-laid-out from Tables 4–6; prose is paraphrased.

## What the paper is

A German expert group (paediatric and adult diabetologists; Abbott advisory board members; writing funded by Abbott) proposes colour-coded "scorecards" for adjusting a bolus by CGM trend arrow. Unlike the Endocrine Society tables (Aleppo 2017, Laffel 2017, Kudva 2018), the adjustment depends on **both** the correction factor (CF, mg/dL per unit) **and** the current glucose band: <70, 70–180, 180–250, >250 mg/dL, plus a ketone-positive column. There are three scorecards: adults with type 1 diabetes (Table 4), insulin-treated type 2 diabetes (Table 5) and children/adolescents with type 1 diabetes (Table 6). Each row says which arrow it applies to for Abbott/Roche (Libre, Eversense), Dexcom and Medtronic sensors, because the same symbol means different rates on different devices. It is a general paper (adults and children), not a paediatric-only paper.

## Arrow definitions and time horizon (Tables 1–2)

- Arrows are computed from the rate of change over the last 15 minutes and indicate roughly how interstitial glucose will move over the **next 30 minutes** if the trend holds (example in text: 2 mg/dL/min ≈ 60 mg/dL in 30 min). Below 1 mg/dL/min counts as stable.
- Rates (mg/dL/min) per device: Libre ↗/↘ 1–2, ↑/↓ >2. Dexcom ↗/↘ 1–2, ↑/↓ 2–3, ↑↑/↓↓ >3. Medtronic 640G ↑/↓ 1–2, ↑↑/↓↓ 2–3, ↑↑↑/↓↓↓ >3. Medtronic Veo ↑/↓ 1–2, ↑↑/↓↓ >2. Eversense ↗/↘ 1–2 (flat 0–1), ↑/↓ >2.
- The paper cites a study where arrow predictions matched later blood glucose in only about 61% (Dexcom G5) and 59% (Libre) of cases. If the arrow and the current curve disagree (e.g., after treating a low the arrow still points down), decide on the current CGM data, not the arrow.

## Previously published methods (Table 3, summarised)

- JDRF/DirecNet: change the total dose by up to 20% (10% for slow, 20% for faster arrows).
- Scheiner: add/subtract a glucose amount to the current value before applying the correction rule (as listed here: 0, 25, 50 mg/dL for the three arrow strengths). Pettus/Edelman: 50, 75, 100 mg/dL.
- Klonoff/Kerr: assume a change of 67, 112 or ≥135 mg/dL over 45 minutes → ±1, ±1.5, ±2 U.
- Aleppo/Laffel: fixed units by CF band, separate adult and child tables.
- Self-report surveys: people often raise doses by >100% for up arrows but cut them by only up to about 50% for down arrows.

## Scorecard values: falling arrows (insulin units added to the calculated meal/correction bolus)

Arrow mapping for these rows: "slight down" = Libre ↘ / Dexcom ↘ / Medtronic ↓; "down" = Libre ↓ / Dexcom ↓ / Medtronic ↓↓; "double down" = Dexcom ↓↓ / Medtronic ↓↓↓ (Libre has no such arrow).

## Rules attached to the scorecards (footnotes and text)

- **Carbohydrate units (CU):** defined individually as the amount of carbohydrate a person knows, from experience, prevents a low; e.g., if 10 g is needed at 50–70 mg/dL, 2 fast-acting CU = 20 g. Below 50 mg/dL: 3 fast-acting CU.
- Ketones positive: use the individually agreed maximal correction regardless of arrow.
- No further correction within 90 minutes, ideally not within 2 hours of the last insulin dose.
- The values are guides to be individualised; the authors call for confirmation in studies (an investigator-initiated study was planned).
- Worked examples in the text: adult T1D, 100 mg/dL, Dexcom ↑↑, CF 40 → +2.5 U. Insulin-treated T2D, 100 mg/dL, Libre ↓, CF 20 → −3 U.
- **Meal bolus:** with strong rising or falling trends the 30-minute glucose may differ a lot from the value used for dosing, so the trend can be built into the mealtime dose and into corrections about 2 h after a meal, for both MDI and pump users. Pump users may also use temporary basal rates and extended/dual-wave boluses.
- **Pumps with predictive low-glucose suspend (PLGS):** use the scorecard for rising arrows; for falling arrows rely mainly on the pump's automatic suspend to prevent hypoglycaemia. Automated insulin delivery (hybrid closed loop) is not mentioned.
- Other factors to weigh: meal composition (carbohydrate, protein, fat), portion size, activity, medication, stress, illness.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance to reducing a meal bolus when glucose is falling / a low is predicted:** high. It is the only scheme here that scales the reduction by current glucose and stops dosing below 70 mg/dL (carbohydrate units instead of insulin). Within 70–180 mg/dL its falling-arrow reductions for adults are identical to the Endocrine Society adult values (Aleppo 2017; e.g., Dexcom ↓↓, CF 25–<50: −3.5 U); at 180–250 mg/dL they are smaller. In the children's card (70–180 mg/dL) the values equal Laffel 2017 for CF 25–<75, but for very insulin-sensitive children they are larger than Laffel's (CF 75–<125: ↘ −0.5 vs 0, ↓ −1 vs −0.5, ↓↓ −1.5 vs −1.0; CF ≥125: ↘ −0.5 vs 0, ↓ −0.75 to −1 vs 0, ↓↓ −1 to −1.5 vs −0.5) and fractional (−0.2 to −0.75 U), which only pumps can deliver. The unit decrement is applied to the whole calculated bolus (meal + correction), so it can cancel a small meal dose entirely.
- **AID:** written for MDI and open-loop/PLGS pumps; for falling arrows with PLGS the pump's suspend is preferred over manual reduction. The Brazilian position statement (PMC7780381) built on this paper and says to avoid arrow adjustments with closed loop. A later crossover trial (Parise 2023, PMC10002216) found this scorecard improved TIR versus the ±10/20% rule in adults.
- **Population:** adults T1D, insulin-treated T2D, children/adolescents T1D (no age or weight bands, only CF bands).
- **Evidence level:** expert opinion (industry-supported); not validated in the paper.
