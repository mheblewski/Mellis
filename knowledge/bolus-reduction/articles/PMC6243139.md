*Our summary (Mellis knowledge base), written in our own words. It is not the original text and not medical advice. Original: Kudva YC, Ahmann AJ, Bergenstal RM, Gavin JR, Kruger DF, Midyett LK, Miller E, Harris DR. Approach to Using Trend Arrows in the FreeStyle Libre Flash Glucose Monitoring Systems in Adults. J Endocr Soc 2018;2(12):1320–1337, https://doi.org/10.1210/js.2018-00294.*

## Key points

- An Endocrine Society expert panel adapted the 2017 Dexcom trend-arrow method (Aleppo et al.) to the FreeStyle Libre systems, which have no double arrows and (in the US versions described) no automatic alarms [Introduction; Section 2A].
- Dose formula: total dose = carbohydrate dose ± correction ± trend-arrow adjustment. The arrow adjustment is a fixed number of units chosen by correction factor (CF) band and is added on top of the usual ICR/CF calculation, never instead of it [Section 2A, Figure 2].
- Use arrow adjustments before meals and for corrections given 4 h or more after a meal; do not use them 2–4 h after a meal bolus [Figure 2; Figure 3].
- Falling quickly (↓) before a meal: consider injecting closer to the meal; at or below about 150 mg/dL (8.3 mmol/L) consider holding the pre-meal dose until glucose is stable. Rising quickly (↑): consider injecting 15–30 min before eating [Figure 2, considerations].
- 2–4 h after a meal, 70–180 mg/dL with a falling arrow: take 15 g fast-acting carbohydrate and recheck in 15–30 min [Figure 3].
- Avoid any corrective bolus in the first 2 h after a meal bolus (insulin stacking) [Figure 3; Section 2B].
- Evidence: expert opinion; the method itself was not tested in a trial [Discussion].

## Trend arrows: rate of change

Libre trend arrows are computed with most weight on the last 15 min of readings; the system measures every minute [Section 1B]. Conversion used: mg/dL × 0.0555 = mmol/L.

| Arrow | Meaning | Rate | Over 30 min |
|---|---|---|---|
| ↑ | rising quickly | > 2 mg/dL/min | > 60 mg/dL |
| ↗ | rising | 1–2 mg/dL/min | 30–60 mg/dL |
| → | changing slowly | < 1 mg/dL/min | up to about ±30 mg/dL |
| ↘ | falling | 1–2 mg/dL/min | 30–60 mg/dL |
| ↓ | falling quickly | > 2 mg/dL/min | > 60 mg/dL |

A flat arrow does not mean "no change". No arrow means the device cannot compute a direction. Predictions beyond about 20–30 min are less reliable [Figure 1].

## Insulin dose adjustment by trend arrow (pre-meal and corrections ≥4 h after a meal)

Units of rapid-acting insulin added to (+) or taken away from (−) the standard ICR/CF result. CF = mg/dL fall per 1 unit [Figure 2].

| CF band (mg/dL per U) | ↑ | ↗ | → | ↘ | ↓ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| < 25 | +3.5 | +2.5 | 0 | −2.5 | −3.5 |
| 25 to < 50 | +2.5 | +1.5 | 0 | −1.5 | −2.5 |
| 50 to < 75 | +1.5 | +1.0 | 0 | −1.0 | −1.5 |
| ≥ 75 | +1.0 | +0.5 | 0 | −0.5 | −1.0 |

Rules around the table [Figure 2; Section 2A]:
- Start at mealtimes and start conservatively. A single up arrow may still under-represent the true rise.
- Older or frail adults: cut increases by at least half (e.g. +1.0 → +0.5 or nothing) and enlarge reductions by at least half (e.g. −1.0 → −1.5 or −2.0).
- Rounding: compute the whole dose first, then round to a whole or half unit; at an exact 0.5 midpoint round up with flat or upward arrows and down with downward arrows.
- The panel kept the Dexcom single-arrow values rather than enlarging them for the missing double arrows, judging this the safer choice for a device without alarms; rates above 3 mg/dL/min were thought uncommon without food or exercise.
- Pump (CSII) users may need more conservative adjustments when they are also using temporary basal rates after meals.
- Do not use arrow adjustments to fix a missed meal dose, a carb under-count from an earlier meal, or over-treated hypoglycaemia; there the arrow is a signal of the error, not a dosing input.

## Post-meal monitoring 2–4 h after a meal bolus

Based on the REPLACE-BG protocol; applies to injections and pumps. In this window use only current glucose with CF/ICR, not arrow adjustments, and give no extra bolus in the first 2 h [Figure 3].

| Glucose 2–4 h after meal | ↑ | ↗ | → / ↘ / ↓ |
|---|---|---|---|
| > 250 mg/dL (13.9 mmol/L) | correct by CF, recheck in 1 h; if still ↑ after another hour: meter check, change pump site, correct again | correct by CF, recheck in 1 h, no further correction for 2 h | no action, recheck in 1 h |
| 181–250 mg/dL | consider correction by CF, recheck in 1 h, no further correction for 2 h | same as ↑ | → no action, recheck 1 h; ↘ or ↓ no action, recheck in 30 min |
| 70–180 mg/dL | no action, recheck in 1 h | no action, recheck in 1 h | → no action, recheck 1 h; ↘ or ↓: 15 g fast-acting carbs, recheck in 15–30 min; if ≤ 70 mg/dL with a falling arrow at 30 min, confirm by meter and take another 15 g |

Further notes [Figure 3; Section 2B]:
- Pump users should correct through the pump bolus calculator, which accounts for insulin on board (IOB).
- People on injections without an IOB-aware calculator are generally advised to give 50% of the calculated correction in this window.
- Confirm with a fingerstick when the arrow is straight up or down (> 2 mg/dL/min) and to confirm a low or impending low.
- After 4 h most carbohydrate is assumed absorbed and bolus IOB gone, so arrow adjustments apply again. Ultra-rapid insulins were outside the scope.

## Scanning frequency (Table 2)

Suggested scans: on waking, before meals, 2 h after meals, at bedtime, at least every 8 h (the reader keeps only 8 h of history); hourly for 4 h to study a meal or after a correction for high glucose; every 15 min after treating a low until stable; every 2 h on sick days; at least every 4 h for older or frail people; before exercise, every 15–30 min during, right after, and again 6–8 h later.

## Exercise: pre-exercise plan by glucose and arrow (Figure 4)

Aerobic or mixed exercise. Below 100 mg/dL (5.6 mmol/L) do not start; above 250 mg/dL (13.9 mmol/L) do not start.

| Arrow | < 100 mg/dL | 100–180 mg/dL (exercise with care) | 181–250 mg/dL | > 250 mg/dL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ↑ or ↗ | wait until > 100 | recheck in 30 min | recheck in 30 min | correct to ≤ 180 |
| → | 15 g carbs | consider 15 g | recheck in 30 min | correct to ≤ 180 |
| ↘ | 15 g carbs | consider 15 g | recheck in 30 min | wait until ≤ 250 |
| ↓ | 30 g carbs | consider 30 g | consider 15 g | wait until ≤ 250 |

Also: wait about 2 h after a bolus before starting; with anaerobic exercise take fewer carbs and do not stack insulin if glucose rises; watch for delayed lows 6–8 h later; plan sessions over 150 min with the care team; consider ketone testing above 250 mg/dL with rising arrows [Figure 4].

## Sick days, interfering substances, special groups

- Illness: scan more often; consider corrections every 2–3 h with ketone checks; use fingerstick values for decisions when glucose is > 250 mg/dL with ketones, or < 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L) or with low symptoms [Section 4A].
- Interference (Libre): ascorbic acid (vitamin C) ≥ 500 mg can read falsely high; salicylic acid (aspirin) ≥ 650 mg can read falsely low; acetaminophen does not interfere with these systems [Section 4A; Table 3 case G].
- Bedtime: arrow adjustments may be used but extra insulin at bedtime calls for caution [Section 4].
- Older and frail adults: higher risk of severe hypoglycaemia; start conservatively [Section 4C]. Young adults: focus on staying in range rather than single numbers [Section 4B].
- Temperature, altitude and humidity are not reported to affect CGM accuracy, though they affect meters [Section 4E].

## Limits of this summary

- Adults only (US label ≥ 18 years) and mainly people on injections; no guidance for automated insulin delivery (AID) or closed-loop systems.
- Expert opinion, not trial evidence; numbers assume well-set ICR and CF and rapid-acting (not ultra-rapid) insulin.
- Tables 1 and 4 of the original (device features, exercise types) are not covered; device details refer to the 2018 US Libre models.
- Read the original before any clinical decision.
