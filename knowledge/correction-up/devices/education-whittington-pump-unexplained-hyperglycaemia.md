# Managing Unexplained Hyperglycaemia on an Insulin Pump - Whittington Health NHS Trust (glucose > 13 mmol/L: one pump correction, recheck in 1-2 h, then pen correction and set change; no sleep within 2 h of a set change)

- **DOI:** none
- **URL:** https://www.whittington.nhs.uk/document.ashx?id=15552

## Rules for unexplained hyperglycaemia (glucose over 13.0 mmol/L)

1. Take a correction bolus with the pump.
2. Check glucose and ketones in 1-2 hours; if unchanged or higher, take a correction injection by syringe or pen (the pump site could be faulty).
3. Change cannula, infusion set and insulin reservoir.
4. Check glucose and ketones again in 1-2 hours and correct through the pump if still above target.
5. Use sick day rules if ketones are positive.
6. Do not go to sleep with unresolved unexplained hyperglycaemia or within 2 hours of a new set, patch or Pod change; change sets early in the day so glucose can be checked 1-2 h after insertion.

- Reminder box: "If in doubt change it out!" - if glucose is above 15.0 mmol/L for more than 2 hours and not reducing, change the cannula.
- Checklist of causes: air or leaks in tubing, unfilled cannula after insertion, dislodged or kinked cannula, set worn longer than 2-3 days, redness or blood at the site, set off for more than 1 h, lumpy skin; forgotten or wrong bolus, alarms, empty cartridge, wrong time or basal; expired or heat-exposed insulin.
- Keep in-date rapid- and long-acting pens as back-up for pump failure.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance (moderate; open-loop pump rule):** the classic "one pump correction, then pen" algorithm with a 1-2 h recheck interval. It applies to pump therapy without automation; for HCL the same trust refers to its HCL leaflet (see the ABCD-DTN UK leaflet). Useful for Trio when the loop is not running (open loop / no CGM), where Trio behaves like a conventional pump.
- **Population:** adults and young people on conventional pumps who can self-manage.
- **Evidence level:** [Ed] hospital patient information.
