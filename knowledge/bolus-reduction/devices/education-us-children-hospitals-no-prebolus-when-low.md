# Do not pre-bolus when low before a meal (Children's Health Dallas article and Diabetes Self-Management tip)

- **Authors:** (1) Children's Health, Dallas, Texas — article quoting Soumya Adhikari, MD (pediatric endocrinologist) and Kim Jernigan, CDE; (2) Diane Fennell, Diabetes Self-Management
- **Journal/year:** (1) web article, Jan 2020, updated Mar 2023; (2) web tip, 4 Mar 2017
- **DOI:** none
- **URL:** (1) https://www.childrens.com/health-wellness/the-importance-of-pre-bolusing-on-insulin ; (2) https://www.diabetesselfmanagement.com/tips/blood-glucose-low-meal-take-insulin/
- **Licence:** paraphrased
- **Saved:** summaries from the two web pages on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus. Patient education material (lower evidence).

## (1) Children's Health Dallas — pre-bolusing

- Pre-bolus = insulin several minutes before the child eats; 5–10 min or longer depending on circumstances, because common rapid insulins start working after about 10–20 min.
- Pre-bolusing should not be "always or never"; watch glucose trends closely (ideally with CGM) when starting.
- The CDE advises NOT to pre-bolus when the child's glucose is low heading into a meal.
- No guidance on dose reduction or giving insulin after the meal.

## (2) Diabetes Self-Management tip

- If glucose is low before a meal, wait until 15 minutes after the first bite before taking the insulin.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance:** Consistent US education rule: low (or falling) at mealtime → no pre-bolus; dose at/after the start of eating (one source: +15 min after the first bite). Neither source says to reduce the dose or subtract treatment carbs.
- **Population:** (1) children; (2) general adult readership; MDI/pump; not AID.
- **Evidence level:** expert/educator opinion.
