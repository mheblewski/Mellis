# A Clinical Guide to Advanced Diabetes Devices and Closed-Loop Systems Using the CARES Paradigm

- **Authors:** Messer LH, Berget C, Forlenza GP (Barbara Davis Center for Diabetes, University of Colorado)
- **Journal/year:** Diabetes Technology & Therapeutics 2019;21(8):462-469
- **DOI:** 10.1089/dia.2019.0105
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6653788/
- **Licence:** paraphrased (© 2019 Mary Ann Liebert, Inc.; free to read on PMC but not under an open licence)
- **Saved:** summary from the PMC page on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus (paraphrased; short quotes only).

## What it is

A framework for clinicians to understand any automated insulin delivery device in five domains: how it **C**alculates insulin, what can be **A**djusted, when to **R**evert to manual mode, key **E**ducation points, and **S**ensor/**S**haring features. It applies the framework to the devices available in 2019: low-glucose suspend and predictive low-glucose suspend (Medtronic 630G/640G, Tandem Basal-IQ) and hybrid closed loop (Medtronic 670G, Tandem Control-IQ); Omnipod Horizon and DIY Loop are mentioned only as systems in development. The framework was later adopted by the ATTD AID consensus (Phillip 2023) and ISPAD 2024.

## Points relevant to meals and hypoglycaemia

- **Meal boluses:** hybrid systems still need the user to bolus for meals and for high glucose; the education points for both HCL systems say a pre-meal bolus remains important for mealtime control. Missed or late boluses are not addressed.
- **Bolus size:** for the 670G the authors suggest the insulin-to-carb ratio may need strengthening by about 10-25 % (the algorithm does not give automatic corrections); for Control-IQ they advise against overriding the bolus calculator, because the system may already have added insulin through automatic corrections and raised basal; Control-IQ uses a fixed 5-hour active insulin time.
- **Hypoglycaemia treatment:** for suspend systems and both HCL systems, the education points advise to "consider treating hypoglycemia with less CHO (e.g., 5–10)" grams when insulin was suspended before the low.
- **Pre-meal lows / falling glucose:** not discussed (the only "before meals" advice concerns calibrating the sensor when glucose is stable).
- **Children:** no child-specific dosing advice, although the framework was developed and used in a paediatric clinic.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance to reducing a meal bolus when falling / predicted low:** low; it is the origin of the "5-10 g after a suspension" rule cited by later consensus papers, and of the advice not to override bolus calculators on AID. Nothing on a reduced meal bolus.
- **Population:** paediatric clinic context, devices for all ages.
- **AID systems:** 670G, Control-IQ (HCL); Basal-IQ, 630G/640G (suspend systems).
- **Evidence level:** clinical guide / expert opinion; full text could not be quoted at length (not openly licensed), so details were taken from the PMC page and are paraphrased.
