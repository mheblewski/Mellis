# Blood glucose — insulin vs food: why give insulin before eating? (Washington University Diabetes Team, St. Louis)

- **Authors:** Washington University (St. Louis) pediatric diabetes team (document author field: K. Hamon)
- **Journal/year:** patient handout, 2020 (PDF created Sept 2021)
- **DOI:** none
- **URL:** https://pediatricendocrinology.wustl.edu/files/2021/09/Meal-time-dosing-2020.pdf
- **Licence:** paraphrased
- **Saved:** summary from the PDF on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus. Patient education material (lower evidence).

## Content

- Insulin vs food is described as a race: dose up to 15–20 min before eating so insulin starts working when glucose starts rising; a graph compares dosing 10–15 min before vs after eating.
- Did not finish the meal after a pre-bolus: depends on the amount missed and the insulin-to-carb ratio; if a significant amount must be replaced, white milk is suggested; candy, soda or juice should not be used to replace missed meal carbs.
- Wants more food: give an additional injection for the extra carbs.
- Young children/toddlers who eat unpredictably may need insulin after eating; most children should move to pre-meal dosing.

## Low before eating (key rule)

- Whenever possible, first treat the low with 15 g carbohydrate, then dose for the meal's carbohydrate and eat.
- The handout states plainly: "Do not count carbs for low treatment in the meal time dose."

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance:** The clearest education statement that hypo-treatment carbohydrate is NOT bolused: treatment carbs are excluded from the meal dose, the meal carbs are dosed normally. Matches the Mellis idea "carbs covering the drop are not bolused", but here the treatment is a separate fixed 15 g before the meal, not a computed share of the meal.
- **Population:** children/teens (pediatric endocrinology clinic), mainly MDI.
- **Evidence level:** clinic patient handout (expert opinion).
