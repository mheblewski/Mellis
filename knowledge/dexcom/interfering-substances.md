*Our summary (Mellis knowledge base), written in our own words. It is not the original text and not medical advice. Original: Dexcom, Inc., "Interfering substances and risks" (Dexcom US web page, © 2026 Dexcom, Inc.), https://www.dexcom.com/en-us/interference.*

## Key points

- The page names two medicines that are known to interfere with Dexcom G6 and G7 sensor readings: hydroxyurea and acetaminophen (paracetamol) [Hydroxyurea; Acetaminophen].
- Hydroxyurea: sensor glucose reads higher than true glucose. Do not use G7 for treatment decisions while taking it; ask the physician about another way of monitoring glucose [Hydroxyurea].
- Acetaminophen / paracetamol: with G6 and G7, a standard or maximum dose of 1 g (1,000 mg) every 6 hours still allows treatment decisions from sensor readings. Taking more than the maximum (for example more than 1 g every 6 hours in adults) may push readings falsely high [Acetaminophen].
- Older systems (G4/G5) were affected by acetaminophen even at usual doses [Acetaminophen].
- Ibuprofen: the page states there are no reports of interference with Dexcom CGM [Acetaminophen].

## Hydroxyurea and falsely high sensor glucose [Hydroxyurea]

- Hydroxyurea is used for some cancers and blood disorders.
- Effect: readings above the real glucose level. How large the error is depends on how much of the drug is in the body.
- Risks named: hypoglycaemia alerts that do not fire, and dosing mistakes such as giving more insulin because of a falsely high value.
- Recommendation: no treatment decisions from the sensor while on hydroxyurea; discuss alternative monitoring (for example BG meter) with the physician.

## Acetaminophen (paracetamol) dose limit [Acetaminophen]

- Up to 1,000 mg every 6 hours: sensor readings can still be used for decisions with G6 and G7.
- Above the maximum dose: readings may look higher than true glucose. The page gives no further instruction; the G7 user guide's general rule (use a BG meter when readings and symptoms do not match) is summarised in `dexcom-g7-user-guide`.
- No difference between G6 and G7 is described on this page; both are covered by the same statements (the hydroxyurea recommendation is worded for G7).

## Not covered by this page

- Vitamin C (ascorbic acid), other substances, MRI, CT, X-ray and diathermy are not discussed here; for imaging see the G7 user guide summary (`dexcom-g7-user-guide`).

## Limits of this summary

- A short US manufacturer web page; other regions' labelling may list further substances or different wording.
- Applies to Dexcom G6 and G7 only, not to other CGM brands (e.g. Libre, Medtronic sensors).
- No study data or numbers on the size of the error are given.
- Check the current Dexcom page or user guide and the treating team before acting.
