# Hypoglycaemia in children with type 1 diabetes (Worcestershire Acute Hospitals NHS Trust, key document WAHT-TP-045 v8)

- **Authors:** Worcestershire Acute Hospitals NHS Trust paediatric team (document owner: Dr West, consultant paediatrician); approved by the Paediatric Quality Improvement meeting, 9 Feb 2024, review due 9 Feb 2027
- **Journal/year:** NHS trust clinical guideline, 2024 (references NICE NG18)
- **DOI:** none
- **URL:** https://apps.worcsacute.nhs.uk/KeyDocumentPortal/Home/DownloadFile/1763
- **Licence:** paraphrased (NHS trust document, no open licence stated)
- **Saved:** summary from the trust PDF on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus. Education / clinical-guideline material (lower evidence than trials).

## Treatment amounts

- Give 5–15 g glucose, calculated as 0.3 g/kg. Example in the flow chart: 3 dextrose tablets (or 110 mL Lucozade Energy) for about 30 kg, 5 tablets (or 165 mL) for about 50 kg; half for under 3 years, double for teenagers.
- Closed-loop (AID) pump users: less glucose, e.g. 5–10 g, or half the usual dose.
- Re-test after 10–15 min; repeat if still < 4 mmol/L. After two failed treatments, escalate.
- Expected effect: treatment should raise glucose by about 3–4 mmol/L.
- Juice/sugar: more is needed than glucose; the document states 10 g oral glucose gives about the same rise as 20 g carbohydrate from fruit juice.
- Chocolate, milk and other fat-containing foods absorb more slowly and should not be the first treatment.

## Hypo at mealtime (key rule)

- If the hypo happens just before a meal when insulin is usually given: treat the hypo first; once glucose is above 4.0 mmol/L (72 mg/dL), give the mealtime insulin as usual.
- After the hypo: give the usual insulin doses; do not add extra insulin for a high reading afterwards, because a rebound rise is expected.
- Follow-up starchy snack (< 15 g) only if no meal is due within 30 minutes; pump users do not need the starchy snack.
- Repeated hypos on closed loop: use the exercise/activity setting (examples: Omnipod 5 target 8.3 mmol/L; Medtronic 780G temp target 8.3 mmol/L).

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance:** Clear UK hospital rule: treat first, wait until > 4.0 mmol/L, then give the usual meal insulin (no explicit reduction, no explicit subtraction of the treatment carbs because the treatment is given separately, before the meal). Also gives the AID-specific smaller dose (5–10 g / half).
- **Population:** children with T1D in hospital/inpatient context; MDI, pump and AID mentioned.
- **Evidence level:** local clinical guideline (expert consensus, based on NICE/ISPAD).
