*Our summary (Mellis knowledge base), written in our own words. It is not the original text and not medical advice. Original: Prince NL, Lochnan HA, Shorr R, Garon-Mailer A, Sun CJ. Systematic Review of Oral Carbohydrate Treatment for Non-Severe Hypoglycemia in Type 1 Diabetes: A Comparison of Insulin Management Systems. Diabetes Metab Syndr Obes 2026;19:568538, https://doi.org/10.2147/DMSO.S568538.*

## Key points

- The usual "15 g, recheck in 15 minutes" rule rests on weak evidence (expert opinion from two small IV-insulin studies) [Abstract; Introduction].
- Goal of the review: which oral carb amounts resolve more than half of non-severe lows (3.0–3.9 mmol/L, ≈54–70 mg/dL) by the first recheck; 7 studies included [Abstract].
- Without closed loop, lows not caused by an insulin overdose: 20 g glucose (exercise) or 0.2 g/kg, 0.3 g/kg or 15 g glucose (real-life) resolved 60–71% at 10–15 min [Discussion; Table 1].
- Lows caused by an excess **subcutaneous bolus** often needed **more than 32 g**; 16 g resolved only 19–31% and 32 g 24–47% [Table 1; Implications].
- On closed loop / low-glucose suspend: 10–15 g sucrose in water resolved 79–100% at 15 min when the starting value was above 2.8 mmol/L (≈50 mg/dL); 20–30 g was given at or below 2.8 [Table 2; Discussion].
- In supervised closed-loop studies, 15–16 g resolved 38–45% at 15 min, rising to 58% at 20 min and 69% at 30 min (Gingras 2018), i.e., glucose kept rising after the recheck [Closed-loop results].
- Rebound hyperglycaemia (most often defined as >10 mmol/L at 60 min) was rare [Results; Discussion].

## Hypoglycaemia treatment without closed loop (MDI / standard pump)

[Table 1; Four studies]
| Study | Cause | Treatment | Recheck | Resolved |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Broz 2021 | exercise | 20 g glucose in water | 15 min | 60% |
| McTavish 2015 | real life | 0.2 g/kg (10.5–19.5 g) / 0.3 g/kg (15–30 g) / 15 g tablets | 10 min | 61% / 71% / 63% |
| Cheng 2024 | s.c. rapid-insulin bolus | 16 g tablets | 20 min | 31% |
| Taleb 2023 #1 | s.c. rapid-insulin bolus | 16 g or 32 g tablets (two glucose levels) | 15 min | 19–21% (16 g); 24–47% (32 g) |

0.3 g/kg added only about 10 percentage points over 0.2 g/kg. Up to about 10% had rebound with 32 g.

## Hypoglycaemia treatment on closed-loop (AID) pumps

[Table 2; Three studies] Gingras 2018 (47 adults + 10 adolescents, supervised): 16 g glucose tablets, 38% / 58% / 69% at 15 / 20 / 30 min. Taleb 2023 #2: 15 g liquid or 16 g tablets, 45% at 15 min. Grassi 2021 (real life, pump with predictive low-glucose suspend when predicted <3.3 mmol/L within 30 min): sucrose 10 g (or 20 g if ≤2.8 mmol/L) → 79%; 15 g (or 30 g if ≤2.8) → 100% at 15 min. The authors explain the better results by the pump lowering or stopping insulin delivery.

## Treating earlier and other findings

- Cheng 2024 found that starting treatment at <4.5 or <5.0 mmol/L (≈81 or 90 mg/dL) rather than <3.9 cut time below 3.9 mmol/L without more rebound [Discussion].
- The authors propose a common rebound definition (>10 mmol/L at 60 min) and suggest guidelines offer a wider range of carb amounts depending on cause and insulin system [Discussion; Implications].

## Limits of this summary

- Adults with T1D (one study mixed in 10 adolescents); no children's data.
- Seven small, heterogeneous studies; only descriptive synthesis was possible; research closed-loop systems, no commercial AID named.
- Methods, risk-of-bias details and references are left out.
- Read the original for clinical decisions.
