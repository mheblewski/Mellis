# Weight-based carbohydrate treatment of hypoglycaemia in people with Type 1 diabetes using insulin pump therapy: a randomized crossover clinical trial

- **Authors:** McTavish L, Corley B, Weatherall M, Wiltshire E, Krebs JD
- **Journal/year:** Diabetic Medicine 2018
- **DOI:** 10.1111/dme.13576
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29285796/
- **Licence:** paraphrased
- **Saved:** summary from the PubMed/Europe PMC abstract on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus.

## Question

In children and adults on insulin pumps, does 0.3 g/kg glucose treat hypoglycaemia better than "usual" fixed doses, without excess rebound?

## Design

- Randomized crossover; each hypo (capillary glucose < 4.0 mmol/L) randomly treated with glucose tablets as either 0.3 g/kg, or usual care: 15 g (adults) / 10 g (children) for glucose 3.0–3.9 mmol/L, double that for < 3.0 mmol/L.
- Re-test at 10 min; repeat dose if still < 4 mmol/L. Up to 10 episodes per participant.

## Results

- 37 enrolled, 35 completed (age 6–68 y).
- Glucose 10 min after treatment was higher with weight-based dosing by 0.33 mmol/L in adults (95% CI 0.005–0.66) and 0.45 mmol/L in children (95% CI 0.18–0.72).
- Odds of resolving the hypo with a single dose at 10 min: OR 3.12 in adults, 2.61 in children, in favour of 0.3 g/kg.

## Authors' conclusion (paraphrased)

0.3 g/kg glucose was more effective than the fixed-dose international recommendations for symptomatic hypoglycaemia in pump users of all ages.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance:** Confirms 0.3 g/kg as the reference "treatment amount" in children on pumps (pre-AID). Useful to define how many grams of a meal can be considered "hypo treatment" (e.g. 30 kg child = 9 g).
- **Related adult trial:** McTavish 2015 (Diabet Med, 10.1111/dme.12730): 0.3 g/kg beat 15 g by 0.26 mmol/L at 10 min; 0.2 g/kg was not better than 15 g; only 3 of ~409 episodes rose above 8 mmol/L (little rebound).
- **AID caveat:** ISPAD 2022 suggests less (5–10 g, or about half) on AID systems; see the AID-specific sources (Pinsker etc.) covered elsewhere in the corpus.
- **Evidence level:** randomized crossover trial, n = 35, capillary glucose outcomes.
