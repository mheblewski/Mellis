# Analysis of guidelines for basal-bolus insulin dosing: basal insulin, correction factor, and carbohydrate-to-insulin ratio

- **Authors:** Davidson PC, Hebblewhite HR, Steed RD, Bode BW
- **Journal/year:** Endocrine Practice 2008;14(9):1095
- **DOI:** 10.4158/EP.14.9.1095
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19158048/
- **Licence:** paraphrased
- **Saved:** summary from the PubMed/Europe PMC abstract on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus.

## Design

- Retrospective analysis of pump patients in a large endocrine practice: well-controlled test group (n = 167; HbA1c ≤ 7%, > 180 days on pump, no severe hypos, C-peptide ≤ 0.5 ng/mL) vs poorly controlled control group (n = 209).
- Algebraic comparison of dosing models; constants fitted to the well-controlled group ("accurate insulin management", AIM).

## Results (AIM formulas)

- Pump TDD = 0.24 × body weight (lb)
- Basal = 0.47 × TDD
- CF (correction factor) = 1700/TDD mg/dL per U
- CIR (carb ratio) = 2.8 × body weight (lb)/TDD g per U

## Notes for Mellis

- **Derived carb sensitivity (our arithmetic, not stated in the abstract):** CF/CIR = 1700/(2.8 × weight lb) ≈ 607/weight (lb) ≈ 276/weight (kg) mg/dL per gram. Examples: 30 kg ≈ 9 mg/dL per g; 50 kg ≈ 5.5 mg/dL per g; 74 kg ≈ 3.7 mg/dL per g. This matches the idea that one gram raises a small child's glucose much more than an adult's.
- Cross-check: King & Armstrong 2007 found CF ≈ 4.44 × ICR in adults (74 kg) ≈ 4.4 mg/dL per g; ISPAD's 0.3 g/kg → 3–4 mmol/L implies ~10–13 mmol/L per (g/kg), i.e. ~6–8 mg/dL per g for a 30 kg child. The weight formula gives a somewhat higher value for children; use the patient's own ISF/ICR whenever available.
- **Population:** adults (and probably some adolescents) on pumps; not children-specific; pre-AID.
- **Evidence level:** retrospective practice data, model fitting.
