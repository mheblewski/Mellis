# Methods for Insulin Bolus Adjustment Based on the Continuous Glucose Monitoring Trend Arrows in Type 1 Diabetes: Performance and Safety Assessment in an In Silico Clinical Trial

- **Authors:** Noaro G, Cappon G, Sparacino G, Boscari F, Bruttomesso D, Facchinetti A (University of Padova)
- **Journal/year:** Journal of Diabetes Science and Technology 2023;17(1):107–116 (online 2021-09-06)
- **DOI:** 10.1177/19322968211043162
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9846415/ (PMID 34486426)
- **Licence:** © 2021 Diabetes Technology Society (free to read in PMC, not under an open licence) → paraphrased summary with short quotes.
- **Saved:** summary from the PubMed abstract and PMC full text on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus. Exact per-method numeric rules are in the paper's supplement, which was not read.

## Methods

- Seven literature trend-arrow adjustments of the standard formula were compared: Buckingham (BU, % change of the bolus), Scheiner (SC) and Pettus/Edelman (PE) (shift the glucose value used, based on glucose expected 30–60 min ahead; SC smaller than PE), Klonoff/Kerr (KL, fixed units, no patient data), Aleppo/Laffel (AL), Ziegler (ZI) and Bruttomesso (BR) (fixed units scaled by the correction factor).
- UVa/Padova simulator, 100 virtual adults (CF 26–67 mg/dL/U), single meal 10–150 g at 13:00, 6 h analysed; mealtime glucose 80, 120, 160, 200 mg/dL; ROC −2 to +2 mg/dL/min; no sensor noise or counting errors.
- Outcomes: time in range 70–180, below 70 (TBR), above 180 (TAR), BGRI.

## Results

- Falling glucose (negative ROC): every method increased TIR and decreased TBR and TAR vs the standard formula; KL, PE and BR were the most effective, BU and SC the most conservative.
- Example at mealtime glucose 80 mg/dL with falling glucose: standard formula median TBR 18.56% (IQR 3.6–44.04), TIR 55.4%, BGRI 9.62; KL median TBR 5.26%, TIR 60.66%, BGRI 8.94. Other methods also brought TBR to about 5%. (No statistically significant difference vs SF was reported at this glucose level.)
- Rising glucose (positive ROC): methods generally overdosed; only BR improved control when mealtime glucose was near hypoglycaemia, SC was the safest otherwise.
- Authors: "decreasing IB for negative trend arrows is safe"; increases for positive arrows call for safer strategies.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance:** Supports reducing (not skipping) the meal bolus when CGM is falling at mealtime, especially when mealtime glucose is near 80 mg/dL, where the unadjusted formula left ~19% of the next 6 h below 70 mg/dL in simulation; reductions cut that to ~5% at the cost of a modest TAR rise. Increases for rising trends were the risky direction.
- Adults only, single meal, open loop (no AID), idealised simulation; "more qualitatively than quantitatively" until clinical confirmation.
- **Evidence level:** in-silico clinical trial (UVa/Padova simulator).
