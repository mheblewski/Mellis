# Effect of Low Bolus Frequency on Automated Insulin Delivery System Performance in Youth With Type 1 Diabetes

- **Authors:** Chatty P, Gera S, Rearson A, Mountain JN, Alicea-Trelles N, Gallop RJ, Marks BE
- **Journal/year:** Diabetes Care 2026;49(2):303-307
- **DOI:** 10.2337/dc25-2082
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41379540/
- **Licence:** paraphrased (abstract only)
- **Saved:** summary from the PubMed/Europe PMC abstract on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus.

## What it is

Pediatric clinic cohort comparing Control-IQ (has automatic correction boluses) with Omnipod 5 (adjusts basal only, no automatic boluses) in youth who rarely bolus.

## Methods

- Youth averaging <=3.0 boluses/day over 90 days: 75 of 290 Control-IQ users and 127 of 535 Omnipod 5 users; propensity-matched 98 youth; regression on TIR.

## Results relevant to correction boluses

- Matched groups gave the same 2.2 user boluses/day; time in automation 80 % (Control-IQ) vs 64 % (Omnipod 5).
- **Adjusted TIR 54.9 % vs 46.9 % (+8.0 points, P = 0.002)** in favour of Control-IQ, which the authors attribute to more time in automation and AID-initiated (automatic correction) boluses.
- Interpretation: in children who under-bolus, algorithm-delivered corrections add measurable TIR; hypoglycaemia was not reported in the abstract.

## Limitations

- Observational, single centre, abstract-level; time-in-automation difference confounds the system comparison; low TIR overall.
