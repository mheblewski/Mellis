*Our summary (Mellis knowledge base), written in our own words. It is not the original text and not medical advice. Original: Slattery D, Amiel SA, Choudhary P. Optimal prandial timing of bolus insulin in diabetes management: a review. Diabet Med 2018;35(3), https://doi.org/10.1111/dme.13525.*

## Key points

- Rapid-acting analogues taken **15–20 minutes before** food give about 30% lower post-meal glucose and less hypoglycaemia than dosing just before the meal; the authors recommend it whenever safely possible [Abstract; What's new; Conclusions].
- **Post-meal bolusing** works worse and carries more risk of (late) hypoglycaemia [Abstract; Conclusions].
- Insulin pharmacology: insulin levels peak 40–60 min after injection but peak action is around 100–120 min; CGM post-meal peaks occur at a mean 70–80 min; CGM lags blood glucose by 4–10 min [PK/PD].
- Supporting trials started from in-range glucose (Cobry 5.5–10 mmol/L, Luijf 3.5–7.8 mmol/L), so the advice applies when pre-meal glucose is in range [Clinical studies; Conclusions].
- A parent unsure of a child's appetite may give a fast analogue within 15 minutes after the child starts eating as a compromise, but this should not become a habit; adults should be discouraged from post-meal dosing [Special circumstances].

## Clinical studies of bolus timing

[Clinical studies; Table]
- Cobry 2010: 23 young people on pumps (mean 18.3 years), glulisine at −20, 0, +20 min. −20 min had the lowest values at 60 min (10.0 vs 12.3 vs 13.1 mmol/L) and 120 min, and lowest peaks; hypos 4 / 1 / 5 (−20 / 0 / +20).
- Luijf 2010: 10 adults on pumps, aspart at −30, −15, 0; −15 best (peak 9.26 vs 11.74 vs 12.29 mmol/L), no difference in lows.
- Brunner: aspart at 0 beat +15 min (peak 11.2 vs 13.2 mmol/L); late lows in 21% of experiments.
- Schernthaner: lispro −20 min best at 60 min; at 90–120 min −20 and 0 similar; no lows at 0 min, three late lows with +15 min.
- Jovanovic and another Schernthaner study also favoured dosing at meal start over +30 min.
- Two hours after a meal glucose is often falling, so post-meal decisions should consider insulin on board [Clinical studies].

## Meal composition, fat and protein, gastric emptying

[Other factors] Adding 35 g fat raised glucose by 2.3 mmol/L at 5 h; 50 g fat caused 5 h of hyperglycaemia; 35 g protein added to a 30 g carb meal raised glucose 2.6 mmol/L at 5 h. Eating protein and fat 15 minutes before carbs lowered post-meal glucose (by 29–37% at 30–60 min; incremental AUC −73%). The authors speculate that pre-bolusing matters most for high-GI food and that **split doses** may suit high-fat/protein meals; one study found an 8-hour dual-wave bolus best after a high-fat meal. High glucose slows gastric emptying; gastroparesis may need dual- or square-wave boluses.

## Special circumstances

[Special circumstances] When eating 15–20 min after a bolus cannot be guaranteed (critical jobs, social meals, unknown carbs), dosing before the meal is still best, but occasional post-meal doses are safer with rapid analogues. Pregnancy and emergency work also need tailored advice [What's new]. Faster analogues would need peak action under 45 min to work well after eating [Conclusions].

## Limits of this summary

- Mostly adults (some adolescents/young adults); MDI and pump; no AID data.
- Narrative review of small crossover and PK/PD studies; industry-funded editorial support.
- Excerpt only; no data on low or falling pre-meal glucose.
- Read the original for clinical decisions.
