# Managing High Glucose Levels and Ketones with Hybrid Closed Loop (HCL) Systems - UCLH children and young people's diabetes service (change the set/Pod if glucose stays > 14 mmol/L for more than 90 minutes; ketones > 1.5 mmol/L mean manual mode and pen corrections)

## Rules (children and young people using MiniMed 780G, Control-IQ, CamAPS FX on Ypsopump, Omnipod 5 in automated mode)

- Target range 3.6-9 mmol/L, at least 70 % of the time in range and less than 4 % below 3.6 mmol/L.
- **When glucose is high:** the HCL system raises basal insulin by itself, and some systems also give automatic correction boluses; check the lowest glucose target is selected; change the set/Pod at least every 3 days.
- **Set/Pod failure is the main cause of DKA on HCL** (set/Pod not working or insulin not absorbing): if glucose stays above 14 mmol/L for more than 90 minutes, change the set/Pod right away. If glucose and ketones improve after the first change, a second change is not needed.
- **High glucose with ketones** means too little insulin: if ketones are above 1.5 mmol/L, switch to manual mode with a temporary basal increase and give correction boluses with an insulin pen (rapid-acting insulin only).
- Drink water or sugar-free fluids; dehydration and some medicines (e.g. paracetamol) can make CGM less accurate, so confirm with a fingerstick when unwell.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance (high, paediatric):** a children's service rule that on HCL a high above 14 mmol/L (about 250 mg/dL) lasting more than 90 minutes should trigger a set/Pod change rather than further corrections, and that ketones above 1.5 mmol/L move the child to manual mode with pen corrections. It does not tell families to add manual corrections on top of the loop in other situations.
- **Population:** children and young people with type 1 diabetes and their families.
- **Evidence level:** [Ed] hospital paediatric patient information.
