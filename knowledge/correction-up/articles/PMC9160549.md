A clinician practice guide written before FDA clearance of Omnipod 5; funded by Insulet, with three Insulet employees among the authors. Not a trial or a consensus statement.

## Key points

- In automated mode the algorithm's own extra insulin counts as insulin on board (IOB) and is subtracted from user boluses, so calculator corrections are often smaller than users expect [Table 2, DIA row; Vignette 2].
- Overriding the calculator with pre-AID-sized corrections caused hypoglycaemia in an 8-year-old; the advice was to bolus 15–20 min before breakfast and follow the calculator ("trust the system") [Vignette 2].
- Glucose > 250 mg/dL (13.9 mmol/L) for more than 90–120 min after a user correction: suspect the infusion site, check ketones, replace the Pod [Troubleshooting Hyperglycemia].
- With elevated ketones, an injection of rapid-acting insulin by pen/syringe may be needed before the Pod change, with glucose and ketones checked every 1–2 h until ketones normalise; the algorithm cannot see injected insulin, so manual mode for 2–4 h may be considered (not always needed) [Troubleshooting Hyperglycemia].
- AID will not remove all highs and lows; the real goal is more time in range (TIR) with an individual TIR target [Ensuring Realistic Expectations].

## Bolus calculators and correction factor across AID systems

- All commercial AID systems have a bolus calculator using ICR and correction factor (ICF, also called ISF) [System Features].
- The Omnipod 5 calculator also uses the CGM trend: suggested doses go up when glucose is rising and down when falling.
- MiniMed AID models use the programmed ICF only in manual mode; in auto mode the system sets its own.
- Omnipod 5 and Control-IQ use the programmed ICF for user corrections in both automated and manual modes.

## Omnipod 5 settings and what they mean in automated mode (Table 2)

| Setting | Range | Effect in automated mode |
|---|---|---|
| Target glucose | 110–150 mg/dL in 10 mg/dL steps, up to 8 segments/day | Main driver of automation: insulin rises if glucose is forecast above target and falls or stops if forecast below; a downward trend can cut insulin even above target, an upward trend can raise it even below target. Also the target for calculator corrections. |
| Duration of insulin action (DIA) | 2–6 h in 30-min steps | Used only for IOB from user boluses; the algorithm tracks its own IOB separately. Both are subtracted from user boluses, so corrections may be smaller. |
| Correction factor | 1–400 mg/dL per unit, up to 8 segments | No effect on automation; only user corrections. May need changing to make user corrections work in automated mode. |
| Insulin-to-carb ratio | 1–150 g/unit in 0.1 g steps, up to 8 segments | Used for meal boluses. Because insulin is often reduced before meals, there may be little pre-meal IOB, so a stronger ICR than on MDI or a standard pump may help. |
| Correct above | from target up to 200 mg/dL | Threshold for calculator corrections; no effect on automation. |
| Reverse correction | on/off | Reduces the meal bolus when glucose is below target. In automated mode, turning it off may help people often below target at meals who then go high afterwards, so they get the full carb dose. |
| Maximum bolus | 0.05–30 units | Limits user boluses only. |

## Pre-bolus

Insulin is often suspended overnight when glucose drifts down or below target, leaving little IOB at breakfast, so bolus timing matters. In the vignette the clinician moved the bolus from "starting to eat" to 15–20 min before breakfast [Vignette 2].

## Onboarding and expectations

- Before starting, review basic self-management, pump and CGM skills regardless of experience; teach Pod site care and a plan for persistent hyperglycaemia (ketones, Pod change); review carb counting and food labels, since meal boluses are not automated [Educating Users].
- Change the Pod every 2–3 days and rotate sites (arms, buttocks, abdomen, legs) to avoid lipohypertrophy; avoid scars, irritation and skin folds such as the waistline [Troubleshooting Hyperglycemia].
- Site failure (kinked, blocked or dislodged cannula, local inflammation) shows as high glucose despite more insulin and raises DKA risk.
- AID is not "set and forget"; users still bolus for meals, respond to alarms and handle signal loss. Some must learn to let go of control and trust the tested algorithm, which accounts for glucose, trend and IOB [Ensuring Realistic Expectations; Revisiting Expectations].

## Staying in automated mode

- In the pivotal trial, children spent a median 96.4% and adults 96.7% of time in automated mode [Troubleshooting Forced Exits].
- The system returns to manual mode after too long at maximum delivery or in suspension. For a maximum-delivery exit: check Pod adhesion, leaks and ketones; if nothing is found, give a correction bolus and restart automated mode. For a prolonged-suspension exit: check glucose by meter; resume if CGM is accurate, otherwise calibrate first.
- Keep Pod and sensor on the same side of the body (line of sight) to avoid "Automated Mode: Limited" from poor communication.
