# Amount and type of dietary fat, postprandial glycemia, and insulin requirements in type 1 diabetes: a randomized within-subject trial

- **Authors:** Bell KJ, Fio CZ, Twigg S, Duke SA, Fulcher G, Alexander K, McGill M, Wong J, Brand-Miller J, Steil GM
- **Journal/year:** Diabetes Care 2020;43(1):59-66
- **DOI:** 10.2337/dc19-0687
- **PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31455688/
- **Licence:** not open access (or open-access status not confirmed). This file contains a paraphrased summary of the abstract and key numbers written for the Mellis corpus, not the publisher's text.

## Summary of the abstract (paraphrased)

- **Population:** adults with T1D on insulin pumps; 9-12 clinic visits each.
- **Phase 1:** meals with 45 g carbohydrate and 0, 20, 40 or 60 g fat (saturated, monounsaturated or polyunsaturated). Insulin by the person's ICR as a **dual-wave 50/50% over 2 h**.
- **Phase 2:** 20-60 g fat meals repeated with a model-predictive bolus (up to two per meal) until glycaemic control was achieved.
- **Dose-response with the same insulin:** more fat gave a dose-dependent **lower** glucose iAUC in the **early period (0-2 h; P = 0.008)** and **higher** iAUC in the **late period (2-5 h; P = 0.004)**. The type of fat made no significant difference to 5-h iAUC.
- **Insulin needed on average (dual-wave):**

| Fat in meal (with 45 g carbohydrate) | Extra insulin vs ICR dose | Upfront / extended split | Extension duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 g | +6% | 74 / 26% | 73 min |
| 40 g | +6% | 63 / 37% | 75 min |
| 60 g | +21% | 49 / 51% | 105 min |

## Notes for Mellis

- Key practical message: below about 40 g fat the extra total insulin is small (+6%) and the main change is *shifting* part of the dose later; at 60 g fat the extra insulin rises to about +21% and half the dose is delayed. Durations here (73-105 min) are much shorter than the 3-8 h FPU extensions of the Warsaw method.
- Adults, open-loop pump, carbohydrate fixed at 45 g.
