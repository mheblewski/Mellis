*Our summary (Mellis knowledge base), written in our own words. It is not the original text and not medical advice. Original: Luijf YM, van Bon AC, Hoekstra JB, DeVries JH. Premeal Injection of Rapid-Acting Insulin Reduces Postprandial Glycemic Excursions in Type 1 Diabetes. Diabetes Care 2010;33(10):2152–2155, https://doi.org/10.2337/dc10-0692.*

## Key points

- Randomised three-way crossover, 10 adults on pumps (insulin aspart), usual breakfast (mean 48 g carbs, mean bolus 6.0 U) [Research design; Results].
- A bolus **15 minutes before** eating beat both 30 minutes before and at meal start; −30 and 0 did not differ [Results].
- Compared with 0 min, −15 min gave 80.6% lower glucose AUC, 31.2% smaller maximum rise, 24.7% lower peak and 148.1% more time at 3.5–10 mmol/L (63–180 mg/dL) [Results].
- Lows (<3.5 mmol/L, 63 mg/dL) did not differ between arms (P = 0.901); none occurred before the meal and no rescue carbs were needed [Results].
- With the −15 bolus glucose fell a little before the meal, so the authors suggest pre-bolusing this way only when pre-meal glucose is **above 5.0 mmol/L (90 mg/dL)** [Conclusions].

## Why timing matters (rapid-acting insulin pharmacology)

[Introduction] Post-meal glucose peaks at about 1–2 h (mean ~75 min), while rapid analogues act most strongly about 100 min after injection, so an earlier bolus lines the two up better.

## Pre-meal glucose conditions

[Research design] Visits started only with glucose 3.5–7.8 mmol/L (63–140 mg/dL) and stable; higher values were first corrected IV; a low start cancelled the visit. So there are **no data** for low or falling glucose at mealtime.

## Limits of this summary

- Ten adults on pumps in a clinic; not children, not AID.
- Small trial; the authors call for larger real-life studies before guidelines adopt it.
- Read the original for clinical use.
