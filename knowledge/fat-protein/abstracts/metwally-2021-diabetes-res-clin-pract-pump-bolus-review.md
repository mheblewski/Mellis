# Insulin pump dosing strategies for meals varying in fat, protein or glycaemic index or grazing-style meals in type 1 diabetes: a systematic review

- **Authors:** Metwally M, Cheung TO, Smith R, Bell KJ
- **Journal/year:** Diabetes Research and Clinical Practice 2021;172:108516
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.diabres.2020.108516
- **PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33096184/
- **Licence:** not open access (or open-access status not confirmed). This file contains a paraphrased summary of the abstract and key numbers written for the Mellis corpus, not the publisher's text.

## Summary of the abstract (paraphrased)

- **Design:** systematic review (March 1995-April 2020) of pump studies in adults and/or children comparing bolus types for high-fat, high-protein, GI-varied and prolonged (grazing) meals.
- **High-fat meals:** all 11 publications (234 participants) found a **dual-wave bolus superior** to a standard bolus.
- **High-protein meals:** all 7 studies (129 participants) found a dual-wave bolus superior.
- **Extra insulin** further improved postprandial glucose but **increased hypoglycaemia risk in 5 of 14 studies**.
- **GI:** one study; dual-wave reduced glucose and hypoglycaemia risk for low-GI meals.
- **Grazing meals:** no studies.
- Meta-analysis not possible (heterogeneity). Optimal split, duration and total dose still unknown.

## Notes for Mellis

- Open-loop pump evidence only. Supports splitting/extending as the default shape for fatty meals, and flags that adding insulin carries hypoglycaemia risk in about a third of studies.
