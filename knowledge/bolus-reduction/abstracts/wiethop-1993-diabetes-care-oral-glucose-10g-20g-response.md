# Alanine and terbutaline in treatment of hypoglycemia in IDDM

- **Authors:** Wiethop BV, Cryer PE
- **Journal/year:** Diabetes Care 1993;16(8):1131–1136
- **DOI:** 10.2337/diacare.16.8.1131
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8375243/
- **Licence:** paraphrased
- **Saved:** summary from the PubMed/Europe PMC abstract on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus.

## Design

- Model of clinical hypoglycaemia: subcutaneous insulin 0.15 U/kg in adults with type 1 diabetes; treatments given during hypoglycaemia about 2 h after the insulin injection.
- Arms included placebo, oral glucose 10 g and 20 g, glucagon 1 mg s.c., oral alanine 40 g, oral and s.c. terbutaline.

## Key numbers (oral glucose)

| Treatment | Start glucose | Peak glucose | Time to peak | Approx. rise |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Glucose 10 g | 3.0–3.3 mmol/L | 5.4 ± 0.4 mmol/L | within 30 min | ~2.1–2.4 mmol/L (~40 mg/dL) |
| Glucose 20 g | 3.0–3.3 mmol/L | 6.8 ± 0.7 mmol/L | within 45 min | ~3.5–3.8 mmol/L (~65 mg/dL) |
| Glucagon 1 mg | 3.0–3.3 mmol/L | 11.8 ± 0.8 mmol/L | within 60 min | — |

- The responses to glucose and glucagon were transient (glucose fell again afterwards while insulin was still acting). The "approx. rise" column is simple subtraction from the abstract values.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance:** This is the source of the often-quoted "10 g raises ~40 mg/dL in 30 min, 20 g raises ~60–65 mg/dL in 45 min, then the effect wanes" (often misattributed to Brodows 1984). ISPAD 2022 cites it (with Brodows and Georgakopoulos) for "20 g glucose raised glucose by ~2.5–3.6 mmol/L (45–65 mg/dL) at 45 min" in adults, which was then extrapolated to 0.3 g/kg in children.
- **Per-gram estimate in adults:** about 0.18–0.24 mmol/L (3.3–4.3 mg/dL) per gram of glucose at the peak, with ongoing insulin action. Body weight not given in the abstract.
- **Implication for meals eaten while low:** the rise from pure glucose is short-lived when insulin is still active, so after a hypo a meal (or slower carbohydrate) is still needed; a bolus for that meal must not assume the treatment glucose will keep glucose up.
- **Population:** small number of adults with T1D (n not stated in abstract), clinical research setting, pre-analogue era.
- **Evidence level:** small physiological crossover study.
