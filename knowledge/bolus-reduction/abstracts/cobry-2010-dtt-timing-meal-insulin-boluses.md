# Timing of meal insulin boluses to achieve optimal postprandial glycemic control in patients with type 1 diabetes

- **Authors:** Cobry E, McFann K, Messer L, Gage V, VanderWel B, Horton L, Chase HP
- **Journal/year:** Diabetes Technology & Therapeutics 2010;12(3):173–177
- **DOI:** 10.1089/dia.2009.0112
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20151766/
- **Licence:** paraphrased
- **Saved:** summary from the PubMed/Europe PMC abstract, the Slattery 2018 review and the Mozzillo 2022 review table on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus.

## Design

- Crossover study in Colorado, 23 adolescents and young adults (12–30 y, mean 18.3; 11 pediatric) on pumps, insulin glulisine.
- Bolus 20 min before the meal (PRE), immediately before (START) or 20 min after the meal started (POST). Same breakfast each visit (fixed carbohydrate, < 20 g fat).
- Pre-meal glucose had to be in range before testing (100–180 mg/dL per Mozzillo; 5.5–10 mmol/L per Slattery).

## Results

- 60 and 120 min glucose lower with PRE than START (p = 0.0029; 0.0294) and POST (p = 0.001; 0.0408). START and POST did not differ.
- Glucose AUC: PRE 159.5 vs START 187.0 and POST 184.5 mg/dL (both p < 0.05). Peak glucose lowest with PRE.
- Hypoglycaemic episodes (per Slattery): POST 5, START 1, PRE 4; not significantly different (per Mozzillo).

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance:** A post-meal bolus (+20 min) gave about the same post-meal glucose as a bolus at meal start in this group, and the most hypo episodes, consistent with insulin peaking after the meal carbs. For a meal eaten while low, dosing at or shortly after the start (once glucose is recovering) costs little compared with START, while pre-bolusing is not advised.
- **Population:** adolescents/young adults on pumps, pre-AID.
- **Evidence level:** small randomized crossover.
