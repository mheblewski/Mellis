# Predictors of Time in Target Glucose Range in Real-World Users of the MiniMed 780G System

- **Authors:** Castañeda J, Mathieu C, Aanstoot HJ, Arrieta A, Da Silva J, Shin J, Cohen O
- **Journal/year:** Diabetes, Obesity and Metabolism 2022;24(11):2212-2221
- **DOI:** 10.1111/dom.14807
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35791621/
- **Licence:** paraphrased (abstract only; article CC BY-NC per Europe PMC metadata but full text not retrieved)
- **Saved:** summary from the PubMed/Europe PMC abstract on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus.

## What it is

Real-world analysis of 12,870 MiniMed 780G users looking for predictors of time in range after starting advanced hybrid closed loop.

## Methods

- CareLink uploads Aug 2020-Jul 2021; univariate and multivariable models; 2977 had baseline sensor data.

## Results relevant to correction boluses

- A **higher daily auto-correction dose predicted lower TIR** (as did higher basal share, more system-triggered exits and more alarms) - large auto-correction totals mark people whose meal insulin or settings are insufficient, rather than a benefit of more correction.
- Settings with the highest TIR: **active insulin time 2 h and target 100 mg/dL**. A shorter active insulin time (which lets the algorithm count less insulin as still active and so give more correction) **was not associated with more time below 70 mg/dL**.

## Limitations

- Associations only; manufacturer-authored; abstract-level detail; 780G-specific settings.
