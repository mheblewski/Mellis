# Impact of macronutrient content of meals on postprandial glucose control in the context of closed-loop insulin delivery: a randomized cross-over study

- **Authors:** Gingras V, Bonato L, Messier V, Roy-Fleming A, Smaoui MR, Ladouceur M, Rabasa-Lhoret R
- **Journal/year:** Diabetes, Obesity and Metabolism 2018;20(11):2695-2699
- **DOI:** 10.1111/dom.13445
- **PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29931719/
- **Licence:** not open access (or open-access status not confirmed). This file contains a paraphrased summary of the abstract and key numbers written for the Mellis corpus, not the publisher's text.

## Summary of the abstract (paraphrased)

- **Population:** 15 adults with T1D on an experimental closed-loop system (Montreal).
- **Design:** randomised four-way crossover breakfasts with fixed carbohydrate (75 ± 1 g) and added protein and/or fat (35 ± 2 g): carbohydrate only, high protein, high fat, high fat + protein. The closed-loop algorithm generated bolus and infusion rates.
- **Main outcome:** adding fat, protein or both did **not** change 5-h glucose AUC, mean glucose or peak (P > 0.05).
- **But:** with the high-fat + high-protein meal the glucose **peak was 40 min later** (P = 0.03) and **5-h post-meal basal insulin need was 39% higher** (P = 0.04).

## Notes for Mellis

- Early closed-loop evidence that the loop can absorb much of a moderate fat/protein load by itself (raising basal ~39%), at the cost of a later peak. Adults, research algorithm, 35 g fat/protein only.
