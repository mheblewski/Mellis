# Timing of bolus in children with type 1 diabetes using continuous subcutaneous insulin infusion (TiBoDi Study)

- **Authors:** Scaramuzza AE, Iafusco D, Santoro L, Bosetti A, De Palma A, Spiri D, Mameli C, Zuccotti GV
- **Journal/year:** Diabetes Technology & Therapeutics 2010
- **DOI:** 10.1089/dia.2009.0117
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20105045/
- **Licence:** paraphrased
- **Saved:** summary from the PubMed/Europe PMC abstract, the Mozzillo 2022 review table, and the authors' 2011 letter in Diabetes Care (PMC3024389, 10.2337/dc10-1965) on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus.

## Design

- 30 children/young people with T1D on pumps (aspart), age 6–20 y, Italy; standard lunch (55% carbohydrate) on 3 hospital days.
- Bolus 15 min before, immediately before, or immediately after the meal (randomized). Pre-prandial glucose had to be 80–140 mg/dL (per Mozzillo 2022 table).

## Results

- 1 h after the meal: 177 ± 71 mg/dL with bolus after the meal vs 136 ± 52 (15 min before; p = 0.044) and 130 ± 54 (just before; p = 0.024). Differences at other time points and in AUC were not significant; variability of AUC was lowest with the −15 min bolus.
- Mild hypoglycaemia: 12 patients had 1 episode each (per Mozzillo table).

## Authors' conclusion and a caution

- The authors concluded the bolus should be given before rather than after eating, "even if the patient is hypoglycemic before meals".
- **Caution (our reading):** the protocol only enrolled meals with pre-meal glucose 80–140 mg/dL, so the trial did not test children who were hypoglycaemic before the meal; this part of the conclusion is not supported by the study design.

## 2011 letter (same group, unpublished TiBoDi data, 69 young people 4–21 y)

- With pre-meal glucose > 10 mmol/L, a bolus 15 min before the meal did not prevent post-meal hyperglycaemia; giving the correction 45 min and the meal bolus 15 min before eating worked best (2-h glucose 7.9 vs 10.3–10.8 mmol/L).
- The letter frames the 15-min pre-bolus as appropriate when pre-meal glucose is in the normal range.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance:** Shows pre-bolus benefit is modest in children (mainly the 1-h value). It is the only pediatric source found that argues for pre-bolusing even when low, but without data from low starting glucose — contradicts NHS/US education material (treat first, then dose; or dose after eating).
- **Population:** children/adolescents on pumps, pre-AID.
- **Evidence level:** small randomized within-patient study.
