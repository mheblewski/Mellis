# Effectiveness of postprandial Humalog in toddlers with diabetes

- **Authors:** Rutledge KS, Chase HP, Klingensmith GJ, Walravens PA, Slover RH, Garg SK
- **Journal/year:** Pediatrics 1997;100(6):968
- **DOI:** 10.1542/peds.100.6.968
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9374566/
- **Licence:** paraphrased
- **Saved:** summary from the PubMed/Europe PMC abstract on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus.

## Design

- Authors from the Colorado (Barbara Davis Center) pediatric group. Children under 5 years with T1D.
- Group 1 (n = 5): regular human insulin before the meal vs the same dose of lispro (Humalog) after a meal of equal carbohydrate.
- Group 2 (n = 5): lispro before vs lispro after the meal. Long-acting insulin unchanged. Glucose at fasting and 1, 2 and 4 h after the meal.

## Results

- 2-h glucose excursions were significantly smaller with post-meal lispro than with pre-meal regular insulin.
- Lispro before vs after the meal gave similar excursions.

## Authors' conclusion (paraphrased)

Giving lispro after the meal works in toddlers and lets the dose match what the child actually ate, improving safety and flexibility.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance:** Origin of the pediatric practice "dose after the meal for unpredictable young eaters", which also applies when glucose is low or falling before a meal: dose for what was eaten, after eating.
- **Population:** toddlers < 5 y, injections, 1990s insulins; very small (n = 10).
- **Evidence level:** very small crossover-style clinical study.
