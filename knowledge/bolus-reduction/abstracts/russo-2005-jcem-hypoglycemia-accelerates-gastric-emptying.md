# Insulin-induced hypoglycemia accelerates gastric emptying of solids and liquids in long-standing type 1 diabetes

- **Authors:** Russo A, Stevens JE, Chen R, Gentilcore D, Burnet R, Horowitz M, Jones KL
- **Journal/year:** Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism 2005
- **DOI:** 10.1210/jc.2005-0513
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15899955/
- **Licence:** paraphrased
- **Saved:** summary from the PubMed/Europe PMC abstract (plus the abstract of Schvarcz et al., Diabetes Care 1995, 10.2337/diacare.18.5.674) on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus.

## Design

- 20 adults with long-standing T1D (mean age 45.9 y, diabetes 18 y; 12 with autonomic neuropathy), Royal Adelaide Hospital.
- Gastric emptying of a solid/liquid meal (100 g minced beef + 150 mL water, radiolabelled) measured by scintigraphy on two days: during clamped hypoglycaemia (~2.6 mmol/L, from 15 min before to 45 min after the meal) and during euglycaemia.

## Results

- Both solids (p < 0.001) and liquids (p < 0.05) emptied faster during hypoglycaemia.
- The acceleration was larger in people whose emptying was slow at euglycaemia, and was not changed by autonomic neuropathy.
- Authors' interpretation: faster gastric emptying is probably part of the body's counter-regulation of hypoglycaemia.

## Related study: Schvarcz et al. 1995 (healthy adults)

- 8 healthy volunteers, clamp: time to empty 50% of a meal fell from 43.0 to 16.3 min (solids) and from 38.0 to 15.4 min (liquids) during insulin-induced hypoglycaemia (p < 0.001).
- Slattery 2018 also notes the opposite: hyperglycaemia slows gastric emptying.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance:** Mechanistic support that carbohydrate in a meal eaten while LOW is delivered to the gut faster than the same meal at normal glucose, so part of the meal does act on the low. This argues against assuming a meal is useless as hypo treatment, but it does not quantify how many grams act as fast treatment, and fat/protein still slow absorption (Brodows: milk 20 g → only ~1 mmol/L).
- **Not studied:** children; mixed carbohydrate meals eaten during hypoglycaemia with measured glucose response; AID.
- **Evidence level:** small physiological clamp studies (n = 20 and n = 8), adults.
