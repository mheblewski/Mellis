*Our summary (Mellis knowledge base), written in our own words. It is not the original text and not medical advice. Original: Szadkowska A, Chobot A, Głowińska-Olszewska B, Jarosz-Chobot P, Mianowska B, Myśliwiec M, Szypowska A, Zubkiewicz-Kucharska A, Walczak M. Guidelines of the Polish Society of Pediatric Endocrinology and Diabetology and Pediatric Section of Diabetes Poland on insulin therapy using hybrid closed-loop systems in children and adolescents. Pediatr Endocrinol Diabetes Metab 2024;30(3):132–147, https://doi.org/10.5114/pedm.2024.144041.*

National guideline of two Polish societies for hybrid closed-loop (HCL) therapy in children, written for the systems available in Poland in 2024 (MiniMed 780G with SmartGuard, CamAPS FX). This summary covers settings, pausing HCL, bolus timing, missed boluses, corrections and hyperglycaemia/ketones.

## Key points

- Give meal boluses about 15 min before eating; earlier (or a larger bolus) may help with high-glycaemic-index food [Section 4.3.1].
- If glucose is low before a meal, treat the hypoglycaemia first, then bolus and eat once glucose is stable [Section 4.3.1].
- Missed or late meal bolus: within 30–60 min of starting the meal, enter half of the carbohydrates and give the suggested bolus; after more than 60 min skip the meal bolus (the system is already correcting) or give a calculator correction with current glucose and 0 g carbs, and nothing if glucose is in range [Section 4.3.2].
- Corrections go through the system's own calculator (glucose + 0 g carbs). "Fake carbs" (carbs not eaten) are discouraged [Section 4.4].
- Persistent CGM glucose > 250 mg/dL (13.9 mmol/L): confirm with a meter; change the set if it lasts > 2 h without a cause; give one user correction in automatic mode; if no response in 90 min, give the next correction by pen/syringe and switch automatic mode off for 2–4 h [Section 7].
- Glucose > 250–300 mg/dL (13.9–16.7 mmol/L), usually for > 4 h, with blood β-hydroxybutyrate (BOHB) > 3 mmol/L or urine ketones ≥ +++ suggests ketoacidosis and needs immediate medical care [Section 7].

## Settings: bolus calculator and automatic mode (Tables II and IV)

Bolus calculator parameters [Table IV]:
- Insulin-to-carbohydrate ratio (ICR): grams of carbohydrate covered by 1 unit.
- Active insulin time.
- Insulin sensitivity factor (ISF): glucose fall per 1 unit, usually from the 1800 rule.
- Target glucose for corrections.

Starting settings for automatic mode [Table II] (default marked):
- MiniMed 780G SmartGuard: target 100 mg/dL (default), 110 or 120 mg/dL; active insulin time 2 h (default), range 2–8 h. Adjusting active insulin time is usually advised before starting automatic mode.
- CamAPS FX: target 104 mg/dL (default), range 80–198 mg/dL; active insulin time cannot be changed. Start-up needs body weight and daily insulin dose; the algorithm uses the entered ICRs; extra manual boluses can use the entered correction factor and target. Two identical basal programmes must be stored in the pump for use if automatic mode stops. Access requires the developer's training, confirmed by a physician [Section 1.2].

## When to pause HCL (Section 2.4)

- Problems with sensors or sets (e.g. severe skin reactions) that stop pump or CGM use.
- Malfunction of a component (pump, CGM, phone for CamAPS FX).
- Full remission needing no insulin, or partial remission needing basal only.
- On request (holidays, sports competitions).
- Intercurrent illness with fast-changing insulin needs that automatic mode follows too slowly.

## Meal bolus timing and rules (Section 4.3.1; Table V)

- Pre-bolus about 15 min, not during or after the meal. A late bolus leads to an early spike; the system then raises its automatic insulin, and the sum of the late bolus, increased basal and auto-corrections can cause hypoglycaemia. Late boluses may also lead the system to lower meal doses on following days.
- Bolus for every meal containing carbohydrate. Snacks up to 20 g without a bolus did not cause large spikes in studies, but still add variability and are best avoided.
- If carb counting is hard, fixed carbohydrate amounts for small, standard and large meals (by age and weight) can be used.
- If the calculator suggests 0 units, confirm it anyway so the meal is recorded and the algorithm works correctly.

## Correction doses (Section 4.4)

- MiniMed 780G gives automatic correction boluses; a user correction can be added by entering a meter glucose value.
- CamAPS FX also corrects automatically; entering glucose with 0 g carbs in its calculator gives an extra correction.
- Entering fictional carbohydrates to force a correction may impair the system's response, lower its performance and increase variability.

## Hyperglycaemia and ketones (Section 7)

For persistent hyperglycaemia above 250 mg/dL on CGM:
1. Confirm with a glucose meter.
2. Change the infusion set if it lasts over 2 h with no known cause.
3. Give a user correction bolus in automatic mode.
4. No response within 90 min: give the next correction by pen or syringe. Rationale: after an occlusion and several pump corrections, the system's active insulin may be falsely high and block further boluses.
5. After a pen correction, turn automatic mode off for 2–4 h so the system does not add corrections while pen insulin is acting.
6. Hyperglycaemia lasting > 4 h: measure blood or urine ketones.
7. Glucose above 350–400 mg/dL (19.4–22.2 mmol/L): give the first correction by pen/syringe and replace both the set and the insulin at once.
8. Glucose > 250–300 mg/dL usually for > 4 h with BOHB > 3 mmol/L (or urine ketones ≥ +++): dangerous, likely DKA; immediate medical attention.

## Sick days with hyperglycaemia (Section 9.1)

With glucose > 250 mg/dL for more than 4 h, vomiting, or ketonaemia: switch to manual mode and follow the hyperglycaemia steps above and the Diabetes Poland (PTD) recommendations.

## Limits of this summary

- Children and adolescents with type 1 diabetes in Poland; only MiniMed 780G and CamAPS FX are covered. Open-source systems such as Trio or Loop are not addressed.
- National expert guideline (consensus of two societies); evidence grades are not reproduced here.
- Only selected sections are covered (settings, pausing HCL, bolus timing, missed bolus, corrections, hyperglycaemia, illness); patient selection, exercise, other sections are left out.
- mmol/L conversions are ours (the guideline uses mg/dl). Read the original before clinical decisions.
