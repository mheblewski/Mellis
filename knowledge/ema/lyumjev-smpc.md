*Our summary (Mellis knowledge base), written in our own words. It is not the original text and not medical advice. Original: Lyumjev 100 units/mL and 200 units/mL (insulin lispro), Summary of Product Characteristics, Annex I of the EPAR product information, European Medicines Agency / Eli Lilly Nederland B.V. (first authorised 24 March 2020, renewed 13 January 2025; version retrieved 2026-10-16), https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/lyumjev.*

## Key points

- Lyumjev is insulin lispro with two added substances that speed up absorption: citrate (raises local vascular permeability) and treprostinil (widens local blood vessels) [Section 5.1].
- Onset of action: 20 minutes after the dose, 11 minutes earlier than Humalog (standard insulin lispro), in a clamp study with a single 15-unit dose [Section 5.1].
- In the first 30 minutes the glucose-lowering effect was three times that of Humalog [Section 5.1].
- Peak (maximum glucose-lowering effect): between 1 and 3 hours after injection [Section 5.1].
- Duration of action: 5 hours, 44 minutes shorter than Humalog; late action (from 4 hours on) 54% lower than Humalog. Total effect is the same unit for unit [Section 5.1].
- Timing: inject 0–2 minutes before the start of a meal, with the option of up to 20 minutes after starting to eat [Section 4.2].
- Hypoglycaemia may come earlier after a dose than with other mealtime insulins [Section 4.4, 4.8].
- Approved from age 1 year (100 units/mL); no clinical experience under 3 years. The 200 units/mL pen is for adults only and must not be used in a pump [Section 4.1, 4.2].
- Pump use: the 100 units/mL vial may stay in a pump for at most 9 days [Section 6.6].
- Infusion site reactions were common in pump users (38% in PRONTO-Pump-2, mostly mild) [Section 4.8].

## Pharmacodynamics: onset, peak, duration [Section 5.1]

- Clamp study, 40 people with type 1 diabetes, single 15-unit dose: onset 20 min; first-30-minute effect 3 times Humalog; maximum effect 1–3 h; late effect after 4 h 54% lower; duration 5 h; total glucose infused similar [Section 5.1, Figure 1].
- A faster early and smaller late effect was also seen in type 2 diabetes [Section 5.1].
- Early onset and total action were similar whether injected in the abdomen, upper arm or thigh [Section 5.1].
- Standardised test meal (0–5 h): in type 1 diabetes, post-meal glucose was 32% lower than with Humalog when Lyumjev was given at meal start, and 18% lower when given 20 minutes after the start [Section 5.1].
- 200 units/mL and 100 units/mL have comparable effect; no dose conversion when switching strength [Section 5.1, 4.2].

## Pharmacokinetics [Section 5.2]

- Type 1 diabetes, subcutaneous: insulin lispro appears in the blood about 1 minute after injection (5 minutes faster than Humalog); time to half-maximum concentration 14 minutes shorter; seven times more insulin in the first 15 minutes and three times more in the first 30 minutes than Humalog [Section 5.2].
- Time to maximum concentration: 57 minutes. At 3 hours there is 41% less insulin in the blood than with Humalog; overall exposure lasts 60 minutes less. Total exposure and Cmax similar to Humalog [Section 5.2].
- Day-to-day variability: 13% for total exposure, 23% for Cmax [Section 5.2].
- Bioavailability about 65% in abdomen, upper arm and thigh. Fast absorption is kept at all three sites; in the thigh the peak is later and lower. No data for the buttocks [Section 5.2].
- Exposure rises in proportion to the dose from 7 to 30 units [Section 5.2].
- Pump (CSII): time to half-maximum concentration 14 minutes (9 minutes shorter than Humalog); 1.5 times more insulin in the first 30 minutes [Section 5.2].
- Intravenous half-life in healthy people: median 44 minutes [Section 5.2].
- Children 8–11 and adolescents 12–17 years (0.2 units/kg): differences from Humalog similar to adults; in the pump there was only a trend to faster absorption [Section 5.2].
- Age, sex and race did not change the pharmacokinetics; renal and hepatic impairment are not known to affect them [Section 5.2].

## Timing of the mealtime dose (bolus timing) [Section 4.2, 5.1]

- Standard: 0–2 minutes before the meal; option of dosing up to 20 minutes after the start of the meal [Section 4.2].
- PRONTO-T1D (adults, multiple daily injections, 26 weeks, n = 1,222): mealtime Lyumjev lowered HbA1c slightly more than mealtime Humalog (difference −0.08%); Lyumjev given 20 minutes after the meal start was slightly worse than mealtime Humalog (+0.13%). Mealtime Lyumjev gave much smaller 1-hour and 2-hour post-meal rises (1 h: about −1.55 mmol/L, −28 mg/dL vs Humalog) [Section 5.1, Table 2].
- Severe hypoglycaemia in that trial: 5.5% (mealtime Lyumjev), 5.7% (Humalog), 4.6% (post-meal Lyumjev) [Section 5.1, Table 2].
- Giving Lyumjev at a different time relative to the meal did not change the frequency of hypoglycaemia in a clinically relevant way [Section 4.8].
- Missed mealtime dose: check glucose to decide whether insulin is needed, then resume the usual schedule at the next meal [Section 4.2].

## Time in range with CGM [Section 5.1]

- Blinded CGM subset of PRONTO-T1D (n = 269): mealtime Lyumjev gave better post-meal control at 0–2, 0–3 and 0–4 hours after meals [Section 5.1].
- Daytime (6 am to midnight) time in range: 603 minutes at 3.9–10 mmol/L (71–180 mg/dL), 44 minutes more than Humalog; 396 minutes at 3.9–7.8 mmol/L (71–140 mg/dL), 41 minutes more [Section 5.1].

## Dosing and dose adjustment [Section 4.2, 4.4]

- Start dose takes into account the type of diabetes, weight and glucose levels; adjust by metabolic needs, glucose monitoring and goals; the early onset must be considered [Section 4.2].
- Dose changes may be needed when switching insulin, with changes in physical activity, other medicines, meal amount, type or timing, kidney or liver function, and during acute illness [Section 4.2].
- Switching from another mealtime insulin: unit for unit [Section 4.2].
- Illness or emotional stress may raise insulin needs; exercise right after a meal may increase the risk of hypoglycaemia [Section 4.4].
- Insulin needs can fall with renal or hepatic impairment; monitor more closely [Section 4.2].
- Junior KwikPen gives 0.5-unit steps (0.5–30 units) for people who need finer dose changes; KwikPen gives 1–60 units in 1-unit steps [Section 4.2].

## Pump use (CSII) [Section 4.2, 4.4, 4.8, 5.1, 6.6]

- Lyumjev 100 units/mL is suitable for CSII and then covers both bolus and basal needs; fill the reservoir from a vial [Section 4.2].
- Follow pump and infusion-set instructions; change the infusion set as its instructions say; rotate sites [Section 4.2].
- Pump malfunction or a blocked infusion set can make glucose rise fast and lead quickly to hyperglycaemia and ketoacidosis; find and fix the cause promptly; injections may be needed in the meantime [Section 4.2, 4.4].
- Carry a spare or other way to give insulin in case the delivery system fails [Section 4.2].
- Maximum time in a pump: 9 days from a 100 units/mL vial; polyethylene or polyolefin tubing is compatible [Section 6.6]. An opened vial, cartridge or pen lasts 28 days [Section 6.3].
- PRONTO-Pump (12-week crossover): no difference in infusion set failures vs Humalog; more time at 3.9–7.8 mmol/L (71–140 mg/dL) 1 and 2 hours after breakfast [Section 5.1].
- PRONTO-Pump-2 (16 weeks, n = 432): HbA1c non-inferior to Humalog; lower 1-hour (−1.34 mmol/L) and 2-hour (−1.54 mmol/L) post-meal glucose; infusion site reactions in 38%, 3.3% stopped because of them [Section 4.8, 5.1].
- The 200 units/mL pen must not be used in a pump or intravenously, and insulin must not be drawn from it into a syringe (risk of overdose) [Section 4.2, 4.4 (200 units/mL SmPC)].

## Hypoglycaemia [Section 4.3, 4.4, 4.8, 4.9]

- Hypoglycaemia is a contraindication: do not give Lyumjev during hypoglycaemia [Section 4.3].
- It is the most common side effect; its timing follows the insulin's time-action profile and may come earlier than with other mealtime insulins [Section 4.4].
- Symptoms can vary between people and over time; awareness may be reduced in long-standing diabetes; severe episodes can cause seizures, unconsciousness or death [Section 4.4].
- Severe hypoglycaemia in 26-week trials: 5.5% in adults with type 1, 0.9% with type 2, 0.7% in children [Section 4.8].
- Treatment: mild episodes usually with oral glucose; more severe episodes (coma, seizure, neurological impairment) with glucagon or concentrated intravenous glucose. Ongoing carbohydrate intake and observation may be needed, because hypoglycaemia can return after apparent recovery [Section 4.9].
- Large amounts of alcohol with insulin can cause severe hypoglycaemia; beta-blockers may blunt warning signs [Section 4.5].
- Driving: take precautions, particularly with reduced awareness or frequent episodes [Section 4.7].

## Hyperglycaemia and DKA [Section 4.4, 4.5]

- Too little insulin or stopping treatment can cause hyperglycaemia and diabetic ketoacidosis, which can be fatal; patients should learn the signs of ketoacidosis and get help at once if it is suspected [Section 4.4].
- Medicines that may raise insulin needs include corticosteroids, thyroid hormones, oral contraceptives, diuretics, sympathomimetics and growth hormone; medicines that may lower them include other glucose-lowering drugs, salicylates, sulphonamides, some antidepressants, ACE inhibitors, angiotensin II receptor blockers and somatostatin analogues [Section 4.5].

## Children and adolescents [Section 4.2, 4.8, 5.1]

- 100 units/mL: from age 1 year; no clinical experience under 3 years; doses set individually as in adults; 0–2 minutes before a meal with the option of up to 20 minutes after the start when needed [Section 4.2].
- PRONTO-Peds (3 to < 18 years, n = 716, 26 weeks, with basal insulin): HbA1c similar to Humalog both for mealtime and post-meal (within 20 min) Lyumjev [Section 5.1, Table 4].
- Injection site reactions in 6.2% of children (mild or moderate); otherwise the safety profile matched adults [Section 4.8].

## Injection sites and handling [Section 4.2, 4.4, 6.6]

- Subcutaneous injection in the abdomen, upper arm, thigh or buttocks; rotate within a region to lower the risk of lipodystrophy and cutaneous amyloidosis [Section 4.2].
- Injecting into affected (lumpy) sites can delay absorption; switching suddenly to unaffected tissue has caused hypoglycaemia, so check glucose and consider dose changes [Section 4.4].
- Do not use if cloudy, coloured, with particles, or after freezing [Section 6.6].

## Limits of this summary

- Covers the SmPC (Annex I) for 100 and 200 units/mL; labelling, package leaflet and most trial tables (including type 2 diabetes detail) are left out.
- Clamp and pharmacokinetic numbers come from single-dose studies (mostly 15 units in adults); real-life action varies with dose, site and activity.
- Paediatric evidence covers ages 3–17 years; experience under 3 years and over 75 years is limited.
- For prescribing or dosing decisions read the current EMA product information and follow the treating team.
