# Tandem Control-IQ: automatic correction bolus = 60% of the correction to 110 mg/dL when glucose is predicted >= 180 mg/dL in 30 min, at most once per 60 min, max 6 U; bolus calculator subtracts IOB that includes all automated insulin

## Summary in one paragraph

Control-IQ predicts CGM glucose 30 minutes ahead every 5 minutes. When the 30-minute prediction is at or above 180 mg/dL and Control-IQ is already increasing basal (or at its maximum), the pump gives an automatic correction bolus of 60% of the correction calculated from the user's Personal Profile correction factor and the predicted CGM value, aiming at 110 mg/dL. Automatic corrections occur at most once every 60 minutes, never within 60 minutes of the start, cancellation or completion of any manual or automatic bolus, are capped at 6 units each (not adjustable), and are not given during Sleep activity. The manual bolus calculator (no trend arrows, CGM value auto-populated) adds a correction when glucose is above the profile target and subtracts IOB only from that correction portion; with Control-IQ on, IOB includes all basal delivered above and below the programmed basal rate plus all boluses, including automatic corrections, and insulin duration is fixed at 5 hours. Clinical tools (PANTHER, Berget 2022, Messer's CARES) tell users to give corrections only as recommended by the pump's calculator and not to override it upwards, because automated insulin is already on board.

## Automatic correction bolus (User Guide 7.4, Section 29.2)

- **Trigger:** predicted CGM value "at or above 180 mg/dL 30 minutes in the future" and Control-IQ is either Increasing Insulin Delivery or delivering Maximum Insulin Delivery.
- **Size:** "60% of the total correction bolus calculated based on the Personal Profile correction factor and predicted CGM reading". Target for the automatic correction: 110 mg/dL. (A UK NHS supplement, Cambridge University Hospitals, adds that the calculation also uses insulin on board; Berget 2022 Table 1: "60% of calculated correction dose using a fixed target of 110 mg/dL". The PANTHER tool words it as "uses 60% of the programmed correction factor", which reads differently; the user guide wording is the primary one.) [IOB handling inside the automatic correction: unverified in the primary guide]
- **Frequency:** at most once every 60 minutes, and not within 60 minutes of the start, cancellation or completion of an automatic or manual bolus (for an extended bolus, the 60 minutes start after the "deliver now" part). The guide states that "the percentage and duration between boluses is designed to avoid insulin stacking". Section 7: "If you deliver a manual bolus, Control-IQ technology will not be able to deliver an automatic correction bolus until 60 minutes after the manual bolus has completed."
- **Cap:** maximum 6 units per automatic correction; cannot be increased, "but you may choose to deliver a manual bolus after the automatic correction bolus delivery is complete". Technical specification table: Maximum Automatic Bolus Size 6 units.
- **No sound or vibration** when an automatic correction starts (screen icon only); it can be cancelled like a manual bolus.
- **Activities:** no activity: target range 112.5-160 mg/dL, basal decreased at predicted <= 112.5, set to 0 at predicted <= 70, increased at predicted >= 160 mg/dL; automatic corrections on. Sleep: 112.5-120 mg/dL, increase at >= 120, **no automatic correction boluses**. Exercise: 140-160 mg/dL, suspend at predicted <= 80, automatic corrections allowed.
- **Maximum insulin delivery** (basal increase ceiling) depends on correction factor, Control-IQ's estimated total daily insulin and current IOB. Berget 2022 Table 1 adds a 2-hour safety constraint of 50% of the programmed TDI in any 2-hour period.
- **Updates (not verified in a primary guide):** Control-IQ+ (2024-2025, labelled for type 1 diabetes from age 2, TDD >= 5 U, weight >= 20 lb/9 kg) is described by diaTribe as still giving about 60% of a full correction with a 110 mg/dL target, up to once per hour; the 6-U cap for Control-IQ+ was not confirmed. [unverified]

## Manual bolus calculator and IOB (User Guide 7.4, Sections 5 and 7.2)

- **Above target BG:** "the insulin for the food bolus and the correction bolus will be added together. If IOB is present, it is subtracted only from the correction portion of the bolus." (So IOB never reduces the carb part when glucose is above target.)
- **Between 70 mg/dL and target:** option to reduce the food bolus (reverse correction prompt) and IOB also reduces the bolus. **Below 70 mg/dL:** the food bolus is reduced automatically, plus IOB.
- **What IOB contains with Control-IQ on:** "IOB includes all basal delivered above and below the programmed basal rate, in addition to all bolus insulin delivered." Insulin duration is fixed at 5 hours with Control-IQ and cannot be changed. So automatic corrections and above-basal Control-IQ delivery are subtracted from a manual correction; below-basal (reduced/suspended) delivery counts negatively.
- **CGM auto-population:** with Control-IQ on, the current CGM value is entered into the calculator automatically; "only the current CGM reading is used to calculate the correction bolus. The trend arrow is not used" (also an FDA labelling requirement in DEN190034, because CGM point values without trend may give inaccurate boluses).
- **Stacking advice:** "Sometimes you should not treat at all, but rather watch and wait, including stacking insulin. Do not take insulin doses too close together. If you have recently given a bolus, you might wait 60 minutes to see if your readings respond to the bolus."
- **Post-correction hypoglycaemia data (Section 32.10, pivotal trials DCLP3 and DCLP5):** after correction boluses calculated with auto-populated CGM values, one or more CGM readings < 54 mg/dL within 5 hours occurred after 4% (DCLP3, n = 17,023 boluses) and 6% (DCLP5, n = 12,323) of boluses; three consecutive readings < 70 mg/dL after 8% and 15%. When the starting sensor glucose was > 250 mg/dL (DCLP3), auto-populated boluses were followed by < 54 mg/dL in 5% vs 4% for manually entered values, and five or more readings < 70 mg/dL in 13% vs 9%; the guide reports this as an increased incidence of lows after auto-populated corrections from > 250 mg/dL.

## When to give a manual correction / high glucose (manufacturer and clinical tools)

- **User guide warnings:** if BG does not fall after a bolus, check the infusion set for occlusion, air bubbles, leaks or cannula dislodgement; always be ready to inject insulin another way, because pump-only rapid insulin means interruption can lead to very high BG or DKA. "Delivering large boluses, or delivering multiple boluses back to back may cause hypoglycemia." Set High and Low CGM alerts so that highs can be treated per the healthcare provider.
- **PANTHER Control-IQ tool (2023, clinician worksheet and patient after-visit summary):** "Give correction boluses for hyperglycemia, following the dose recommended by the pump to avoid the risk of hypoglycemia." "Do not override boluses to give more insulin than the pump recommends (may cause hypoglycemia if Control-IQ has been increasing insulin delivery)." "There may be a lot of IOB from AID that user is not aware of. Bolus calculator factors in IOB from increased AID when calculating correction bolus dose." If bolusing after a meal, reduce the dose by entering fewer carbs than eaten, because Control-IQ will already have increased delivery. Hyperglycaemia > 300 mg/dL (16.7 mmol/L) for 2 hours or more: check ketones first; if ketones > 1.0 mmol/L (moderate/large urine), give a syringe injection and change the infusion set. Change sets every 2-3 days or as needed for persistent hyperglycaemia. Weakening the correction factor by 10-20% if lows follow corrections 2-3 h later "will impact both user-given and auto-correction boluses".
- **Berget 2022 Table 1** (US AID comparison): Control-IQ auto-correction boluses "maximum 1/hour only if there is no user-initiated bolus in the past hour; 60% of calculated correction dose using a fixed target of 110 mg/dL"; target range 112.5-160 mg/dL non-adjustable; Control-IQ and Omnipod 5 use the programmed correction factor for user-initiated corrections in both automated and manual modes.
- **Consensus (already in corpus, not repeated):** Phillip 2023 Table 1/8 (autocorrection 60% to 110 mg/dL, can occur 1 h after a previous bolus; no reverse correction; no CGM trend in calculator) and Messer 2019 CARES (do not override the Control-IQ bolus calculator).

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance:** Control-IQ is the clearest commercial example of a deliberately partial automatic correction: 60% of the needed correction, once per hour, max 6 U, with a 60-minute lockout after any manual bolus. Its manual calculator still adds a full correction (to the user's target) but nets out IOB that includes every automated unit, and it applies IOB only to the correction part when glucose is above target. A meal card that adds a correction is therefore standard practice on Control-IQ, provided the correction is reduced by all insulin on board, including automated insulin.
- **Evidence level:** manufacturer labelling (design description), FDA summary, clinical education tools. The DCLP3/DCLP5 post-correction hypoglycaemia figures are post-hoc labelling analyses.
