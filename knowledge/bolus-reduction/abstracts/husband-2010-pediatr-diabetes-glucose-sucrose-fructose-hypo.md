# The effectiveness of glucose, sucrose, and fructose in treating hypoglycemia in children with type 1 diabetes

- **Authors:** Husband AC, Crawford S, McCoy LA, Pacaud D
- **Journal/year:** Pediatric Diabetes 2010;11(3):154–158
- **DOI:** 10.1111/j.1399-5448.2009.00558.x
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19663922/ (trial NCT00706693)
- **Licence:** paraphrased
- **Saved:** summary from the PubMed/Europe PMC abstract and the ClinicalTrials.gov record description on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus.

## Design

- Randomized crossover, 33 children aged 5.4–15.5 y (mean diabetes duration 3.1 y), Calgary, Canada.
- Each child treated 5 spontaneous hypos with each of: glucose (BD glucose tablets), sucrose (Skittles), fructose (fruit leather "Fruit to Go").
- Success = meter glucose ≥ 4.0 mmol/L 15 min after treatment.
- Dose by age, not weight (as reported in secondary descriptions): 10 g carbohydrate for younger and 15 g for older children (not verified in the full text).

## Results

- Treatments differed overall (p = 0.001). Glucose and sucrose were not different; fructose was clearly less effective than both (p < 0.001).
- After the study most children preferred an effective option (glucose or sucrose).

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance:** Fructose-rich carbs (fruit products, much of fruit juice) are poor hypo treatment; sucrose ≈ glucose. When a child eats a meal while low, fruit/fructose-heavy or fat-rich parts should not be counted as "fast treatment".
- **Population:** school-age children, injections/pump (pre-AID).
- **Evidence level:** small randomized crossover trial.
