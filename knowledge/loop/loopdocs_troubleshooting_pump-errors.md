# LoopDocs: common Medtronic pump errors with Loop

## Medtronic pump errors: scope and caution [section "Medtronic Pump Errors"]

The Medtronic pumps used with Loop are older, usually second-hand and out of warranty; LoopDocs says its repair notes are used at your own risk, while several common errors are fixable or harmless.

## Medtronic A21 error [section "A21 error"]

Common on pumps stored without a battery, so most used pumps show it at first; not serious. Scroll with the down arrow (light-bulb key) to read how to clear it: ESC, then ACT. A small black/white bar at the right of the pump screen is a scroll bar. If A21 appears on a pump that was not stored, fit a fresh battery; an old battery or battery cap is the likely cause, and the cap should be checked and cleaned of dirt or rust.

## Medtronic Batt Out Limit error [section "Batt Out Limit"]

Refers to the internal pump battery (not the replaceable AAA), which cannot be replaced and wears out. Mostly an annoyance: changing the AAA faster may help; at worst time and date must be set at every battery change. Accepting the defaults is fine because Loop sets time and date itself, but LoopDocs reminds you to set the time through the RileyLink afterwards.

## Medtronic Button Error [section "Button Error"]

Usually water, moisture or dust under the button pad. The fix (under 30 minutes) is lifting the button-pad sticker and cleaning the layers beneath, where dirt or rust is often visible. Afterwards, clear packing tape over the pump face keeps water and dirt out of the seams.

## Medtronic cracks and missing plastic [section "Crack/Missing Piece Repairs"]

Cracks or missing bits near the battery cap or reservoir sleeve are common. Small cracks: plastic cement or epoxy. Bigger missing pieces: epoxy or Sugru. Teflon thread tape on the battery cap stops the filler sticking while recreating the threads.

## Medtronic Motor error [section "Motor error"]

Often a badly seated reservoir or tubing cap. First disconnect from the infusion site. Remove and reseat the reservoir and prime again; if it persists, replace the tubing (new tubing cap); if still not resolved, replace the reservoir too.

## Medtronic A33 error and safety warning [section "A33 error"]

- Safety first: always disconnect the tubing from the infusion set, and never press on the bulging end cap, because pushing it back may deliver a dangerous amount of insulin.
- Cause: a loose drive support (end) cap, usually during priming; the plunger loses pressure and the pump reports A33.
- Repair described: with the reservoir out, push the drive so the end cap sticks out as far as possible, peel and keep the sticker, apply super glue gel (not liquid glue) generously around the cap, press it back in hard with a napkin for a few seconds, remove napkin bits, and let it dry 10-15 minutes. Then press firmly on the plunger; if the cap does not move, the repair held (otherwise glue again). Finish with glue on top and reattach the sticker.

## Medtronic A32 and E22 error loop [section "A32 and E22 error loop"]

Appears to kill the pump. Medtronic support described A32 as flash-memory failure and E22 as needing software reinstallation. Typically E22 appears, and once cleared A32 follows, repeating with each restart. LoopDocs knows of no fix.
