*Our summary (Mellis knowledge base), written in our own words. It is not the original text and not medical advice. Original: Edd S, Laron-Hirsch M, Castañeda J, van den Heuvel T, Petrovski G, Cohen O. A narrative review of meal management with the MiniMed 780G system: evolution of dietary management in type 1 diabetes. Diabetes, Obesity and Metabolism 2026;28(Suppl 1):61-68, https://doi.org/10.1111/dom.70310. Note: industry narrative review; five of six authors are Medtronic employees and the sixth has received Medtronic fees [Conflict of interest].*

## Key points

- Narrative (not systematic) review of studies with the MiniMed 780G advanced hybrid closed loop (SmartGuard), which gives automatic basal plus auto-correction micro-boluses every 5 minutes [MiniMed 780G System; Search Strategy].
- Bolus timing: a bolus at the start of the meal gave 4-hour TIR similar to a bolus 10 minutes before (82% vs 77%). A full pre-meal bolus gave the best results (TIR 87% in one study); bolusing later than 30 minutes after eating gave larger excursions [Bolus Timing].
- If the pre-meal bolus is missed, the authors suggest a partial bolus for 50% of the carbs within 30 minutes of eating; after 30 minutes they see no advantage in a manual bolus with this system, because auto-corrections take over [Bolus Timing].
- Unannounced meals: up to about 20-30 g carbohydrate were handled acceptably; larger unannounced meals lowered TIR (67.5% vs 77.7% with announcement), so announcing meals is still recommended [Missed Bolus].
- High-fat/high-protein dinners: closed loop with plain carb counting or a predefined bolus gave 5-hour TIR similar to an open-loop dual-wave bolus with a 30% insulin increase, and better 10-hour TIR; open loop needed night corrections after 73% of meals [Fasting & Diets].
- Real-world data: settings of glucose target 100 mg/dL (5.5 mmol/L) and active insulin time 2 hours, and 2-3 carb ratios a day, were linked with the best TIR [Abstract; Real World].

## How the 780G handles meals (device-specific)

- Safe Correction Bolus reduces a correction if a low is predicted within 2 hours; Meal Detection allows more aggressive auto-corrections after an unbolused meal; Safe Meal Bolus cuts a manual bolus if a low is predicted within 4 hours [MiniMed 780G System].
- The authors advise not to override these reductions (for example with "assisted carbs"). The 780G has no dual-wave bolus; the authors regard it as unnecessary on this system [MiniMed 780G System].

## Bolus timing and missed bolus studies

- Hirsh et al. (13 adults): pre-meal 10 minutes vs at meal start similar; 50% bolus at 30 or 60 minutes after eating gave TIR of 63% and 56% [Bolus Timing].
- Grassi et al. (22 people, 60 g meals): no bolus (TIR 62%) and a 50% bolus 1 hour after eating (54%) did not differ; both were worse than a correct pre-meal bolus [Bolus Timing].
- Shalit et al. (14 adults): not announcing meals up to 60 g did not raise time above 250 mg/dL (13.9 mmol/L); meals up to 20 g had the same TIR announced or not; no serious adverse events [Missed Bolus].
- Tornese et al. (14 children and adolescents): unannounced snacks of 20 g or less raised glucose by 50 mg/dL (2.8 mmol/L) or less at 2 hours; up to 30 g gave 2-hour TIR of 70% or more [Missed Bolus].

## Simplified carb counting

- Adolescents (Petrovski et al., 34): three fixed personal carb amounts (snack, regular, large) gave TIR 73.5% vs 80.3% with precise counting; HbA1c did not differ, and both groups met TIR above 70% and HbA1c below 7% over 18 months. Auto-corrections nearly doubled in the fixed group [Carb Counting].
- Adults (Minsky et al., 14): a multistep fixed amount (17-23 g steps) gave TIR 80.5%, versus 77.7% with precise counting and 75.4% with one fixed amount [Carb Counting].

## Children: carb ratios over time

- In users aged 15 or under, TIR and the share reaching the target fell over 12 months while total daily insulin rose but carb ratios stayed the same; the authors advise reassessing carb ratios often in growing children [Real World].
- More than 2 carb ratios a day brought no clinically relevant gain (effects below 1% TIR) [Real World; Table 1].

## Other diets

- During Ramadan (449 users), TIR fell only from 71.7% to 70.7%, and average targets (TIR above 70%, time below 70 mg/dL under 4%, GMI below 7%) were kept [Fasting & Diets].

## Limits of this summary

- Industry-authored narrative review of mostly small, often single-arm studies; results apply to the MiniMed 780G algorithm and settings, not to Trio, Loop or oref-based systems.
- The 50%-within-30-minutes advice and the view that dual-wave is obsolete are specific to this system and its authors.
- Ramadan guidance and statistical methods are left out; read the original for clinical decisions.
