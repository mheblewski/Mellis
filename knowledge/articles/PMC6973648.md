*Our summary (Mellis knowledge base), written in our own words. It is not the original text and not medical advice. Original: Battelino T, Danne T, Bergenstal RM, et al. Clinical targets for continuous glucose monitoring data interpretation: recommendations from the International Consensus on Time in Range. Diabetes Care 2019;42(8):1593-1603, https://doi.org/10.2337/dci19-0028.*

## Key points

- An international ATTD panel (February 2019, clinicians, researchers and people with diabetes) agreed CGM targets for time in range (TIR), time below range (TBR) and time above range (TAR) [Introduction].
- Standard range for type 1 and type 2 diabetes: 70-180 mg/dL (3.9-10.0 mmol/L). Most adults should aim for TIR above 70%, TBR below 4% under 70 mg/dL and below 1% under 54 mg/dL (3.0 mmol/L), TAR below 25% over 180 mg/dL and below 5% over 250 mg/dL (13.9 mmol/L) [Table 3].
- The first priority is to bring TBR down to target; only then work on TIR or TAR [Time in Ranges].
- Targets must be personalised. Older or high-risk people get looser TIR (above 50%) and stricter hypoglycaemia limits (below 1% under 70 mg/dL) [Table 3; Clinical Validity of Measures].
- TIR of 70% corresponds roughly to A1C 7% (53 mmol/mol) and TIR 50% to about 8% (64 mmol/mol); each 10% more TIR is about 0.5% lower A1C [Relationship Between TIR and A1C].
- Small gains count: an increase of 5% TIR is described as clinically meaningful [Goal Setting].

## Targets by group (our layout of Tables 3 and 4)

| Group | Range | TIR | TBR | TAR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Type 1 / type 2 diabetes | 70-180 mg/dL (3.9-10.0 mmol/L) | >70% (more than 16 h 48 min a day) | <4% below 70 mg/dL (<1 h); <1% below 54 mg/dL (<15 min) | <25% above 180 mg/dL (<6 h); <5% above 250 mg/dL (<1 h 12 min) |
| Older or high-risk, type 1 / type 2 | 70-180 mg/dL (3.9-10.0 mmol/L) | >50% (more than 12 h) | <1% below 70 mg/dL (<15 min) | <10% above 250 mg/dL (<2 h 24 min) |
| Pregnancy, type 1 | 63-140 mg/dL (3.5-7.8 mmol/L) | >70% (more than 16 h 48 min) | <4% below 63 mg/dL (<1 h); <1% below 54 mg/dL (<15 min) | <25% above 140 mg/dL (<6 h) |
| Pregnancy, type 2 or gestational diabetes | 63-140 mg/dL (3.5-7.8 mmol/L) | no percentage set (too little evidence) | no percentage set | no percentage set |

- Times per day follow from the percentages of a 24-hour day; the panel says either form may be used, and suggests that "less than 1 hour" and "less than 15 minutes" below range may motivate people more than 4% and 1% [Time in Ranges; Goal Setting].
- High-risk includes people with cognitive deficits, kidney disease, joint disease, osteoporosis or fracture, cardiovascular disease, or those in assisted care [Older and/or High-Risk Individuals].

## Children, adolescents and young adults

- The panel restates ISPAD advice for people aged 25 or under: aim for the lowest A1C reachable without undue severe hypoglycaemia or loss of quality of life; A1C 7.0% (53 mmol/mol) where comprehensive care is available [Goal Setting].
- A higher goal such as A1C below 7.5% (58 mmol/mol) may fit when the young person cannot describe hypo symptoms, has hypoglycaemia unawareness or past severe hypoglycaemia, lacks analogue insulin or advanced technology, or cannot check glucose regularly. That corresponds to a TIR target of about 60% [Goal Setting].
- For children, adolescents and high-risk people the panel recommends a stepwise approach with small, achievable goals [Goal Setting].

## The 10 core CGM metrics for clinical care [Table 2]

1. Days of CGM wear (14 days recommended).
2. Time CGM is active (70% of 14 days' data recommended).
3. Mean glucose.
4. Glucose management indicator (GMI).
5. Glycaemic variability as %CV, target 36% or less.
6. TAR level 2: above 250 mg/dL (13.9 mmol/L).
7. TAR level 1: 181-250 mg/dL (10.1-13.9 mmol/L).
8. TIR: 70-180 mg/dL (3.9-10.0 mmol/L).
9. TBR level 1: 54-69 mg/dL (3.0-3.8 mmol/L).
10. TBR level 2: below 54 mg/dL (3.0 mmol/L).

- At least 70% wear over the latest 14 days tracks 3-month mean glucose, time in ranges and hyperglycaemia well; hypoglycaemia exposure in variable patients may need around 4 weeks of data [Standardization of CGM Metrics].
- The Ambulatory Glucose Profile (AGP) is the recommended one-page report, with levels named Very High, High, Low and Very Low [Standardization of CGM Data Presentation].

## TIR, A1C and complications

- From 545 adults with type 1 diabetes (Beck et al.): TIR 20% ≈ A1C 9.4%; 30% ≈ 8.9%; 40% ≈ 8.4%; 50% ≈ 7.9%; 60% ≈ 7.4%; 70% ≈ 7.0%; 80% ≈ 6.5%; 90% ≈ 6.0%, with wide 95% intervals (about ±1.3 points) [Table 5].
- A second dataset (Vigersky and McMahon, type 1 and type 2) gave a steeper slope, about 0.8% A1C per 10% TIR [Table 5].
- In DCCT data, each 10% lower TIR was linked with 64% higher hazard of retinopathy progression and 40% higher hazard of microalbuminuria; time below 70 and 54 mg/dL was linked with more severe hypoglycaemia [Association With Complications].
- The panel notes the type 1 targets reflect what hybrid closed-loop (AID) therapy can achieve, and that people on injections with CGM may also reach them [Time in Ranges].

## Pregnancy (brief)

- Goal is to raise TIR quickly and safely; 70% TIR at 63-140 mg/dL is reached by about a third of women with type 1 diabetes in late pregnancy, and a 5-7% higher TIR in the 2nd and 3rd trimesters is associated with better neonatal outcomes [Pregnancy].

## Limits of this summary

- Expert consensus based on mostly observational and lower-level evidence; long-term outcome trials for CGM metrics did not exist at the time.
- Adult-focused; the paediatric part is limited to the ISPAD A1C statement and a TIR of about 60% for a 7.5% A1C goal.
- Pregnancy, AGP report design and goal-setting methods are shortened here; read the original for clinical decisions and for later updates to these targets.
