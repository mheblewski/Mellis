# Treating glucose levels under 4 mmol/L: children & young people with type 1 diabetes (Royal United Hospitals Bath, leaflet DIA/010)

- **Authors:** Royal United Hospitals Bath NHS Foundation Trust, paediatric diabetes team
- **Journal/year:** patient leaflet, March 2023 (ref DIA/010)
- **DOI:** none
- **URL:** https://www.ruh.nhs.uk/patients/services/clinical_depts/paediatrics/paediatric_diabetes/documents/patient_info/DIA010_MDI_Hypoglycaemia.pdf
- **Licence:** paraphrased (© Royal United Hospitals Bath NHS Foundation Trust)
- **Saved:** summary from the trust PDF on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus. Patient education material (lower evidence).

## Amounts (0.3 g/kg rounded by age group)

| Age group | Fast-acting glucose |
|---|---|
| Toddler | 3 g |
| Pre-school | 5 g |
| Primary school (5–10 y) | 10 g |
| Secondary (10–18 y) | 15 g |

- Steps: give treatment, wait 15–20 min, re-check; repeat until above 4 mmol/L; then check again 20–30 min later.
- A follow-up carbohydrate snack without insulin (same amount as the treatment, 3–15 g) only when exercise, alcohol or too much insulin is the likely cause.

## Low sensor reading while eating a meal (key rule)

- If the sensor alerts below 4 mmol/L while the child is eating a meal, hypo treatment is usually not needed.
- Consider the usual glucose treatment only if the child has symptoms, or glucose is below 3.5 mmol/L with a downward arrow while eating.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance:** The only source found that explicitly treats meal carbohydrate as sufficient for a mild sensor low that happens DURING a meal: no separate treatment unless symptomatic or < 3.5 mmol/L and falling. It does not say how to dose insulin for that meal.
- **Population:** children/young people on MDI (leaflet code "MDI").
- **Evidence level:** local patient leaflet (expert opinion).
