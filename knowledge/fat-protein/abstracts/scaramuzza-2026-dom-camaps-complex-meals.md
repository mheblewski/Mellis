# Optimising the CamAPS FX automated insulin delivery system for exercise and complex meals in adolescents with type 1 diabetes: a prospective interventional study in a camp setting

- **Authors:** Scaramuzza AE, Marigliano M, Moser O, Abate Daga F, Bombaci B, Franceschi R, Presti DL, Moretti C, et al.
- **Journal/year:** Diabetes, Obesity and Metabolism 2026;28(2):1556-1560 (online 2025)
- **DOI:** 10.1111/dom.70295
- **PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41267125/
- **Licence:** not open access to automated tools; nothing from the paper is reproduced.

## What could be verified

- PubMed lists no abstract text for this short report, and the publisher and repository pages blocked automated download, so the paper itself was **not read**.
- A search-engine summary of the paper (not the paper) reported: 26 adolescents on CamAPS FX at a diabetes camp; for complex meals (rich in carbohydrate, fat and protein) three strategies were compared - **100% standard bolus, 50/50 split bolus, and 70/30 split using the CamAPS "slowly absorbed meal" option** - and the **50/50 split** gave the highest postprandial TIR and the lowest hyperglycaemia exposure. Small sample and camp setting limit generalisation.

## Notes for Mellis

- Treat these numbers as **unverified** until the paper is read. It is the only identified study that tests split boluses *under AID* in adolescents.
