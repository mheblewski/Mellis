# A bolus calculator is an effective means of controlling postprandial glycemia in patients on insulin pump therapy

- **Authors:** Gross TM, Kayne D, King A, Rother C, Juth S (Medtronic MiniMed)
- **Journal/year:** Diabetes Technology & Therapeutics 2003;5(3):365–369
- **DOI:** 10.1089/152091503765691848
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12828818/
- **Licence:** paraphrased (subscription article; PubMed abstract only)
- **Saved:** summary from the PubMed abstract on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus. (Often cited as "Calculation of mealtime insulin doses using the Bolus Wizard"; this is the indexed title.)

## Design

- Randomised crossover, 7 days per arm, 49 adults with type 1 diabetes experienced with CSII.
- Arm 1 "Bolus Calculator": physicians programmed each patient's parameters (current BG, target BG, carb-to-insulin ratio, grams of carbohydrate, insulin sensitivity factor) into a PDA running the Medtronic MiniMed calculator; patients used its recommended pre-meal dose. Arm 2 "Standard Bolus": patients' usual empirical estimate.

## Results

- With standard bolusing, significantly more correction boluses were needed for post-meal highs (p = 0.008) and more supplemental carbohydrate was eaten to treat lows (p = 0.046).
- Average deviation of the 2-h post-meal glucose from target was similar between methods.
- Patients found the calculator easy and trusted its doses.

## What could not be verified

- The abstract does not say how the calculator handled a pre-meal BG below target (negative correction) or IOB, nor how many lows occurred before meals.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance:** Early trial evidence that a formula-based dose using current BG vs target (which includes reducing the dose when BG is lower) led to less carbohydrate needed for lows than "dosing by feel", without worse post-meal control.
- **Evidence level:** small randomised crossover trial, 1 week per arm, adults, pre-CGM.
