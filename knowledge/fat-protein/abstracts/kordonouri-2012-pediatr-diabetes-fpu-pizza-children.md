# Benefit of supplementary fat plus protein counting as compared with conventional carbohydrate counting for insulin bolus calculation in children with pump therapy

- **Authors:** Kordonouri O, Hartmann R, Remus K, Bläsig S, Sadeghian E, Danne T
- **Journal/year:** Pediatric Diabetes 2012;13(7):540-544
- **DOI:** 10.1111/j.1399-5448.2012.00880.x
- **PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22765260/
- **Licence:** not open access (or open-access status not confirmed). This file contains a paraphrased summary of the abstract and key numbers written for the Mellis corpus, not the publisher's text.

## Summary of the abstract (paraphrased)

- **Population:** 42 patients aged 6-21 years with T1D ≥ 1 year (5.2 ± 3.1 years) and pump therapy ≥ 3 months (Hannover, Germany). ClinicalTrials.gov NCT01400659.
- **Meal:** salami pizza (50% energy from carbohydrate, 34% fat, 16% protein; one third of the age-adjusted daily energy requirement) at lunch on four days.
- **Arms:** normal vs dual-wave bolus, each with carbohydrate counting (CARB) or carbohydrate + fat/protein counting (CFP, i.e. the Warsaw/Pańkowska FPU method).
- **6-h glucose:** CFP gave lower AUC (805 ± 261 vs 926 ± 285 mg/dL × 6 h) and lower average glucose (137.8 ± 46.2 vs 160.5 ± 51.9 mg/dL) than CARB (p < 0.001). The benefit held for both normal bolus (ΔAUC 169, ΔAV 30.6 mg/dL) and dual-wave bolus (ΔAUC 73, ΔAV 14.8 mg/dL).
- **Hypoglycaemia (< 70 mg/dL) after the meal:** **35.7% with CFP vs 9.5% with CARB (p < 0.001)**. No severe hypoglycaemia.

## Notes for Mellis

- Key paediatric safety signal: the **full FPU dose roughly quadrupled postprandial hypoglycaemia** (35.7% vs 9.5%). This is why later groups reduced the FPU factor (for example Trio's default 50% "Fat and Protein Percentage") and why ISPAD 2022 warns about hypoglycaemia with the FPU formula.
