*Our summary (Mellis knowledge base), written in our own words. It is not the original text and not medical advice. Original: Smart CE, Evans M, O'Connell SM, McElduff P, Lopez PE, Jones TW, Davis EA, King BR. Both dietary protein and fat increase postprandial glucose excursions in children with type 1 diabetes, and the effect is additive. Diabetes Care 2013;36(12):3897-3902, https://doi.org/10.2337/dc13-1195.*

## Key points

- 33 children and adolescents (8-17 years) with type 1 diabetes ate four breakfasts with the same 30 g carbohydrate and the same insulin dose (from their own insulin-to-carbohydrate ratio), varying only fat (4 g vs 35 g) and protein (5 g vs 40 g) [Abstract; Test meals].
- High protein alone raised glucose more than the low-fat/low-protein meal from 180 minutes on (excursion 2.4 vs 0.5 mmol/L, P = 0.02); high fat alone did so from 210 minutes (1.8 vs −0.5 mmol/L, P = 0.01) [Abstract; Results].
- Fat and protein together had an additive effect: highest glucose from 180 to 300 minutes; at 5 hours the high-fat/high-protein (HF/HP) meal was 5.4 mmol/L (about 97 mg/dL) above the low-fat/low-protein meal [Results].
- High fat alone lowered glucose in the first 60-90 minutes (slower gastric emptying); adding protein removed this early dip [Results; Conclusions].
- Protein reduced the risk of post-meal hypoglycaemia (odds ratio 0.16, P < 0.001); fat did not significantly (OR 0.50, P = 0.08) [Hypoglycemic events].
- The authors suggest high-fat/high-protein meals need both extra insulin and a prolonged delivery (dual-wave or split bolus) to avoid early lows and late highs [Conclusions].

## Design

- Randomized 4×4 crossover at two Australian paediatric centres; pump or MDI (4 or more injections a day); HbA1c 8.0% (64 mmol/mol) or below [Research Design and Methods].
- Pancake test meals; whey protein isolate added protein without changing fat or carbohydrate. Energy: 180, 330, 460 and 615 kcal. Children of 45 kg or less got 75% servings [Test meals].
- Insulin given 10 minutes before eating as a standard (normal) bolus or injection; 5 hours of fasting and sedentary activity after; blinded CGM (iPro2) [Research Design and Methods].
- Hypoglycaemia defined as capillary BGL below 3.6 mmol/L (about 65 mg/dL); treated with 15 g oral carbohydrate and analysis stopped at that point [Methods; Statistical analysis].

## Glucose curves by meal

- Peak excursion: about 4.7 (LF/LP), 4.4 (LF/HP), 4.3 (HF/LP) and 5.9 mmol/L (HF/HP) [Peak glucose excursion].
- Time to peak: about 79, 96, 126 and 143 minutes respectively; fat and protein both delay the peak [Peak glucose excursion].
- At 180 minutes, the HF/HP excursion (4.2 mmol/L) roughly equalled the sum of the HP-only and HF-only excursions [Results].
- Symptomatic hypos in the 5 hours: 14 after LF/LP, 10 after HF/LP, 4 after LF/HP, 1 after HF/HP; none severe [Hypoglycemic events].

## Practical messages from the authors

- Expect significant hyperglycaemia 3-5 hours after meals high in fat and/or protein, most marked when both are high [Conclusions].
- Extra insulin for fat should be given as an extended or split bolus rather than all upfront, to prevent early hypoglycaemia; protein also seems to need extra insulin independent of fat [Conclusions].
- They note that published fat-protein unit methods reduced highs but had hypoglycaemia rates of 33-35%, so the amount and timing of extra insulin still need research [Conclusions].
- The evening meal deserves particular attention, because no further boluses are usually given overnight [Conclusions].
- Post-meal glucose checks may be better at 3 hours than 2 hours, since even low-fat/low-protein meals had not returned to baseline by 2 hours [Conclusions].

## Limits of this summary

- Children and adolescents with good control, single breakfasts, 30 g carbohydrate; effects beyond 5 hours were not measured.
- Insulin was fixed and given as a standard bolus; the study does not test any specific extra-dose formula or bolus split.
- No AID/loop system was used; how Trio or Loop would respond is not addressed.
- Read the original for clinical decisions.
