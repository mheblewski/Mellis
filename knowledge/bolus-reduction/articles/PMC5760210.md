*Our summary (Mellis knowledge base), written in our own words. It is not the original text and not medical advice. Original: Aleppo G, Laffel LM, Ahmann AJ, Hirsch IB, Kruger DF, Peters A, Weinstock RS, Harris DR. A Practical Approach to Using Trend Arrows on the Dexcom G5 CGM System for the Management of Adults With Diabetes. J Endocr Soc 2017;1(12):1445–1460, https://doi.org/10.1210/js.2017-00388.*

## Key points

- Trend-arrow adjustment is an extra step on top of the usual calculation (ICR for carbs + correction factor/CF to target); it never replaces it [Section 2; Fig. 3].
- The Endocrine Society writing group gives fixed **unit** add-ons or deductions, chosen by the person's CF band, instead of percentages or "pretend" glucose values [Section 4; Fig. 3].
- For a **falling** arrow before a meal the deduction ranges from −0.5 U (very insulin-sensitive, CF ≥75 mg/dL/U) to −4.5 U (CF <25 mg/dL/U with ↓↓) [Fig. 3].
- With ↓↓ before a meal: consider giving insulin closer to the meal; if glucose is near or under 150 mg/dL (8.3 mmol/L), consider holding the pre-meal dose until the trend has stabilised [Fig. 3, considerations].
- Frail or older adults: make reductions at least 50% larger and increases at least 50% smaller (these modifications are not based on clinical data) [Fig. 3; Section 5B].
- Do not use the arrow table in the 4 hours after a meal; no corrective action in the first 2 hours after a meal bolus (REPLACE-BG approach) [Fig. 4; Section 4].
- If the adjusted dose comes out at zero or below, the person may eat without insulin and monitor closely, or, when the result would be negative, 15 g fast-acting carbohydrate and monitoring may suit better than the usual meal [Table 4, case H].
- No clinical trial has tested the approach; it is expert opinion funded by an unrestricted Dexcom grant [Introduction; Acknowledgments].

## Dexcom G5 trend arrows: rate of change and expected 30-minute change

Arrows reflect roughly the last 10 minutes of CGM data and are read as the expected change over the next 30 minutes; predictions beyond 20–30 minutes are less reliable. A flat arrow means "steady", not zero change [Fig. 1]. (mmol/L values below are our conversion with the paper's factor 0.0555.)

| Arrow | Meaning | Rate | Expected change in 30 min |
|---|---|---|---|
| ↑↑ | rising fast | >3 mg/dL/min (>0.17 mmol/L/min) | more than +90 mg/dL (≈ +5 mmol/L) |
| ↑ | rising | 2–3 mg/dL/min | +60 to +90 mg/dL (≈ +3.3 to +5 mmol/L) |
| ↗ | rising slowly | 1–2 mg/dL/min | +30 to +60 mg/dL (≈ +1.7 to +3.3 mmol/L) |
| → | steady | change ≤1 mg/dL/min | — |
| ↘ | falling slowly | 1–2 mg/dL/min | −30 to −60 mg/dL |
| ↓ | falling | 2–3 mg/dL/min | −60 to −90 mg/dL |
| ↓↓ | falling fast | >3 mg/dL/min | more than −90 mg/dL |
| no arrow | rate cannot be calculated | — | — |

## Adult trend-arrow adjustment table (pre-meal, and corrections ≥4 h after a meal)

Units of rapid-acting insulin added to (+) or taken from (−) the standard ICR + CF dose. Columns are CF bands in mg/dL per unit (approx. mmol/L per unit in brackets, our conversion) [Fig. 3].

| Arrow | CF <25 (<1.4) | CF 25–<50 (1.4–<2.8) | CF 50–<75 (2.8–<4.2) | CF ≥75 (≥4.2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ↑↑ | +4.5 | +3.5 | +2.5 | +1.5 |
| ↑ | +3.5 | +2.5 | +1.5 | +1.0 |
| ↗ | +2.5 | +1.5 | +1.0 | +0.5 |
| → | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| ↘ | −2.5 | −1.5 | −1.0 | −0.5 |
| ↓ | −3.5 | −2.5 | −1.5 | −1.0 |
| ↓↓ | −4.5 | −3.5 | −2.5 | −1.5 |

Further notes from the figure and text:
- MDI users with whole-unit pens can round to the nearest unit; the 0.5 U steps suit sensitive people with half-unit pens [Section 4].
- Start conservatively and see how the changes work for the individual [Fig. 3 legend].
- ↑↑ before a meal: consider bolusing 15–30 minutes before eating [Fig. 3].
- The table is built around a 30-minute horizon; against a 60-minute horizon it is conservative (example: CF 60 with ↑ adds 1.5 U) [Fig. 5].

## Bolus reduction for falling glucose before a meal: worked cases

[Table 4]
- 52-year-old on a pump, 220 mg/dL with ↓, 30 g carbs, CF 50, ICR 1:15: calculated 4.0 U, minus 1.5 U → 2.5 U (on MDI could round to 2.0 U).
- 75-year-old frail man on MDI, 240 mg/dL with ↓, 50 g carbs, CF 60: calculated 4.5 U; table says −1.5 U, extra reduction for frailty, total −2.5 U → 2.0 U.
- 38-year-old after much walking, 160 mg/dL with ↓↓, 45 g carbs, CF 45, ICR 1:15: calculated 3.7 U, minus 3.5 U → about 0 U; she eats without insulin and monitors the afternoon.

## Other published methods compared (DirecNet, Scheiner, Pettus/Edelman, Klonoff/Kerr)

[Section 3; Fig. 2] For falling arrows: DirecNet cuts the **total** dose by 10% (↘) or 20% (↓, ↓↓); Scheiner doses for current glucose minus 0 / 30 / 60 mg/dL (↘ / ↓ / ↓↓); Pettus/Edelman doses for current glucose minus 50 / 75 / 100 mg/dL. Rising arrows mirror these (DirecNet +10 / +20 / +20%; Scheiner +0 / +30 / +60 mg/dL; Pettus/Edelman +50 / +75 / +100 mg/dL). Klonoff/Kerr adds or removes 1, 1.5 or 2 U for rates of 1–2, 2–3 and >3 mg/dL/min. For every falling arrow, suspending or delaying insulin and/or taking carbohydrate may be needed depending on current glucose and hypo risk. The authors see these methods as broadly similar and safe, but complex, numeracy-dependent and lacking post-meal guidance; DirecNet's percentage scales awkwardly with big carb meals.

## Post-meal window (REPLACE-BG): stacking, hypo prevention, rescue carbs

[Fig. 4; Section 4] Uses only the CF, not the arrow table.
- First 2 h after a meal bolus: no correction (avoid insulin stacking / "chase bolusing").
- 2–4 h, 150–250 mg/dL with ↑ or ↑↑: consider a CF correction, then no further correction for 2 h.
- 2–4 h, >250 mg/dL with ↑/↑↑: confirm by fingerstick, check ketones if >300 mg/dL, correct by injection (pump users too); if still ↑↑ an hour later, confirm, correct again and change the infusion site.
- Near 150 mg/dL: ↘ → recheck CGM in 30 min; ↓ or ↓↓ → recheck in 15 min.
- Near 100 mg/dL with ↘ or ↓: consider 15 g fast-acting carbohydrate, recheck in 20 min; if <70 mg/dL and still falling, confirm by fingerstick and take another 15 g; keep checking every 15 min if it keeps dropping or does not rise. Near 100 mg/dL with ↓↓: same but 30 g.
- Pump bolus calculators account for insulin on board; MDI users without an IOB-aware calculator are generally advised to take 50% of the calculated correction in this window (case E: 2.0 U → 1.0 U).
- After 4 h, most carbohydrate is assumed absorbed and no meal IOB remains, so the table applies again (case F).
- Missed or miscalculated boluses: do not use the arrows; dose for the carbs eaten and the glucose at the meal, preferably within 2 h. Also avoid arrow adjustment after over-treating a low [Section 4].

## Exercise, sick days, acetaminophen

- Exercise: aerobic activity can cause acute and delayed lows up to 24 h; adjust conservatively before exercise, do not add insulin for up arrows during exercise, and treat impending lows with carbohydrate [Section 5C].
- Sick days: fingerstick every 2–4 h, consider corrections every 2–3 h with ketone testing; use fingerstick for decisions when >250 mg/dL with ketones or <70 mg/dL or symptoms [Section 5A].
- Acetaminophen (paracetamol) can make G5 readings falsely high for up to 6 h or longer; base all treatment on fingersticks for 6 h (case G) [Sections 1, 5A].
- Do not rely only on CGM in the first 24 h of a sensor [Section 1].

## Limits of this summary

- Adults on open-loop MDI or pump therapy with Dexcom G5; the paper does not discuss automated insulin delivery (Trio/Loop), where the algorithm already adjusts basal/SMB for the trend.
- Expert opinion; the authors state no trial has tested the table, and the frailty modifications are not data-based.
- Education checklists, patient selection, acetaminophen product list and data-sharing details are left out.
- Read the original before any clinical decision; doses must be individualised with the care team.
