# Hypoglycaemia in children and young people with diabetes (East of England CYP Diabetes Network shared guideline, hosted by Norfolk and Norwich University Hospitals)

- **Authors:** East of England Children & Young People's Diabetes Network, Shared Guidelines Group (updated by E. Nicol and J. Ashford, Cambridge University Hospitals, with PDSNs and dietitians from the region); NNUH local author V. Datta; network version June 2023, review 2026
- **Journal/year:** NHS regional clinical guideline, 2023 (NNUH Trust Docs ID 17742)
- **DOI:** none
- **URL:** https://www.nnuh.nhs.uk/publication/download/hypoglycaemia-in-children-and-young-people-with-diabetes
- **Licence:** paraphrased (text © East of England Children & Young People's Diabetes Network)
- **Saved:** summary from the NNUH PDF on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus. Education / clinical-guideline material (lower evidence than trials).

## Aims of treatment

- Treat promptly but avoid over-treatment; restore glucose above 4 mmol/L without causing hyperglycaemia.
- A high glucose 2–4 h after a hypo may not need a correction dose.
- Amount depends on weight, MDI vs pump, time since the last insulin dose and recent exercise.
- Initial treatment 0.3 g/kg of glucose-containing treatment, expected to raise glucose by 3–4 mmol/L; may need titrating to the starting level to prevent over-treatment. Sensor users may have individual plans.
- Weight table for 0.3 g/kg: 10 kg = 3 g, 20 kg = 6 g, 30 kg = 9 g, 40 kg = 12 g, 50 kg = 15 g.

## Hypo before a meal (key rule)

- Check glucose, treat the hypo and recover glucose to above 4 mmol/L.
- Then give the insulin for the meal and eat straight away — do NOT wait the usual 10–15 minutes between insulin and eating.
- Never omit the next insulin dose after a hypo; give the usual pre-meal insulin once above 4 mmol/L. Two or more hypos in 24 h may need dose adjustment.
- Pump users generally do not need complex carbohydrate after simple-sugar treatment unless they will be active; MDI users get a long-acting snack (e.g. toast, apple, biscuit, 200 mL milk) if the next meal is more than 1–2 h away.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance:** Explicit pediatric rule for a low at mealtime: treat → > 4 mmol/L → full usual meal dose → eat immediately (no pre-bolus delay). The treatment carbs are given before and separately from the meal; the guideline does not tell families to subtract them from the meal bolus or to reduce the meal bolus.
- **Population:** children and young people up to 19th birthday; MDI and pump; no AID-specific rule in the sections read.
- **Evidence level:** regional consensus guideline citing ISPAD.
