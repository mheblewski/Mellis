# van der Hoogt M. (2017) – protein and fat in an age-adjusted dinner raise insulin needs in children on pumps

Source: van der Hoogt M., "The effect of protein and fat meal content on the insulin requirement of type 1 diabetic children", MSc (Dietetics) dissertation, North-West University (supervisor Prof. M. Pieters), 2017, http://hdl.handle.net/10394/26370; summarised in Furthner et al., Nutrients 2021;13:3558 (meal ~40 g carbohydrate, 15.3 g fat, 26.6 g protein, adjusted to age).

- Design: home-based randomised crossover; 22 children aged 4–17 on insulin pumps; two dinners with the same carbohydrate, low fat/low protein vs high fat/high protein, normal bolus; CGM and finger pricks for 10 h, corrections every 2 h if needed.
- Results (summary, own words): after the high fat/high protein dinner correction insulin was about eight times higher (≈1.2 vs 0.15 U) and total meal insulin about 30% higher (≈3.5 vs 2.7 U); digestion lasted much longer (≈6 h vs 3 h) and the glucose area was larger.
- Roughly 1 U of correction per 8 g protein and per 4 g fat was observed; duration of diabetes and total daily insulin per kg changed the response.
- Why it matters: the "high fat" meal had only about 15 g fat – far less than the 30–40 g of adult studies – yet still needed more and later insulin in children. Thresholds in grams taken from adult studies are too high for small children's portions.
