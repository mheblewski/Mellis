# Treatment of insulin reactions in diabetics

- **Authors:** Brodows RG, Williams C, Amatruda JM
- **Journal/year:** JAMA 1984;252(24):3378–3381
- **DOI:** 10.1001/jama.1984.03350240024032
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6389915/
- **Licence:** paraphrased
- **Saved:** summary from the PubMed/Europe PMC abstract plus the ISPAD 2022 hypoglycaemia guideline's citations of this study on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus.

## Design

- Modified glucose clamp in insulin-dependent (type 1) diabetes: plasma glucose lowered to 55 mg/dL with steady free insulin around 50 µU/mL.
- Patients then ate 20 g carbohydrate as milk, orange juice or D-glucose, or 40 g carbohydrate as orange juice. An outpatient part tested glucose on spontaneous hypos.

## Findings (paraphrased)

- 20 g of D-glucose corrected hypoglycaemia without rebound hyperglycaemia and kept glucose up for at least 40 minutes.
- The glucose (dextrose) content of a food, not just total carbohydrate, determines the rise.
- As cited in ISPAD 2022: 40 g carbohydrate as juice gave about the same rise as 20 g glucose; whole milk with 20 g carbohydrate (435 mL) gave a minimal rise of ~1 mmol/L (18 mg/dL).
- The paper provides a table of common foods supplying 20 g of D-glucose (not reproduced).

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance:** Strongest classic evidence that carbohydrate eaten with fat/protein or as lactose/fructose (milk, juice) raises glucose much less per gram than glucose. For a meal eaten while low, the meal's carbohydrate cannot be assumed to act as glucose tablets; ISPAD therefore advises fast glucose first and avoiding fat-containing foods (chocolate) as first treatment.
- **Not verified:** the specific "10 g → +40 mg/dL" figures are NOT in this abstract; they come from Wiethop & Cryer 1993 (separate summary).
- **Population:** adults with insulin-dependent diabetes, clamp study (n not in abstract).
- **Evidence level:** small physiological study; old insulins (pre-analogue).
