*Our summary (Mellis knowledge base), written in our own words. It is not the original text and not medical advice. Original: Phillip M, Nimri R, Bergenstal RM, et al. Consensus Recommendations for the Use of Automated Insulin Delivery Technologies in Clinical Practice. Endocr Rev 2023;44(2):254–280, https://doi.org/10.1210/endrev/bnac022.*

This summary covers the training and follow-up part of the consensus. The rules on meals, hypoglycaemia, high glucose, set failure and ketones are in the companion summary (id phillip-2023-attd-aid-consensus-meals).

## Key points

- Moving to AID should be individualised. People new to CGM benefit from several days of CGM first; people new to pumps should follow MDI-to-pump protocols and consider 1–2 weeks of sensor-augmented pump (SAP) use first [Training for AID Systems].
- AID training sits on top of core diabetes skills: users must be proficient in diabetes basics, carbohydrate counting, pump and CGM use [Prioritize Comprehensive Education].
- A universal pre-AID checklist is recommended (Table 10).
- Device drop-out is highest in the first 3–6 months; plan early follow-up, ideally within 2–4 weeks of starting [Implement Universal Early Follow-Up].

## Training before and at AID start

- CGM lead-in time is for learning alarms, trend arrows and data review, which may give better starting settings; users must be able to insert and replace sensors and troubleshoot failures.
- Pump education: rotating sites, changing sets, handling occlusions. Users switching pump brands may run a few days in SAP mode to learn the interface and the bolus calculator, which may need dose changes.
- Users and carers should show they understand the system's features and troubleshooting. Training can be in person or by video call, with e-learning, videos and simulation apps; industry-certified trainers keep a role [Training for AID Systems].

## Pre-AID education checklist (Table 10)

- Core knowledge: insulin action time; blood glucose and blood ketone testing; nutrition; physical activity; treating hypoglycaemia; carbohydrate counting; psychological concerns.
- Pump basics: set changes and site rotation; connecting and disconnecting; bolus vs basal insulin; data review; sport, holidays, alcohol, menstrual cycle; infusion set failures, manual injections, ketone checks and emergency management.
- CGM basics: sensor changes and site rotation; pairing and set-up; calibration where needed; using trend line, trend arrows, alerts, sharing and downloads.
- AID before device training: the options and their burden vs benefit; keeping the basic skills above; what to expect; how closed loop differs from open-loop therapy; the need for early follow-up.

Users should also know how to check their progress in phone reports, cloud accounts or clinic AID reports.

## Early follow-up and routine assessment

- An "Initial Device Optimization" plan should: check system use, reset expectations, optimise dosing and behaviour, troubleshoot, and help the user build trust in the system — by phone with data review, video or visit [Implement Universal Early Follow-Up].
- With poor prior control, eye checks before and after starting AID may be needed.
- Clinic assessment has four parts (Table 11): a system description using the CARES framework (how it Calculates insulin, what can be Adjusted, when to Revert to standard pump settings, key Education points, Sensor and Sharing features); assessing CGM metrics (TIR, TAR, TBR, mean glucose, GMI, variability); optimising settings, including comparing AID basal with open-loop basal; guiding behaviour (bolusing, special modes, set-change frequency).

## AID report: insulin metrics

The proposed standard AID report separates automated insulin (daily amount, units per hour, automatic correction boluses and their share of TDD) from user-initiated insulin (food boluses, correction given with food, correction-only boluses) and counts user overrides per day [Figure 1, item 4].

## Limits of this summary

- All ages with type 1 diabetes; no age-specific content. Commercial AID systems only; open-source systems are outside the consensus.
- Expert consensus (education recommendations are expert opinion).
- Only the training, follow-up and report-metrics parts are covered here; read the original for clinical decisions.
