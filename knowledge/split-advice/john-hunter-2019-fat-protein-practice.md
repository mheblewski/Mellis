# Paterson M., Smith T.A., King B.R., Smart C.E. (2019) – the John Hunter Children's Hospital approach to dietary fat and protein

Source: Paterson M., Smith T.A., King B.R., Smart C.E. Managing the ups and downs from dietary protein and fat: The John Hunter Children's Hospital approach in type 1 diabetes care. Australian Diabetes Educator 2019;22(3). https://ade.adea.com.au/managing-the-ups-and-downs-from-dietary-protein-and-fat-the-john-hunter-childrens-hospital-approach-in-type-1-diabetes-care/

Clinical practice from the paediatric team behind several key trials (Smart 2013, Lopez 2014/2017/2018, Paterson 2016/2017), summarised in own words:
- No fixed gram threshold; high fat/protein meals in their studies had e.g. 35–60 g fat and 40–50 g protein; protein alone needs 75–100 g to raise glucose, but 12.5–75 g added to a carbohydrate meal has a measurable effect.
- Pumps: start with +20% on the carbohydrate bolus, increase by 10% steps up to +40% guided by glucose at 3 and 6 h; deliver as a combination (dual-wave) bolus 60/40 or 70/30 over 2–3 h (their own practice 70/30 over 2.5 h).
- Injections: the same +20% start, whole dose before the meal; up to 130% of ICR as one pre-meal injection without hypoglycaemia in preliminary data.
- Cautions: the Pańkowska equation and the food insulin index were linked with high hypoglycaemia rates; individual needs vary widely (−17% to +108% extra insulin in one study); extended boluses alone fail in the first 2 h. Adjust individually with the diabetes team, checking glucose at 3 and 6 h.
