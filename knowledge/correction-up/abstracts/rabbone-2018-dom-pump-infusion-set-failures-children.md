# Insulin pump breakdown and infusion set failure in Italian children with type 1 diabetes: a 1-year prospective observational study (4.5 failures per person-year; hyperglycaemia fixed by a set change is the most common event)

- **Authors:** Rabbone I, Minuto N, Toni S, Lombardo F, Iafusco D, Marigliano M, Schiaffini R, Maltoni G, Frongia AP, Scardapane M, Nicolucci A, Cherubini V, Bonfanti R, Scaramuzza AE; Diabetes Study Group of the Italian Society of Pediatric Endocrinology and Diabetology (ISPED)
- **Journal/year:** Diabetes, Obesity and Metabolism 2018;20(11):2551-2556
- **DOI:** 10.1111/dom.13419
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29893044/
- **Licence:** paraphrased from the abstract (publisher copyright)
- **Saved:** summary from the Europe PMC abstract on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus.

## What it is

A 1-year prospective registry of pump and infusion-set failures in 1046 children and adolescents (mean age 12.2 ± 4.1 years; diabetes from age 6.7 ± 3.6 years; pump use "from the age of 3.3 ± 2.2 years" per the abstract, which probably means about 3.3 years of pump use) from 25 Italian paediatric diabetes centres (conventional pumps, pre-AID era).

## Key findings

- Overall 4.5 failures per person-year.
- Event rates (per person-year as reported in the abstract): hyperglycaemia episodes solved by changing the infusion set 8.4; air bubbles 7; kinking 2.8; bleeding 2.4; set dislodgement 2; pump blockage 2; tunnelling 1.9; lipohypertrophy 1.8; infection 0.3.
- Preschool children (< 6 years) had more bubbles, hyperglycaemia episodes and lipohypertrophy; adolescents more tunnelling and pump blockage.
- Insulin aspart was associated with fewer bubbles and hyperglycaemia; glulisine with more lipohypertrophy and pump blockage; oblique cannulas with fewer failures of all types except infection.
- Lipohypertrophy was the only failure type associated with worse HbA1c; the authors stress continuous education.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance (moderate):** quantifies how often "unexplained" highs in children are actually set problems - hyperglycaemia resolved by a set change was the single most frequent event, and it was most frequent in preschoolers. Supports the rule that persistent unexplained hyperglycaemia in a child on a pump/AID should first trigger a site check/change (and ketone check), not repeated corrections through the same site.
- **Note on numbers:** the abstract's per-event rates add up to more than its stated average of 4.5 failures/person-year; quote the individual rates only as the abstract reports them.
- **Population:** children and adolescents on conventional pumps.
- **Evidence level:** [M] prospective observational multicentre study.
