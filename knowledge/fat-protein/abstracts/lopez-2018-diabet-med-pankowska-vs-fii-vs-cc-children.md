# A randomized comparison of three prandial insulin dosing algorithms for children and adolescents with type 1 diabetes

- **Authors:** Lopez PE, Evans M, King BR, Jones TW, Bell K, McElduff P, Davis EA, Smart CE
- **Journal/year:** Diabetic Medicine 2018;35(10):1440-1447
- **DOI:** 10.1111/dme.13703
- **PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29873107/
- **Licence:** not open access (or open-access status not confirmed). This file contains a paraphrased summary of the abstract and key numbers written for the Mellis corpus, not the publisher's text.

## Summary of the abstract (paraphrased)

- **Population:** 33 children and adolescents (two Australian paediatric centres), pumps.
- **Design:** randomised crossover; high-protein or high-fat meal with similar carbohydrate; insulin by carbohydrate counting (CC), the **Pańkowska equation (FPU)**, or the **Food Insulin Index (FII)**; 5 h fasting after the meal; 300 min CGM.
- **Glucose:** versus CC, the Pańkowska equation gave lower glucose excursion from **90 to 240 min** after the high-protein meal (p < 0.05) and a lower peak.
- **Hypoglycaemia:** risk was significantly **higher with the Pańkowska equation**: OR 0.76 for CC vs Pańkowska and 0.81 for FII vs Pańkowska.
- FII was no better than CC.
- **Conclusion:** the full Pańkowska (FPU) dose reduces late hyperglycaemia at the cost of more hypoglycaemia.

## Notes for Mellis

- Furthner 2021 (existing corpus articles/PMC8537759.md) reports that in this study the Pańkowska equation gave about 17% more insulin than CC for the high-fat meal (meal compositions: 47 g carb/27 g fat/16 g protein and 48 g carb/13 g fat/34 g protein).
- Basis for ISPAD 2022's caution that the original FPU formula raises hypoglycaemia risk and for the suggestion to use a reduced FPU factor.
