## Key points

- Targets for children under 7 after remission: HbA1c <7% (<53 mmol/mol); >50% time in tight target (TIT) 3.9–7.8 mmol/L (70–140 mg/dL) or >70% TIR 3.9–10 mmol/L (70–180 mg/dL) (B); preschoolers can safely reach HbA1c <6.5% (48 mmol/mol) [What is new; Recommendations].
- Giving meal and correction insulin **before** the meal is better than during or after it (B); about 15 minutes before the meal is advised for all toddlers, even unpredictable eaters and with faster aspart, and on hybrid closed loop [Recommendations; Bolus dosing].
- CGM is the recommended monitoring method (C); pumps preferred under 7 (E); HCL/AID should be made available for this age (A) [Recommendations].
- Mild hypo: oral glucose 0.3 g/kg raises glucose roughly 2.5–3.6 mmol/L (45–64 mg/dL), but **5–7 g carbohydrate is usually enough** in young children; avoid over-treating [Treatment of mild hypoglycemia].
- On HCL, if basal was suspended before a low, **less carbohydrate** may be needed than on a standard pump [HCL section].
- On HCL avoid **late carb boluses**: the algorithm has already raised insulin for the rise, and a late bolus on top can cause a low [HCL section].

## Bolus timing and split bolus for unpredictable eaters

[Bolus dosing]
- When intake is uncertain, a pump can split the dose: part before the meal, the rest during it; or a combination (dual-wave) bolus with the remainder over 20–40 minutes, cancelled if the child stops eating.
- Carb-count errors of up to 5–7 g are usually harmless; bigger errors show up as high or low glucose 2–3 h later and can be handled then with carbs or a small correction at least 2 h after the meal.
- Big breakfast peaks are common (morning insulin resistance); options include bolusing 10–20 minutes before breakfast, fewer or lower-GI carbs, and a very low basal for the following 3 h; pushing the ratio too hard risks a pre-lunch low.

## Insulin dose rules for small children

[Bolus dosing]
- Bolus insulin is often 60–80% of the total daily dose (TDD); basal can be only about 20–40%.
- The "500 rule" for the carb ratio usually underdoses young children; 330/TDD or 250/TDD (50–100% more insulin) may fit, and 150/TDD may be considered at breakfast; then refine from real meals.
- Correction factor: the usual 100/TDD (mmol/L) or 1800/TDD (mg/dL) often needs smaller corrections at 3–6 AM and larger ones from 9 PM to midnight.
- Active insulin time for rapid analogues is often only 2–3 h in preschoolers.
- MDI: half-unit pens/syringes (E).

## Hybrid closed loop (AID) in young children

[HCL section] Trials suggest higher TIR, especially overnight, and less parental stress. Challenges: very small doses (often well under 10 U/day), big differences across the day and between days. Families need to relearn habits (timely boluses, trusting the system) and must keep injection and fingerstick skills for device failures.

## Meal routines

[Meal-time routines] Structured meals, no grazing (C), meals limited to about 20 minutes, no force-feeding, one small snack between meals (7–15 g carbohydrate with insulin). Fear of lows can lead to post-meal bolusing and under-bolusing, which become habits and cause highs; lows from refused food are usually mild.

## Hypoglycaemia treatment in infants and preschoolers

[Hypoglycemia; Treatment of mild hypoglycemia] Preferred: glucose tablets, gel or drink that the child accepts quickly. Infants: gel, diluted juice or glucose polymer by spoon or bottle; no honey under 1 year. Fat-containing foods (milk, chocolate) slow the rise; sweets are not routine because of caries. Severe hypos in under-7s are now rare (≤3% per year in registries); CGM with predictive alerts, suspend-before-low and HCL help; caregivers should learn to act on downward arrows. Fear of hypoglycaemia, more than actual frequency, relates to higher HbA1c.
