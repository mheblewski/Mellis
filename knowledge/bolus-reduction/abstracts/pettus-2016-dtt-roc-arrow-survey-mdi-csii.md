# Use of Glucose Rate of Change Arrows to Adjust Insulin Therapy Among Individuals with Type 1 Diabetes Who Use Continuous Glucose Monitoring

- **Authors:** Pettus J, Edelman SV
- **Journal/year:** Diabetes Technology & Therapeutics 2016;18(Suppl 2):S2-34–S2-42
- **DOI:** 10.1089/dia.2015.0369
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4717500/
- **Licence:** paraphrased (Copyright 2016 Mary Ann Liebert, Inc.; free to read in PMC, no open licence stated)
- **Saved:** summary from the PMC full text on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus.

## Design

- Online survey (May–August 2013), 70 questions, of 222 adults with T1D using Dexcom real-time CGM ≥6 days/week: 166 pump (CSII) and 56 MDI users. Mean age about 45–48 y, diabetes duration about 20–22 y, self-reported HbA1c 6.8%. Recruited via US endocrinology practices; descriptive, self-reported.
- Scenarios: (A) correction 4 h after last insulin/food, CGM 220 mg/dL, target 120 mg/dL, with flat, one or two arrows up/down. (B) Meal of 50 g carbohydrate at CGM 110 mg/dL with two arrows up or two arrows down.

## Results relevant to falling glucose

| Scenario | CSII | MDI | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Correction at 220 mg/dL, ↓ (falling 2–3 mg/dL/min) | −37% | −50% | 0.024 |
| Correction at 220 mg/dL, ↓↓ (falling >3 mg/dL/min) | −38% | −52% | 0.034 |
| Meal (50 g) at 110 mg/dL, ↓↓ | −44% | −55% | 0.019 |
| Correction ↑ / ↑↑ | +108% / +138% | +120% / +146% | 0.13 / 0.72 |
| Meal ↑↑ | +77% | +95% | 0.013 |

- About 58–61% adjusted the timing of the meal bolus by the arrow (delaying the meal after insulin when rising).
- The authors suggest pump users cut less because they can also lower or suspend basal or give several small boluses; MDI users may make bigger mealtime changes to avoid extra injections later.
- Self-reported effects of CGM: most reported fewer/less severe lows and lower HbA1c.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance to reducing a meal bolus when glucose is falling / a low is predicted:** real-world behaviour benchmark, not a rule. Experienced adult CGM users cut a 50 g meal bolus by about **44% (pump) to 55% (MDI)** when glucose was 110 mg/dL with two arrows down, i.e., much more than the 10–20% DirecNet rule. They did not skip it on average. Useful as context for how large a "reduce, not skip" adjustment people actually make.
- **AID:** none (2013, pre-AID; pump users could suspend basal manually).
- **Population:** adults with T1D, well controlled, highly educated; Dexcom only.
- **Evidence level:** self-report survey, descriptive; Dexcom funded the manuscript; both authors consult for Dexcom.
