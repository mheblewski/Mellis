*Our summary (Mellis knowledge base), written in our own words. It is not the original text and not medical advice. Original: Pettus J, Edelman SV. Recommendations for Using Real-Time Continuous Glucose Monitoring (rtCGM) Data for Insulin Adjustments in Type 1 Diabetes. J Diabetes Sci Technol 2017;11(1):138–147, https://doi.org/10.1177/1932296816663747.*

## Key points

- Dose for the glucose **expected in 30 minutes**, not the current value: add or subtract 50 / 75 / 100 mg/dL (≈2.8 / 4.2 / 5.6 mmol/L) for one-step, two-step and fast arrows, then use the usual ICR and correction factor [Utility of ROC Arrows; Figure 3].
- For down arrows the most conservative option is to **delay insulin until the arrow turns flat** [Figure 3].
- Pre-meal glucose at target but falling (110 mg/dL ↘, expected ~60): eat first, watch the trend, and bolus only if glucose rises or levels off above target [Clinical Scenarios].
- 30 minutes was chosen because rapid-acting analogues need about that long to act meaningfully [Utility of ROC Arrows].
- Avoid stacking: a correction within 2 h of the last bolus raises hypo risk; rapid analogues can take 90–120 min to peak and may still act 3–5 h after the dose [Utility of ROC Arrows; Table 2].
- Arrows average the last 20–30 minutes and lag when the direction changes (e.g., after treating a low) [Limitations].
- Based on the authors' experience and a survey of 222 successful users; no trial [Introduction; Discussion].

## Trend arrows (ROC) on different CGMs

[Figure 2] Dexcom G4/G5: → change ≤1 mg/dL/min; ↗/↘ 1–2 mg/dL/min (0.06–0.11 mmol/L/min); ↑/↓ 2–3 mg/dL/min (0.11–0.17); ↑↑/↓↓ >3 mg/dL/min (>0.17). Abbott Navigator II uses per-hour bands: flat <60 mg/dL/h, angled 60–120 mg/dL/h (3.3–6.7 mmol/L/h), vertical >120 mg/dL/h; no double arrows. Medtronic 530G shows only ↑/↓ (1–2 mg/dL/min) and ↑↑/↓↓ (≥3 mg/dL/min). Arrow meanings are not standardised, so users switching devices must relearn them [Conceptual Shift].

## Adjusted glucose for dosing (Pettus/Edelman method)

[Figure 3] Values are what to add to or subtract from the current CGM value before calculating the dose; the actual expected 30-minute change is in brackets.

| Arrow | Add / subtract (mg/dL) | Expected change in 30 min (mg/dL) |
|---|---|---|
| ↑↑ | +100 | +90 to +150 |
| ↑ | +75 | +60 to +90 |
| ↗ | +50 | +30 to +60 |
| → | 0 (dose for current value) | — |
| ↘ | −50 | −30 to −60 |
| ↓ | −75 | −60 to −90 |
| ↓↓ | −100 | −90 to −150 |

The lower end of the range is the conservative choice; e.g., 120 mg/dL ↑ can be dosed as 180 mg/dL, or up to 210 mg/dL depending on other factors [Utility of ROC Arrows]. Things to weigh: variable insulin onset/peak/duration, meal composition and size, prior and planned exercise, glucose-raising drugs, stress and illness [Table 1].

## Meal bolus with falling or rising glucose: scenarios

Settings used: target 110 mg/dL, ICR 1:15 g, CF 1:50 mg/dL, meal 45 g [Clinical Scenarios].
- **110 mg/dL ↗**: expected 160; 3.0 U carbs + 1.0 U correction = 4 U (even the largest adjustment would add only 1.2 U). Bolusing about 20 minutes before eating is advised; with faster rises (↑ or ↑↑) some patients bolus and **wait for the bend** in the CGM curve before eating, which may avoid later corrections and stacking.
- **110 mg/dL ↘**: expected 60; the 3.0 U meal dose could take the person low, so eat first and monitor. Insulin may or may not be needed depending on meal size and composition, insulin on board, current glucose and rate of fall; give it only if glucose rises or stabilises above target.
- **220 mg/dL ↑**: dose for 295 → 3.7 U instead of 2.2 U (about +70%); surveyed users reported an average +110%. Very high glucose (e.g., >250–300 mg/dL) may need a stronger CF (example 1:35 instead of 1:50).
- **220 mg/dL ↓**: expected 145; many would wait for the arrow to level off, correct if it settles above goal, and act against a low if it does not level off.

## Survey of rtCGM users

Among 222 users with mean HbA1c 6.9% and little hypoglycaemia, adjustments for arrows were much larger than the 10–20% used in trials: at 220 mg/dL they raised a correction by a mean 140% for ↑↑ and cut it by 42% for ↓↓, so doses for the same glucose ranged 1.6–6.7 U [Evidence for Efficacy]. The authors' anticipated-glucose method is deliberately more conservative than this [Discussion].

## Practical cautions

- After exercise, people are more insulin-sensitive; dose conservatively [Practical Considerations].
- High-fat/protein meals cause delayed rises; the method fits carbohydrate meals best [Practical Considerations].
- Active insulin lasts about 3–5 h; reduce corrections given in that window [Practical Considerations].
- Acetaminophen (paracetamol) falsely raises readings and arrows; check by fingerstick when symptoms do not match the CGM; do not use arrows when the trend graph is turning; new users should first gain confidence in the sensor [Limitations].
- Treat lows promptly without over-reacting; carbohydrate effect may take more than 15 minutes to show [Table 2].

## Limits of this summary

- Adults with type 1 diabetes on MDI or pump with Dexcom G4/G5; automated insulin delivery is not discussed.
- Expert opinion of two endocrinologists living with T1D plus a self-report survey; not a trial.
- Display screenshots and background on SMBG are left out.
- Read the original and individualise with a clinician before use.
