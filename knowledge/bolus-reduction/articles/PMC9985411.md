*Our summary (Mellis knowledge base), written in our own words. It is not the original text and not medical advice. Original: Phillip M, Nimri R, Bergenstal RM, et al. Consensus Recommendations for the Use of Automated Insulin Delivery Technologies in Clinical Practice. Endocr Rev 2023;44(2):254–280, https://doi.org/10.1210/endrev/bnac022.*

This summary covers the parts of the consensus on commercial AID systems, target populations, starting and setting up AID, training (Tables 1, 6–9) and day-to-day clinical use (Tables 12–13). A companion summary (id phillip-2023-attd-aid-consensus-training) covers the education checklist and follow-up.

## Key points

- AID raises time in range with no rise, or a fall, in hypoglycaemia; it should be considered for everyone with type 1 diabetes [Key points box].
- Meal boluses are still the user's job: give them usually 10–20 min before eating, except with incipient hypoglycaemia, gastroparesis or a high-protein/fat meal; a bolus given during or after the meal can stack with the algorithm's own extra insulin and cause a low [Table 12].
- Missed or late meal bolus: consider half the bolus 30–60 min after the meal start; after more than 60 min give only a system-recommended correction based on the rise [Table 12; Table 9].
- Hypoglycaemia on AID (> 15 min below 70 mg/dL, 3.9 mmol/L): start with 5–10 g carbohydrate (except around exercise or after a clearly over-sized meal bolus) and wait 15 min before treating again [Table 12]. Users should expect to need fewer carbs than usual, as the system has often already cut or stopped insulin [Table 9].
- Do not "trick the system": fictitious carbohydrates, overriding the calculator, post-meal boluses, manual corrections or insulin outside the system reduce performance and raise hypo risk, unless the user has been taught a specific exception (e.g. prolonged exercise after a meal) [Table 9; Clinical recommendations].
- Suspect infusion set failure when glucose stays above 250 mg/dL (13.9 mmol/L) and/or does not fall by at least 50 mg/dL within 1 h of a correction bolus [Table 9].
- Sustained high glucose: meter check, ketones, set change; a correction by pen/syringe may work better because IOB can be falsely high after failed pump boluses; after an injection turn AID off for 2–4 h so IOB stays accurate [Table 12].
- Elevated ketones, especially with normal glucose: consider stopping AID and using open-loop sick-day rules [Table 12].
- No separate CGM targets for AID users are recommended at present [Table 12].

| System | Algorithm target | Automatic correction boluses | Exercise / activity option |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medtronic 670G/770G | fixed 120 mg/dL (6.7 mmol/L) | none; manual corrections aim at 150 mg/dL using algorithm parameters | temp target 150 mg/dL (8.3 mmol/L) |
| Medtronic 780G | 100 mg/dL (5.6) default; 110 or 120 mg/dL | yes, toward 120 mg/dL once auto basal is at maximum; not based on programmed ISF | temp target 150 mg/dL |
| CamAPS FX | 104 mg/dL (5.8) default; 80–200 mg/dL (4.4–11.0) | via stronger basal changes; optional "Boost"; manual corrections optional with programmed ISF | "Ease off" |
| Diabeloop | 110 mg/dL (6.1) default; 100–130 mg/dL; "Zen" mode +20–40 mg/dL | yes | activity mode |
| Tandem Control-IQ | range 112.5–160 mg/dL (6.2–8.9); sleep 112.5–120 | 60% of the computed correction if glucose is predicted above 180 mg/dL (10.0), aiming at 110 mg/dL | activity range 140–160 mg/dL |
| Omnipod 5 | 110–150 mg/dL (6.1–8.3), 10 mg/dL steps | via stronger basal changes; manual optional | activity target 150 mg/dL |

Safety limits differ by system (e.g. maximum hourly or 4–7 h basal, maximum 2 h or 24 h delivery, minimum delivery for 1.5–6 h, maximum bolus). Approved ages ranged from 1 year (CamAPS FX, including pregnancy) to 2, 6 or 7 years upward; Diabeloop 12–18 (DBL4T) or > 18 years. All listed systems use rapid-acting insulin; CamAPS FX also ultra-rapid [Table 1].

## Who should be offered AID (Table 6, ADA grades)

- Strongly consider for all with type 1 diabetes: school-age children 7–14 years (A), adolescents and adults (A).
- Consider: adults over 65 (B), pre-school children under 7 (B), people with moderate/severe hypoglycaemia or unawareness (C), pregnancy with type 1 diabetes (C), chronic renal failure or gastroparesis (C).
- Other insulin-treated diabetes: type 2 (C), cystic fibrosis-related (C), after pancreatectomy (E). Supervised AID in hospital if not contraindicated (E).
- Pregnancy needs lower targets than most systems allow. Candidates are assessed for meal-dosing skills, motivation, willingness to train, dexterity/vision and funding.

## Starting and setting up AID (Tables 7–8)

- Users and carers should show competence in intensive insulin therapy first. People new to technology may start a pump or CGM first; CGM for 1–2 weeks before AID may help, and MDI users may start the pump 1–2 weeks earlier with training on set problems and early ketosis/DKA.
- Existing pump settings can be kept if control is acceptable, but review ICR, corrections, basal and basal share of TDD, and carbohydrate intake.
- Choose settings from recent CGM data; with poor control use the highest available target for the first weeks; err on the conservative side to avoid lows.
- Set realistic expectations: users still handle meal boluses and their timing, sensor and pump care, exercise adjustments, and manual insulin during CGM warm-up or lost connection.
- Long diabetes duration or poor control: possible transient retinopathy worsening (eye check first), temporary neuropathic pain, insulin oedema, rise in microalbuminuria.
- Watch adjunct drugs (e.g. SGLT inhibitors).

System-specific starting points [Table 8]:
- Medtronic 670G/770G/780G: needs TDD from 3–7 days; reduce manual-mode basal by 10% if it exceeds 50% of TDD; strengthen ICR by 10%; active insulin time 2–4 h (shorter is better); 780G target 100 or 120 mg/dL, 120 initially for high A1c, children and older adults; autocorrection every 5 min (780G only); TDD adapts daily with fading memory over 6 days.
- Control-IQ: weight, TDD, basal, ICR, CF; basal about 50% of TDD; sleep target 112.5–120 mg/dL, best extended past the usual breakfast bolus; autocorrection can occur 1 h after a bolus at 60% toward 110 mg/dL; exercise mode 140–160 mg/dL, basal stops at 80 mg/dL (4.4 mmol/L), must be switched off by hand.
- CamAPS FX: weight and TDD; basal about 50% of TDD; maximum bolus 50% of TDD; daily maximum 3 × TDD on Dana pumps; extended bolus feature on with Dana pumps; "Ease off" raises target by 40 mg/dL (2.2 mmol/L) for 10 min to 24 h; adapts continuously, independent of pump settings.
- Omnipod 5: basal, ICR, CF, active insulin time; reduce basal by 10% if > 50% of TDD; targets 110–150 mg/dL in up to 8 segments; adapts at each pod change (first pod constrained; TDD estimated as basal × 2); "HypoProtect" target 150 mg/dL with all delivery at 50%, for 1–72 h; active insulin time applies only to user boluses.
- Reverse correction below target: CamAPS FX yes; Omnipod 5 yes (can be turned off); Control-IQ no; 780G uses a "safe bolus". Extended bolus only on Control-IQ (up to 2 h). CGM trend used in the bolus calculator only by Omnipod 5.

## Pre-bolus and meal boluses on AID (Table 12)

- AID raises its own insulin once glucose starts rising after a meal, so timing the user bolus before the carbohydrate matters; late boluses risk stacking and hypoglycaemia.
- Usual pre-bolus 10–20 min; not when hypoglycaemia is imminent, with gastroparesis or a high-protein/fat meal. Faster insulins may shorten the gap; adjunct drugs and meal composition can change timing.
- Check ICR after starting AID by looking at the post-meal rise, aiming for less than 60 mg/dL (3.3 mmol/L) above pre-meal. Some systems work better with an ICR about 10–20% stronger than open-loop settings.
- Extended boluses have limited value on most AID systems, because the algorithm will add insulin for the prolonged rise after, for example, high-fat meals.
- Low-carbohydrate intake may help adults on AID.
- Users should bolus accurately for all meals and snacks [Table 9].

## Hypoglycaemia treatment on AID

- A hypo event is more than 15 min below 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L). Start with 5–10 g carbohydrate; exceptions are exercise-related lows and lows after a markedly over-estimated meal bolus. Wait 15 min before re-treating to avoid swings [Table 12].
- Look at CGM arrows, trend and IOB when deciding how much; the system may already have reduced or suspended insulin [Table 9].
- Alerts: start with low alerts only at 65–70 mg/dL (3.6–3.9 mmol/L); add high alerts later at a high threshold of 250–300 mg/dL (13.8–16.7 mmol/L); plan responses to alerts to avoid alarm fatigue [Table 12].

## High glucose, ketones and set failure

- Signs of set failure: glucose stays above 250 mg/dL and/or falls less than 50 mg/dL within 1 h after a correction bolus [Table 9].
- Sustained hyperglycaemia: check with a meter, check ketones, change the set. DKA remains a risk with any pump; a user correction may be needed and may be more effective by pen/syringe. After an injected correction, AID off for 2–4 h [Table 12].
- Keep self-management skills: meter and ketone checks, giving an injection, knowing default basal settings, calculating a correction if the system fails, spotting emergencies, carrying supplies [Table 9].
- Disconnecting for more than 15–30 min: suspend insulin so the algorithm does not count insulin that was not delivered [Table 9].

## Exercise on AID (Tables 12–13)

- Set a higher (temporary) target ideally 1–2 h before, especially for long aerobic exercise; it may be kept afterwards if a later low is a worry.
- Avoid exercising at the peak of bolus insulin: the pre-meal bolus can be cut by about 25–75% if prolonged exercise is planned within 3 h of the meal. Table 13 gives 0–25% bolus reduction 1–3 h before (up to 75% for prolonged exercise) and up to 50% at the post-exercise meal.
- Avoid carbs 15–60 min before exercise unless glucose is heading low, because AID will respond with more insulin. If glucose is below 120 mg/dL (6.7 mmol/L), 15 g about 10 min before (Table 12) or 10–20 g at the start or 10 min before (Table 13) may be advised, especially for moderate exercise. Carbs during long exercise may be taken without entering them; with AID suspended or in open loop, carbs can be taken freely.
- Basal: exercise target 1–2 h before, during (or suspend, confirming suspension) and for 0–6 h after aerobic exercise. Anaerobic exercise may need no insulin change; afterwards reduce bolus or cancel the exercise target. Overnight: exercise target up to 6 h and/or an uncovered bedtime snack.
- If the pump is removed during exercise, suspend delivery.

## Sick days, procedures, open loop

- Consider leaving AID for open-loop sick-day plans (monitor ketones, raise insulin), especially with elevated ketones and normal glucose [Table 12].
- Open loop may also be chosen for ketones, steroid bursts, sports competitions, pregnancy or altered mental state [Table 9].
- Small procedures (e.g. gastroscopy): AID can run with a temporary target or hypo-protect mode.
- Keep open-loop settings up to date; use a more conservative ICR when off AID for long, since ICR is often strengthened for AID; have an MDI plan for pump failure.
- Rapid-acting insulin is recommended; ultra-rapid may be considered for people who often miss boluses or bolus right before eating, if approved for the system.

## Limits of this summary

- Covers commercial AID systems available around 2021–2022; open-source systems (Loop, Trio, AndroidAPS, OpenAPS) are outside the consensus, and device details may have changed.
- Expert consensus; most practical rules are expert opinion, and only Table 6 carries ADA evidence grades. All ages, but no child-specific meal or hypo amounts.
- Other parts of the article (evidence review, payer criteria, AID report, psychosocial aspects) are not covered here.
- Read the original before clinical decisions.
