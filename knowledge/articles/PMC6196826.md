*Our summary (Mellis knowledge base), written in our own words. It is not the original text and not medical advice. Original: Bergenstal RM, Beck RW, Close KL, et al. Glucose Management Indicator (GMI): a new term for estimating A1C from continuous glucose monitoring. Diabetes Care 2018;41(11):2275-2280, https://doi.org/10.2337/dc18-1581.*

## Key points

- GMI replaces the older name "estimated A1C" (eA1C). It is calculated from CGM mean glucose and expressed in the same units as A1C, but the new name avoids implying it should match a lab A1C [Introduction; Why Was the Term GMI Selected?].
- Formula: GMI (%) = 3.31 + 0.02392 × mean glucose in mg/dL; GMI (mmol/mol) = 12.71 + 4.70587 × mean glucose in mmol/L. It comes from pooled Dexcom trial data (N = 528) [How Is GMI Calculated?; Fig. 1].
- Use at least 10, preferably 14 or more, days of CGM data [How Is GMI Calculated?; Introduction].
- Rule of thumb: each 25 mg/dL (about 1.4 mmol/L) higher mean glucose adds about 0.6% to GMI [How Is GMI Calculated?].
- GMI and lab A1C often differ: they were identical in 19% of people, differed by 0.3 points or more in 51% and by 0.5 or more in 28%. A person's gap tends to stay stable over time [Table 3].

## GMI for a given CGM mean glucose [Table 1]

- mg/dL to GMI %: 100 → 5.7; 125 → 6.3; 150 → 6.9; 175 → 7.5; 200 → 8.1; 225 → 8.7; 250 → 9.3; 275 → 9.9; 300 → 10.5; 350 → 11.7.
- mmol/L to GMI mmol/mol: 5 → 36.2; 6 → 40.9; 7 → 45.7; 8 → 50.4; 9 → 55.1; 10 → 59.8; 12 → 69.2; 14 → 78.6; 16 → 88.0; 18 → 97.4.

## Why GMI and lab A1C disagree

- CGM measures interstitial glucose every few minutes; A1C reflects glucose bound to haemoglobin over red blood cell life (about 120 days). Red cell lifespan and glycation rate differ between people, and conditions such as haemoglobinopathies or haemolytic anaemia change A1C [Table 2].
- Short-term changes widen the gap: illness, steroids or DKA raise GMI above a simultaneous A1C; a new diet, intense exercise or a newly effective medicine can push GMI below it [How Can GMI Be Explained…].
- If GMI is always clearly below the lab A1C, do not set the A1C goal too low and check that time in hypoglycaemia is not excessive; if it is always above, a slightly lower A1C goal may be safe [How Can GMI Be Explained…].

## GMI among the core CGM metrics

- The authors list mean glucose, GMI, time in range 70-180 mg/dL (3.9-10.0 mmol/L), time below 70 and 54 mg/dL (3.9 and 3.0 mmol/L), time above 180 and 250 mg/dL (10.0 and 13.9 mmol/L) and coefficient of variation. Hypoglycaemia time and variability add safety information that A1C cannot show [Why Is It Important to Use Both…].
- GMI can show a trend within 2-4 weeks after a therapy change [Why Is It Important to Use Both…].

## Limits of this summary

- All data came from Dexcom sensors in four randomized trials (type 1 and type 2 diabetes); the authors say the formula may not be exactly the same for other sensors.
- This is a Perspective proposing a term and a formula, not a guideline; patient-explanation wording in Table 2 is condensed here.
- Read the original before using GMI to set individual A1C targets.
