# A Clinical Training Program for Hybrid Closed Loop Therapy in a Pediatric Diabetes Clinic (Barbara Davis Center quality-improvement programme, 670G)

- **Authors:** Berget C, Thomas SE, Messer LH, Thivener K, Slover RH, Wadwa RP, Alonso GT
- **Journal/year:** Journal of Diabetes Science and Technology 2020;14(2):290-296 (online 2019)
- **DOI:** 10.1177/1932296819835183
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7196862/
- **Licence:** paraphrased (© 2019 Diabetes Technology Society; free to read on PMC, not openly licensed; short quotes only)
- **Saved:** summary from the PMC full text on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus.

## What it is

A Plan-Do-Study-Act quality-improvement project at the Barbara Davis Center (Colorado) that built a hybrid closed-loop training programme for 72 children and adolescents already using pumps, starting the Medtronic 670G: (1) an in-person group class refreshing pump and CGM use, (2) a live video class on HCL use, and (3) three follow-up phone calls in the first 4 weeks.

## Points relevant to high glucose and corrections

- **Curriculum:** the HCL class taught how to bolus for meals, treat hypoglycaemia and "give high BG corrections" in auto mode, how to respond to alerts, and why the system exits automation. The 670G exits to manual mode for prolonged hyperglycaemia (sensor glucose > 250 mg/dL for 3 hours or > 300 mg/dL for 1 hour), or after 90 min of "Safe Basal" when delivery has been at minimum or maximum for 1-3 h or CGM data are missing.
- **Results:** in the first 4 weeks sensor wear was 83 % and time in HCL 72 %; time 70-180 mg/dL rose from 49 % to 62 % (P < .001); there were 5.8 HCL exits per week, the top reasons being "high sensor glucose" (1.98/week), "maximum insulin delivery" (0.79/week) and "BG required" (0.63/week).
- **Follow-up calls:** carb ratios were strengthened in 75 % of patients (mean +24 %); active insulin time was changed in 44 % (80 % of changes shortened it); the most common re-education topics were pre-meal bolusing (65 %), **instruction on correction boluses for high blood glucose (48 %)** and responding to alerts to prevent exits (45 %).
- **Lesson:** prolonged high glucose was the main barrier to staying in automation; close follow-up in the first weeks was needed to re-teach corrections and bolus behaviour.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance (moderate):** shows that, in a large paediatric centre, families needed repeated teaching on how to correct highs within the system (about half of families in the first month) and that most problems were behavioural (late or missed meal boluses, weak carb ratios) rather than the algorithm. It is a 670G-era programme (no automatic correction boluses), so its correction teaching is not directly transferable to systems with automatic corrections such as Trio (SMB).
- **Population:** children and adolescents (paediatric clinic), previous pump users.
- **AID systems:** Medtronic 670G.
- **Evidence level:** [Ed] quality-improvement / education programme report (single centre, no control group).
