# Bolus Calculator Settings in Well-Controlled Prepubertal Children Using Insulin Pumps Are Characterized by Low Insulin to Carbohydrate Ratios and Short Duration of Insulin Action Time (DIA 2-3 h in pump calculators; night correction factor about 40 % weaker than daytime)

- **Authors:** Hanas R, Adolfsson P
- **Journal/year:** Journal of Diabetes Science and Technology 2017;11(2):247-252 (online 2016)
- **DOI:** 10.1177/1932296816661348
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5478012/
- **Licence:** paraphrased (© 2016 Diabetes Technology Society; free to read on PMC, not openly licensed; short quotes only)
- **Saved:** summary from the PMC full text on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus.

## What it is

A retrospective analysis from two Swedish paediatric clinics of pump bolus-calculator settings that families and the team had titrated over 6-12 months in 21 well-controlled prepubertal children (age 7.0 ± 2.3 years, range 2-10; HbA1c 7.0 ± 0.5 %, 53 mmol/mol; no severe hypoglycaemia since diagnosis; TDD 0.7 ± 0.1 U/kg/day; basal 38 ± 11 %). Conventional pumps (Animas Vibe, Medtronic), not AID.

## Key findings

- **Duration of insulin action (DIA):** the clinics started at 4 h but moved to 3 h because families found the calculator gave too little insulin when IOB was present; titrated DIA was 2.6 ± 0.5 h (range 2-3 h); target glucose 5.3 ± 0.4 mmol/L.
- **Why short DIA "worked":** the pumps subtracted IOB only from correction boluses, not from meal boluses, and counted IOB from both meal and correction boluses; a short DIA thus compensated for how these particular calculators handled IOB. The authors note that pharmacodynamic studies show a 0.2 U/kg aspart dose acting about 4 h in pubertal and 5 h in prepubertal children, i.e. longer than the settings chosen.
- **Correction factor ("rule" = ISF x TDD, mmol/L):** median 113 in the morning (about 2034 in mg/dL terms), 120 during the rest of the day and **200 during the night** (about 3600 in mg/dL terms). Children needed **less correction insulin than the 100 rule** predicts, and at night roughly 40 % less than in the daytime.
- **Carb ratio ("rule" = ICR x TDD):** median 211 at breakfast and 435 for other meals - young children need more meal insulin than the 500 rule gives, especially at breakfast.
- **Conclusion:** prepubertal children "seem to need more bolus insulin for meals than calculated from the 500 rule, especially at breakfast, but less insulin for corrections than calculated from the 100 rule"; 2-3 h DIA worked in these calculators; 30- or 15-min DIA steps would help.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance (moderate, paediatric night corrections):** the most concrete paediatric data that correction doses in young children should be weaker at night (night correction factor about 1.7 times the daytime value in mmol/L per unit) and weaker overall than adult rules suggest. Useful as a caution against adding full daytime-strength corrections to an evening/night meal card in a child.
- **Caution on DIA:** the short 2-3 h DIA reflects old pump calculators that ignored meal IOB in meal boluses; it is not a pharmacodynamic DIA and does not transfer to oref/Trio, whose IOB model uses a long exponential curve (Trio docs: 10 h). Walsh 2014 argues the opposite (4.5-6.5 h) for calculators that subtract all IOB.
- **Population:** prepubertal children 2-10 years on conventional pumps.
- **Evidence level:** [M] small retrospective observational study (n = 21), no control group.
