# Effective treatment of hypoglycemia in children with type 1 diabetes: a randomized controlled clinical trial

- **Authors:** McTavish L, Wiltshire E
- **Journal/year:** Pediatric Diabetes 2011;12(4 Pt 2):381–387
- **DOI:** 10.1111/j.1399-5448.2010.00725.x
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21443586/
- **Licence:** paraphrased
- **Saved:** summary from the PubMed/Europe PMC abstract and the ISPAD 2022 hypoglycaemia guideline's description of this trial on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus.

## Question

Which of four oral treatments works best for hypoglycaemia in children with T1D when every treatment is dosed by body weight (0.3 g carbohydrate per kg)?

## Design

- Randomized trial at a diabetes camp (New Zealand). Each hypo episode was randomly assigned (sealed envelope) to one of: glucose tablets, jellybeans, orange juice, or sugar mints (Mentos).
- Dose for every product: 0.3 g carbohydrate/kg body weight, pre-calculated per child.
- Glucose measured at 0, 2, 5, 10 and 15 min; symptoms recorded.

## Results

- 39 children, 191 hypo episodes (1–12 per child; 2 excluded for protocol violation): 52 glucose tablets, 45 jellybeans, 44 juice, 48 mints.
- The change in glucose at 10 and 15 min and the glucose at 15 min differed between groups; jellybeans gave the lowest and slowest rise and tended to need a repeat dose more often (p = 0.058).
- Glucose tablets, juice and mints did not differ significantly.
- Symptoms were present in 112 episodes; median time to symptom resolution 12 min (IQR 8–15).
- As described in ISPAD 2022 (Abraham et al., section on treatment of hypoglycaemia): 0.3 g/kg of a fast-acting product raised median glucose by about 1–1.3 mmol/L at 10 min and 2–2.1 mmol/L at 15 min, without rebound hyperglycaemia at the next meal.

## Authors' conclusion (paraphrased)

0.3 g/kg of fast carbohydrate (not jellybeans) resolved hypoglycaemia in most children; normalising glucose often took about 15 minutes.

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance:** Basis of the ISPAD weight-based dose (0.3 g/kg; 9 g for a 30 kg child, 15 g for 50 kg). Gives the observed early rise per dose: ~2 mmol/L (~36–38 mg/dL) at 15 min after 0.3 g/kg, i.e. roughly 6.7–7 mmol/L per g/kg at 15 min. This is the rise at 15 min, not the peak (the ISPAD expected total rise is 3–4 mmol/L).
- **For meals eaten while low:** only fast sugars were tested; no meal or mixed food arm. Jellybeans (glucose syrup in a gel matrix) were already slower — supports treating with a fast sugar first rather than relying on meal carbs.
- **Population:** children with T1D at camp (exact ages and regimen in the full text, not verified here); pre-AID era (injections/pumps).
- **Evidence level:** randomized (per episode) controlled trial, single camp, moderate size.
