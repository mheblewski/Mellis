# Bolus Calculators

- **Authors:** Schmidt S, Nørgaard K
- **Journal/year:** Journal of Diabetes Science and Technology 2014;8(5):1035–1041
- **DOI:** 10.1177/1932296814532906
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4455388/ (PMID 24876436)
- **Licence:** © 2014 Diabetes Technology Society (free to read in PMC, not under an open licence) → paraphrased summary with short quotes.
- **Saved:** summary from the PMC full text on 2026-10-21 for the Mellis RAG corpus.

## What the review covers

Narrative review of bolus calculators (BCs) — stand-alone devices/paper tools, smartphone apps and pump-integrated calculators — and the trials of their use (mostly HbA1c, glycaemic variability and treatment satisfaction).

## The generic formula

- Meal insulin = carbohydrate ÷ insulin-to-carb ratio; correction insulin = (current BG − target) ÷ correction factor.
- The sum may be multiplied by a factor for the current physiological state (illness, exercise, menstruation…), which the authors note has no research-based quantification and is set by trial and error.
- Then active insulin is removed: "if insulin from previously administered boluses is still active, insulin on board (IOB) should be subtracted."
- The target is a single patient-specific value that may vary by time of day. The review does not discuss target ranges, negative ("reverse") corrections below target, or CGM-trend adjustments.
- Pump calculators "differ subtly" in how they handle IOB and hyperglycaemia correction; clinicians should know each brand's logic. The authors point out that "no advanced BC subtracts excess IOB from a meal bolus when the current BG is above the BG target", i.e. in pumps IOB typically only cancels the correction, which departs from the formula above and risks overdosing.

## Evidence summarised (selected)

| Study | Design | Key result |
|---|---|---|
| Gross 2003 (MiniMed calculator on PDA) | 1-week randomised crossover, CSII, n = 49 | fewer correction boluses and less supplemental carbohydrate for lows with the calculator |
| Garg (PDA calculator) | 12-month RCT, MDI | lower HbA1c but more severe hypoglycaemia; the device had no IOB function |
| Schmidt (BolusCal) | 16-week RCT pilot, n = 51 | HbA1c 8.9% → 8.1%, not different from the mental-calculation arm; better treatment satisfaction |
| Ziegler (ABACUS) | 26-week RCT, n = 193 | HbA1c fall > 0.5% in 56.0% vs 34.4% |
| Cukierman-Yaffe | cross-sectional, n = 88 pump users | using the calculator for ≥ 50% of boluses: HbA1c 0.6% lower, glucose SD 9.1 mg/dL lower |
| Enander | 12-month RCT, children on pumps, n = 45 | no extra benefit on HbA1c or postprandial glucose |

Only one of the reviewed studies reported more adverse events with a calculator (more hypoglycaemia with a device lacking IOB).

## Conclusions

Calculators improve dose accuracy and reduce the calculation burden; evidence for better HbA1c and satisfaction is growing but limited and depends on correct settings and active use ("A BC must be used actively to serve its purpose").

## Notes for Mellis

- **Relevance:** Background for the standard formula and for why IOB must be subtracted (the one study with more severe hypos used a calculator without IOB). It does not answer the reverse-correction question; for that see Buchanan 2021 (pump comparison) and the Trio/Loop/AAPS device files.
- **Evidence level:** narrative review of RCTs and observational studies (mostly adults, pre-AID era).
